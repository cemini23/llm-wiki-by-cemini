## Narrative

**A journalist's account of changing their own viewing habit**, which is a different kind of evidence than an accuracy study.

The author studied political communication in DC and intended a career in politics before moving into journalism. Their observation: on Tuesday night, mainstream coverage was "starting to head" toward the irrelevance that print newspapers already reached — and watching **how Kalshi markets priced the returns** was "far more interesting and useful than most of the coverage on TV and online."

They note they had included a tweet saying something similar in the prior newsletter and thought it **melodramatic at the time**, then changed their mind after a day.

### The specific comparison

> "Votes came in, and prediction markets told us probabilities in real time… it beats the NYT 'needle,' for my money."

That is the concrete claim: not that markets are more accurate, but that the **live probability readout is a better thing to watch** than the alternatives. It is a product-experience claim, and it is measurable in a way accuracy claims are not.

### The honest scale

The author is careful: markets "are far from the dominant form of digesting election news. But they clearly were a growing part of it this election cycle."

So this is an adoption observation, not a dominance claim.

### Why it pairs with the postmortem

The 2026-11-04 postmortem established that markets got direction right and magnitude wrong, because the "blue wave" contract was a 218/49 threshold. This piece explains why the wrong-on-magnitude product still won the night for viewers: **a threshold contract resolving live is exactly what a viewer wants to watch.** The limitation and the appeal are the same design property.
