## Narrative

### Midnite becomes the NFL's UK and Ireland betting partner

**Midnite** — a London-based challenger bookmaker — signed a **three-year** deal running to **2029-03-31** as the NFL's **official sports betting partner** in the UK and Ireland.

**What it covers:** exclusive access to league rights across **broadcast, content, free-to-play, live games, and fan engagement**. Named assets include the **NFL Shield**, the **Super Bowl logo**, the **NFL London Games**, the **Super Bowl** in the UK/Ireland, and rights to the **NFL Dublin Game** should it return. Midnite gets **9 p.m. and late-night sponsorship idents on Sky Sports** NFL coverage, including playoffs and the Super Bowl, plus branding and activation around the London and Dublin games.

**The commercial context:** the UK and Ireland have **more than 17 million NFL fans**, and **3 million+** have attended London regular-season games since 2007. NFL London 2026 opened at Tottenham Hotspur Stadium with **Indianapolis beating Washington 30-13**.

> "The NFL has built an incredible following across the UK and Ireland, and this partnership gives us the opportunity to bring Midnite and the NFL together across a huge range of touchpoints." — Jonathan Shaw, Midnite VP of Growth

**Midnite's position:** founded as a challenger, raised **$35M** in a Series C in January 2026 (led by Raine Partners IV; investors include Discerning Capital, Venrex, Makers Fund, Play Ventures), licensed by the **GB Gambling Commission** and the **Irish Revenue Commissioner**, and builds its sportsbook in-house. It has EFL Championship club deals and has hinted at Canada.

**Why it is recorded:** it is the **international** counterpart to the US NFL-versus-Kalshi fight in `@sources/rss-pm-legal-2026-10-07-09.md`. The NFL is suing to keep prediction markets out of US sports **while simultaneously licensing a UK bookmaker**. The deal also requires Midnite to follow **NFL game-integrity and responsible-gambling policies** — the same conditions the league says the CFTC is not enforcing domestically.

### Everton's Sherif fined £5,000

**Martin Sherif**, a 20-year-old Everton striker, was fined **£5,000** by the FA for breaching betting rules over **15 months**. He was charged in **August 2026**.

**The conduct:** **61 bets** on football matches between **2024-11-20** and **2026-02-12** — **42** in the 2024/25 Premier League season while at Everton, and **19** last season across loan spells at **Port Vale** and **Rotherham United**.

**Critically, Sherif did not place the bets himself.** He **facilitated third-party bets through his own name** — the FA charged him with "causing, permitting, or enabling" bets on accounts he had opened and funded. **Six** gambling accounts were in his name.

**The money:** total stake **£1,974.77**, returns **£1,539.02** — a **net loss of £435.75**.

**No sporting sanction.** The independent commission — chaired by **Ms Sally Clark** with **Mr Andrew Adie** and **Mr Tony Agana** — found the breaches "did not warrant a sporting sanction," citing his **early admission**, his **youth at the time**, and "genuine remorse." He had spoken to Everton academy players about the dangers of betting, and had closed all his accounts.

**Two details the commission did not accept at face value:** it found "his suggestion that he was not aware of the football bets **difficult to accept**," and noted he had **attended education sessions** on gambling and knew the rules.

**Why no ban, when Toney received eight months?** The article attributes it to the **nature** of the breaches — facilitation rather than personal wagering on his own matches, plus the small stake and net loss.
