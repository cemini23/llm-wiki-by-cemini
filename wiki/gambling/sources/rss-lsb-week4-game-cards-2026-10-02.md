## Narrative

### A — JAX @ CIN: the slate's highest total (51.5)

**Lean: Under 51.5.** Cincinnati −2.5; split is roughly **27 / 24.5**. No other Week 4 game is above 51.

The arithmetic: Cincinnati averages **26.7 scored / 21.0 allowed (47.7 combined)**; Jacksonville **27.3 / 12.0 (39.3 combined)**. The 51.5 total sits **3.8 and 12.2 points above those norms**. That is the Under case.

Against it: two of Cincinnati's three games cleared 51.5 (60 and 57 points); Burrow has added **+11.5 expected points** while the run game has cost −4.4; Cincinnati is 4th in explosive-play rate (15.6%) though 17th in success rate (46.0%).

Jacksonville's defense is the anchor: **3rd in EPA allowed per play (−0.10)** and holds opponents to **43.9% success** against a league norm near 47%. Its offense is 4th in EPA per play (0.13) and **2nd in success rate (54.6%)**.

Weather is neutral: mostly sunny, 74°F, **2 mph**, 1% precipitation — no reason to shade the total down. Injuries: DT B.J. Hill (Achilles) and S Kyle Dugger (quad) not participating.

**Mechanism worth keeping:** the two-team averages were 47.7 and 39.3 — both below the posted 51.5. Comparing the *scoring midpoint* to the posted total is the same check used on the college card in `@sources/rss-lsb-cfb-week6-cards-2026-10-01.md`.

### B — DET @ CAR (SNF): cornerback attrition

Detroit −3.5 (ML −190), total 51 (Lions ~27, Panthers ~24). **Carolina placed both starting cornerbacks on IR Wednesday** — Mike Jackson (groin surgery) and Jaycee Horn (torn quad) — with a ~8-week estimate. Replacements are rookie Will Lee III, Akayleb Evans, and Chau Smith-Wade.

| Prop | Price | Case |
|------|-------|------|
| **Amon-Ra St. Brown over 7.5 receptions** | **+115** | 35 targets (32% share), 23 catches, 228 yards; cleared 7.5 in 2 of 3. Breaks even at 46.5% |
| **Jahmyr Gibbs over 35.5 receiving yards** | **−110** | 18 catches on 21 targets, 156 yards, 2 TD; averaging **52.0** vs a 35.5 line |

**Caveats stated in the article:** the desk **passes** on St. Brown's anytime TD at −125, expecting regression from five TDs. It notes Gibbs' receptions over 4.5 (−130) "is the same bet at a different price" — the two-totals-one-player trap the wiki now records as a standing rule. Detroit's Williams holds 31% of air yards on a 16% target share but converts only 56% with no TD on 17 targets.

**Bryce Young:** the desk passes on his Under 249.5. He leads the NFL with **939 passing yards** and has cleared 249.5 in all three games (361, 287, 291). Detroit ranks **last** in passing yards allowed (299.3/game).

### C — SEA @ LAC: take the 7, not 7.5

Seattle −7 (ML −340), total 42.5. **Take Seattle at 7, not 7.5** — a seven-point win pushes at 7 and cashes for the Chargers at 7.5. Los Angeles is only a play at 7.5.

The argument: Seattle's loss to Washington was turnover-driven, not yardage-driven. Darnold threw for 379 and 4 scores, but all **three Seattle giveaways became Washington touchdowns (20 points)**; Seattle outgained Washington **437–258** with 28 first downs to 17. Los Angeles is **0-3** for the first time since 2017 and has lost six straight, scoring **17 or fewer** each time. It reached Buffalo's 30 six times but managed three field goals and one TD **despite five Buffalo turnovers**. Herbert: 59.1% completions, 627 yards, **4 INT vs 3 TD**; the offense averages **14.7 points (29th)**.

Under lean: Seattle's 25-point average plus the Chargers' 14.7 equals **39.7**, below the 42.5 total. A likelier Seattle cover path is ~24-14, which also lands the Under.

**Mechanism:** the number matters more than the side. A half-point on a 7 is the difference between a push and a loss.

### D — MIA @ MIN: the backdoor case at −10.5

Minnesota −10.5, ML −600, total 38.5. Minnesota is **3-0** but wins unconvincingly:

- Defense carries it — **2nd in EPA allowed per play (−0.16)**, **1st in opponent success rate (36.3%)**.
- Offense is poor — 21st in EPA per play, **last in early-down EPA**, 28th in explosive rate, **last in success rate (38.6%)**.
- Wins came by 17, 6, and 7 (**10.0 average**, *under* the spread), aided by a **+4 turnover margin** (regression candidate) and an **86-yard punt return TD**.
- **−600 implies ~86% win probability** — a high bar for an offense in the bottom quartile.

The implied split is ~24.5 / 14.0. Minnesota averages 23.7; Miami 12.0 and has not reached 14. A 24-14 result **falls a point short** for Minnesota backers. Miami lost all three games by 14+.

Miami's backdoor path: its defense is **last in EPA allowed per play (+0.14)** and last in pass defense, with **zero sacks** — but its explosive-play rate is 8.5% (30th), and its only TD pass was a **77-yarder** from Malik Willis to Ryan Miller that trimmed 35-6 to 35-13. Minnesota blitzes at **71%**, which exposes deep throws.

**Injuries:** Justin Jefferson out (ankle, injured on his 7th snap at Tampa Bay); Miami placed **De'Von Achane on IR** 2026-09-28 (torn ACL).
