## Narrative

Two actions landed the same day, pulling in opposite directions on the same question: **who governs sports event contracts.**

### 1. The CFTC sends two rules to the White House

The CFTC sent **two rules to the White House for review** that would define what prediction markets may legally offer nationwide. Both work by further defining **"swap."**

| RIN | Type | Effect |
|-----|------|--------|
| **3038-AF82** | Proposed rule | **Adds event contracts to the "swap" definition** — confirming prediction-market swaps fall under CFTC oversight. Described as "the lead proposal of the two" and the more relevant to Kalshi's sports contracts |
| **3038-AF81** | **Interim Final Rule** | Blocks prediction markets from offering **casino-style games** — slots, blackjack, craps |

**Timing:** neither has an established deadline. Because AF81 is an **Interim Final Rule**, it would **take effect immediately** on White House approval, without a notice-and-comment period.

**Why it matters:** AF82 is the CFTC asserting that event contracts *are* swaps. That is the federal-side answer to the state argument that they are gambling. If AF82 is **not** finalised soon, the article expects states to keep pushing enforcement.

The article quotes the CFTC glossary: "The statutory definition of 'swap' is detailed and comprehensive, though certain agreements, contracts, and transactions are excluded from the definition."

### 2. Ohio orders ten operators to stop

The **Ohio Casino Control Commission (OCCC)** sent cease-and-desist letters to **ten prediction-market operators**, requiring them to halt sports event contracts in the state by **2026-10-16**. Recipients: **Underdog, Gemini Titan, Coinbase, Prophet X, Novig, Robinhood, Polymarket, Plus500, Moomoo, and Webull.**

**Legal basis:** unlicensed sports betting and bookmaking in Ohio. The letters use a shared template demanding the operator "cease and desist from offering, participating in offering, or facilitating those who offer sports event contracts in Ohio," with **written notice of compliance within fourteen days**.

**Penalty exposure:** the OCCC says it may impose a civil penalty equal to the money or value of property unlawfully obtained, and the letters state that offering unlicensed sports betting is a **felony**, exposing operators to civil **and criminal** penalties.

**Kalshi is conspicuously absent.** It received an earlier letter in 2025 and is in **separate litigation**. The article ties the new batch to the **Sixth Circuit rejecting Kalshi's argument** that its contracts fall only under CFTC regulation.

**No operator has responded.**

### Reading the two together

The federal and state tracks are now running in parallel and neither has resolved:

- **Federal:** the CFTC is trying to cement event contracts as swaps (AF82) and to strip the casino-adjacent products (AF81).
- **State:** Ohio is enforcing its gambling statute against ten operators regardless, and its list excludes Kalshi only because Kalshi is already in a different case.

**The practical consequence if states prevail** is named in the article: prediction markets could face a **21+ minimum age** and **a ban on college player props** — the standard shape of a state sportsbook licence.

## Dead Ends

- **The CFTC rules are proposals, not law.** AF82 needs finalisation; AF81 needs White House approval. Neither is in force. Do not model either as an active constraint. [TENTATIVE]
- **Ohio's deadline is 2026-10-16** — the letters are orders, not findings. No penalty has been assessed.
- **No operator action, no pm scp.** This wiki tracks these as awareness. Nothing here changes a bankroll or a bot lane.
