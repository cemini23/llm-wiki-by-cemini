## Narrative

### 1 — The NFL picks a side, and 39 states join

**The NFL filed an amicus brief on 2026-10-08** backing New Jersey against Kalshi, arguing that **states are better positioned** to oversee sports-related contracts. Separately, **39 states plus Washington D.C.** — a bipartisan coalition led by Ohio AG Andy Wilson — urged SCOTUS to take the case.

The league's argument, in its own words:

> "Absent the clarity that only this Court can provide, prediction market operators will continue exploiting the gap between state and federal regulation, endangering consumers and the integrity of sports events across the country."

> "Any delay from the Court will result in increasing consumer harm and risk to game integrity."

**The volume figure that anchors it:** on the season's first Sunday, **$1.8B of $3.3B** in total prediction-market trading — more than half — was NFL-related.

**The league's three complaints against CFTC oversight:**

1. **Manipulable markets** — "a player can alter his performance, a coach can change his team's lineup or an official can make (or not make) certain calls." The NFL has asked the CFTC and the DCMs to adopt a list of prohibited wagers; both declined.
2. **Age** — Kalshi allows **18**; the NFL wants **21**.
3. **Staffing** — "State regulatory bodies have hundreds of employees… Nevada and Pennsylvania, for instance, have close to 400 employees each. The Commission, meanwhile, has only **543 employees** covering the entire country."

**The NFL is isolated among leagues.** Kalshi's spokeswoman pointed out that the company partners with the **NHL and MLB**, and that "all but the NFL have been willing to share data and collaborate to protect game integrity."

**Also filing for the states:** former CFTC chair **Gary Gensler** and former Senator **Chris Dodd**, the sponsor of the act the CFTC relies on. Dodd said his bill "did not set out to authorize nationwide sports betting through derivatives markets or displace decades of state and tribal primacy."

**The CFTC's counter:** spokesman Brooke Nethercott said the CFTC offered the NFL a **memorandum of understanding** and the league declined — "It's unfortunate the NFL declined to sign [an MOU]… which would've provided the league the ability to better discuss, cooperate and exchange information with us."

**Timeline:** Kalshi has until **2026-11-09** to respond. The Court is not expected to decide whether to take the case until **at least December**.

### The circuit split driving it

| Circuit | Ruling | Position |
|---------|--------|----------|
| **Third** (April) | For **Kalshi** | CFTC has exclusive jurisdiction; the contracts **are** swaps |
| **Sixth** (2026-09-29) | For **Ohio/Tennessee** | Contracts do **not** meet the swap definition; states may enforce |
| **Ninth** (August, 3-0) | For **Nevada** | Upheld dissolving the injunction |

Roughly **20 states** are in litigation. A split of this shape makes Supreme Court review likely.

### 2 — The three-state retreat

Prediction markets pulled back further while this was filed.

| State | Development |
|-------|-------------|
| **Michigan** | **Coinbase** agreed on 2026-10-07 to stop offering **new** sports event contracts by **2026-10-10** and close all remaining positions by the same date. Michigan has now halted sports event contracts from **Coinbase, Robinhood, and Kalshi** |
| **Connecticut** | Of the **nine** operators served cease-and-desist orders in September, **Webull, Gemini, and ProphetX** have exited. **Polymarket, Coinbase, Crypto.com, Robinhood, Novig, and Underdog** remain active. Connecticut also sued Kalshi |
| **Missouri** | **PrizePicks** suspended its sports prediction markets ("team predictions"). Existing entries stay active and can pay out; no new picks accepted |

> "Coinbase joining Robinhood and Kalshi in stepping back from these unlicensed sports contracts underscores Michigan's commitment to enforcing its gaming laws." — Henry Williams, MGCB Executive Director

> "Our actions sent a clear message. We will not tolerate illegal prediction market activity that preys upon the most vulnerable consumers." — Bryan Cafferelli, Connecticut DCP Commissioner

Connecticut also issued **nearly 30 subpoenas** to payment processors, app stores, and media.

**The national map (as of 2026-10-07):** courts have **blocked** prediction markets in **Nevada, Michigan, and Washington**. In **seven** more — Connecticut, Iowa, Maryland, New York, Ohio, Tennessee, Utah — a court has **cleared the state to enforce**. In **ten more**, a lawsuit, order, or enforcement threat is pending. **Forty-four state AGs** told the CFTC in July that it has no authority over sports prediction markets.

### 3 — The Seminole Tribe sues DraftKings

The **Seminole Tribe of Florida sued DraftKings** in **Broward County** on 2026-10-08 — a **72-page** complaint naming **DraftKings Inc., CEO Jason Robins, and GUS III LLC** (which operates DraftKings Predictions).

**The claim:** the Tribe holds **exclusive** online sports betting rights in Florida under the 2021 compact (for nearly 25 more years, in exchange for ~$1B/year to the state). DraftKings' **Pick6** and **Super App sportsbook** operate as sports betting, not prediction markets.

**The Tribe's evidence, and it is specific:**

- **"1-800-GAMBLER" appears on DraftKings' own advertisements.** "The warning is DraftKings' own admission, printed on its own advertisements, that the Super App sportsbook is a gambling product and not a financial market."
- **The Florida page looks the same** as a licensed state's page, other than the label "prediction betting."
- DraftKings **separates** its sports page from other predictions.
- The complaint also alleges DraftKings **"secretly funded"** lawsuits attacking the compact (2021 and 2024 pari-mutuel suits, plus a 2025 "Protect the Constitution" LLC suit).

**Relief sought:** an injunction, damages, legal costs, and all revenue earned in Florida — to be used for "consumer protection initiatives and problem-gambling recovery programs." The Tribe also alleges Robins violated Florida's **anti-racketeering act**.

**DraftKings' response:** prediction markets "operate in accordance with applicable law," and Pick6 "is a peer-to-peer fantasy sports variant centered on a simple 'more or less' mechanic tied to individual athlete performance metrics and is not sports betting."

**The legal question underneath it:** the Tribe argues DKeX's sports event contracts violate the **IGRA** as well as the compact — a different hook from the states' Commodity Exchange Act arguments.

**Why only DraftKings?** A quoted observer notes it may be "because then you're going after the instrumentality that has the full-throated support of the White House," rather than Kalshi, Polymarket, Underdog, PrizePicks, or Crypto.com.

## Dead Ends

- **Nothing is decided.** SCOTUS has not granted cert; Kalshi's response is due 2026-11-09 and a grant decision is not expected before December. The Seminole suit is a complaint, and the allegations are unproven.
- **The NFL's position is self-interested.** It earns from DraftKings, FanDuel, and Fanatics partnerships while opposing Kalshi. Read the brief as an industry position, not a neutral integrity finding.
- **"States can enforce" is not "prediction markets are illegal."** The seven enforcement-cleared states have lifted stays; they have not each entered final judgment.
- **No operator action, no pm scp.** Awareness only.
