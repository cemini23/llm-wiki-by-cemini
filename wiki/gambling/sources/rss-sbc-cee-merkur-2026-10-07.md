## Narrative

### CEE market entry — "another Brazil" is the fear

At SBC Summit, a panel of Central and Eastern European operators warned challengers off the region. Panellists: **Irakli Asanishvili** (CEO, Flutter CEE), **Bojan Scekic** (CEO, Balkan Bet), **Mikolaj Cymerman** (CCO, Entain CEE), **Ionut-Valeriu Andrei** (CEO, Loteria Românǎ).

> "There are exciting opportunities across the region, but I can't pinpoint specific markets. We also need to think about the future of the market from a regulatory standpoint — you've seen what's happened in Brazil." — Asanishvili

**The structural barriers named:**

- **Overregulation risk.** Slovakia runs a controversial **flat-rate levy per device** alongside a **27% GGR tax**, which has caused disputes and may deter entrants.
- **Low channelisation.** Heavy tax and restriction push players to unlicensed operators; regulated CEE markets leave channelisation "a lot to be desired."
- **Incumbency.** The landscape is dominated by local giants transitioning from long-successful **retail** models to online. "The barrier for entry for any newcomer is very hard." — Cymerman

**Where there is an opening:** Cymerman singled out **Poland** — a monopoly system for gambling but **private licences issued for betting** — as a market with potential for further opening if "political will" emerges, following the Netherlands, Sweden, and Finland.

Scekic on **Hungary**: "If you're a CEE operator, and your answer to being interested in Hungary is 'no', then you are straight up lying."

**The retail-vs-online split:** Andrei (Loteria Românǎ, Romania's state lottery monopoly) said **>50% of Romanian lottery tickets now sell online**, up from **15% three years ago** — while stressing retail still matters because many Romanians use cash. Asanishvili pushed back on "omnichannel is the future": "I fully believe that humans operate on convenience… If you can build brand, trust and have a variety of payment channels, I don't see why you can't be successful without a major retail presence."

**Why it is recorded here:** it is the clearest statement of the **Brazil contagion fear** — that a regulated market can be removed by decree — and it names the specific taxes and channelisation numbers behind CEE entry economics.

### Merkur Bets goes live on EveryMatrix in Denmark

**Merkur Bets** (formerly **Cashpoint**) went live on **EveryMatrix's** omnichannel platform in Denmark on **2026-10-07** — the first of a **multi-territory migration** that will bring Merkur brands in **Austria, Germany, and Belgium** onto one stack.

Scope: **1,000+ sports betting terminals** across **230+ shops**, plus the online sportsbook and casino simultaneously. It replaces a legacy in-house platform after a migration the companies call complex.

> "Bringing its retail estate, online sportsbook and casino together on our technology in Denmark is a complex omnichannel deployment." — Jonas Groes, co-CEO, EveryMatrix

Belgium was already signalled: **Betcenter** (Merkur Group) announced an EveryMatrix omnichannel sportsbook partnership in July 2026 covering **50+ Belgian shops** and **600+ self-service terminals**. The deal was signed earlier in 2026 covering two brands.

EveryMatrix also powers **Danske Spil's** casino in Denmark (launched September 2026), making it the **dominant technology provider by brands** in the regulated Danish market.

**Why it is recorded here:** vendor consolidation onto **turnkey platforms** is the structural trend behind the operator's own stack — one platform across markets, retail and online. It is industry context for how books are built, not a product the wiki would use.
