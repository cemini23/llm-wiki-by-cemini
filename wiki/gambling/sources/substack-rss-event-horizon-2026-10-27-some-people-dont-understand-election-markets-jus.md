## Narrative

The article uses the Brazilian election as its case. **Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva defeated Flavio Bolsonaro** on the Sunday. Ahead of election day, prediction markets gave Lula **about a 20% chance of winning**.

The reaction is the subject of the piece. Commentators treated the outcome as a market failure. The article quotes Futurism's headline, "'Prediction' Markets Were Comically Wrong About the Brazilian Election", which argued the platforms were "horrendously off the mark".

The author's counter-argument is a base-rate point. A market that prices an event at 20% is wrong when the event does not happen. It is not wrong when the event does happen. An 80% favourite losing is a normal draw from an 80% distribution. Treating a single realised outcome as a verdict on the price confuses calibration with accuracy on one sample.

The second half of the argument is about responsibility. The author holds **Kalshi and Polymarket partly to blame**. Election markets are marketed and framed in a way that invites users to read a probability as a forecast of the result. The platforms therefore share some of the cost when a 20% outcome lands and users conclude the market failed.

This matters for anyone using these markets as signals. Calibration is measured across many events, not one. A single surprising result is evidence about the user's intuition, not about the market's price quality.
