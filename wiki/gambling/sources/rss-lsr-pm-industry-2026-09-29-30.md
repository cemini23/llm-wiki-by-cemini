## Narrative

### A — Kalshi ends its Volume Incentive Program early

Kalshi filed a **2026-09-28 self-certification with the CFTC** to terminate its **Volume Incentive Program**, effective **no earlier than 2026-10-13**. The program had been scheduled to run to **2026-10-01-2027**. Kalshi gave no reason, citing its rule that it may "modify or terminate such programs as the Exchange determines in its sole discretion."

How it worked, per the filing: Kalshi set a fixed reward per eligible market over a term of up to 31 days; traders at term end received a share proportional to their volume. Only trades on the central limit order book priced **$0.03–$0.97** counted (perpetual futures exempt). Event-contract rewards were capped at **$0.005 per contract**.

**Context the filing does not draw:** the CFTC's **2026-08-12 DMO advisory** warned that volume-threshold incentives can encourage trading for rewards, raising wash-trading and prearranged-transaction risk. The *Wall Street Journal* reported that **nearly 1 million** near-identical trades worth **more than $5B** had flowed through Kalshi's **ether perpetual futures** market since August. Kalshi has faced wash-trading questions since.

**What replaces it:** Kalshi submitted a **Deposit and Trading Reward Incentive Program** (CFTC docket, modified version received 2026-09-25, earliest effective 2026-09-28). Instead of a pool split by volume share, it allows time-limited promotions tied to deposits, trading, or both — **3 to 90 days**, targeted by account age, funding status, prior activity, inactivity, geography, and contract category. Individual promotions pay up to **$2,500 per participant**; total rewards cap at **$5,000 per person** over a planned two-year life. Transactions under inquiry for self-matching, wash trading, or prearranged trading are **excluded** from promotional rewards.

The separate **Liquidity Incentive Program** (paying for qualifying resting orders) is unaffected. Per Front Office Sports, the CFTC is reviewing incentive programs **across** prediction-market platforms; Chairman **Michael Selig** could act as soon as this week. The agency has announced no decision or target, and has not said it directed the termination.

### B — Flutter hits a record low

**Flutter Entertainment** (FanDuel's parent) hit an **all-time low** on 2026-09-29 after **Brazil banned online betting** — days before CEO **Peter Jackson** departed. Shares fell **more than 7%** between Friday's close and Monday's open. Flutter has lost roughly **three-quarters of its value** since peaking above **$313 in August 2025**; Investing.com recorded a 52-week low of **$78.78** and a market cap of $14.44B [TENTATIVE — price prints conflict across outlets].

Brazil: Flutter suspended betting and online casino ahead of an **Oct. 6** deadline, expecting **~$70M revenue** and **~$20M adjusted EBITDA** lost if the shutdown runs through 2026. It paid **~$350M** for a **56%** stake in NSX Group in 2025. India's ban on real-money online games costs **$250M in 2026** and **$310M in 2027**; Junglee paid operations shut in August after a **$237M** investment.

FanDuel operations: it retained a leading **39%** share of U.S. sportsbook GGR in Q2, but sportsbook revenue fell **15% YoY** and U.S. adjusted EBITDA dropped **70%** to **$119M**. Flutter acknowledged it under-reinvested 2025 NFL winnings into promotions, causing churn. It cut its 2026 U.S. EBITDA outlook by **>20%** in August, committing ~**$270M** to rebuilding momentum, with higher state taxes adding ~**$40M**. Group outlook was cut **$210M** to **$2.655B**. Analyst targets after the August reset: Wells Fargo $112, Wedbush $126, BTIG $113, Rothschild Redburn $119 (downgraded to Neutral).

### C — Michigan and Massachusetts review DraftKings AI/VIP reporting

The **Michigan Gaming Control Board** told LSR it is **reviewing** issues raised in **New York Times** and **ProPublica** reports on DraftKings' use of AI/ML in promotions and its VIP program.

> "We take concerns about player protection seriously. Michigan's licensed operators are required to meet the responsible gaming obligations in state law and MGCB rules, and we are reviewing the issues raised in these reports." — Lisa Keith, MGCB public information officer

**Massachusetts** regulators opened a parallel review on 2026-09-24. The underlying allegation: DraftKings built a **machine-learning model** on customer betting records to predict how promotions would affect individual users' eventual wins and losses, then targeted customers most likely to lose money or leave. A proposed **class action** (Vest) followed, arguing DraftKings breached its own privacy-notice commitment to use customer data for responsible-play assessment. DraftKings CEO **Jason Robins** defended the company's responsible-gaming approach at SBC Summit.

**Michigan rules context:** the state requires a displayed responsible-gaming logo linking to a dedicated page with a toll-free helpline, resources, and the operator's policy. It maintains a **disassociated persons list** and an internet-gaming responsible-gaming database. Michigan consumer rules **do not address AI directly**.

### D — Fluharty: "Basically moonshine"

At **G2E**, West Virginia Delegate and Play'n Go government-affairs head **Shawn Fluharty** contrasted legal sports betting (a "fine bottle of wine") with prediction markets:

> "It's basically moonshine. It's not licensed. It's not regulated. It's great for tailgates, maybe, but you don't know what the hell you're getting."

> "The states are the adults in the room and they should be responsible for implementing gaming and policing it and putting the guardrails in place."

**Joe Casole** (VP Legal & Regulatory Affairs, IC360) noted that consumers see the same thing in a sportsbook app and a CFTC-regulated prediction-market app — the ability to wager on sporting events or player outcomes — and flagged adjacent products: player trades, college-transfer portals, coach hirings/firings, "asymmetric peripheral scores." The G2E session was "Prediction Markets, Regulatory Oversight and Integrity," moderated by Rebecca Darin Goldberg.

### E — MGM and Caesars stay out

**MGM Resorts** and **Caesars Entertainment** will not follow DraftKings or FanDuel into prediction markets. CEOs **Bill Hornbuckle** (MGM) and **Tom Reeg** (Caesars) spoke on a G2E panel with **Wynn** CEO Craig Billings.

> "Nevada regulators have absolutely said to us, 'If you stretch out and do this, it's going to impact your licensing,' and we decided pretty quickly after that." — Hornbuckle

MGM considered entering after the sector expanded into sports in early 2025, then backed away. The **Nevada Gaming Control Board** warned licensees that offering sports event contracts **in other states** could affect suitability in Nevada. **Kalshi** was forced out in August after the **Ninth Circuit** held federal commodities law does not prevent states from enforcing gambling laws against prediction markets.

Caesars is prepared to enter **only** if it can do so without risking licenses — it operates eight Strip properties. Reeg conceded PM operators could gain a first-mover advantage if event contracts eventually move into regulated gambling, and raised an oversight flag:

> "But there was nowhere in their rules that said I couldn't place a bet." — Reeg, on a Kalshi contract covering whether Caesars would be sold in 2026, a market where he held inside knowledge

He also stated: "These are not swaps. They're not miraculously finding the other side of a 5-team parlay at the same time one side comes in."
