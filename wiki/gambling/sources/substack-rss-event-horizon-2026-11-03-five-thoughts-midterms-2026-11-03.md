## Narrative

**The lead argument is a trap the author builds on purpose.**

### Thought 1: prediction markets better hope Democrats win

If Democrats do not have a big night, "a lot of people are going to be clowning prediction markets." That may be unfair — but as the author has argued repeatedly, **Kalshi and Polymarket made this bed in 2024** by claiming to be better than polls and more accurate than the alternative.

This cycle markets and polls broadly agree on a blue wave, and markets have moved toward Democrats recently. But that is a probability, not a guarantee — the author puts "no blue wave" at about **13%**.

### Thought 2: prediction markets better hope Democrats *don't* win

The author immediately reverses himself, and the reversal is the point.

If Democrats take the House or Senate, **Congressional oversight gets much worse**. Past CFTC hearings on prediction markets "are going to look like ice cream socials" compared with what control changes bring. Several committees become a problem for both the operators and their regulator.

There is already an appetite: some Republicans have been willing to call Donald Trump Jr. before committees on prediction markets.

So the author's conclusion is that **neither outcome is good**, and an operator's real task is deciding which is the lesser evil.

### Thought 3 onward

The third thought begins **"Election markets are almost a rounding error"** — which reframes the entire preceding anxiety. The volume that makes election markets newsworthy is small relative to the venues' total business.

That is consistent with the volume data in the 2026-11-02 issue: parlays were **73.9%** of Kalshi's Saturday volume, sports 17.7%, crypto 7.6%. Election markets do not appear in that split at all.

### The structural observation

The author identifies a bind the venues created. Having marketed accuracy, a bad call damages them more than it damages a pollster, because they made a stronger claim. Having grown through sports and parlays, their regulatory exposure is concentrated in exactly the product that attracts political attention.

**Caveat.** Only the opening sections were read; thoughts three through five are summarised from their lead sentence only.
