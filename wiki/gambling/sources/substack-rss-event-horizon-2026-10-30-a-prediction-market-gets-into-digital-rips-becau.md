## Narrative

The file dates this Event Horizon post to 2026-10-30. It labels the text an RSS teaser and says the full post may be paywalled. The argument is that OG.com, a prediction-market operator that "has not really broken through," launched digital card rips on Thursday. The author, Dustin Gouker, treats the launch as a step toward a "super app" where a user can bet, trade, and speculate in one place.

OG.com Rips sells an unknown physical trading card, shows a digital reveal, and then offers an immediate cash buyback or tracked delivery. The post says the first five rips are free. Categories are NFL, NBA, MLB, and Pokémon. Tiers are Rookie, Emerald, Ruby, Titan, Legend, and OG Eternal. Each tier shows the chance of a card and the top cards in the pool. Gouker says Underdog and PrizePicks already sell rips, Fanatics sells "Instant Rips," and Coinbase has teased a rips product. He says OG.com is the first prediction-market-first app to add rips. He writes that a rip looks and feels like gambling, and that the buyer also owns a card that can ship. He quotes the OG.com site: "OG.com Rips is not regulated by the CFTC and does not offer CFTC-regulated products." He says the launch does not help prediction markets answer claims that the apps are gambling and speculation.

The briefing's "Big Story" reprints a Kalshi blog post. Kalshi announces a $1 billion Series G at a $40 billion post-money valuation, co-led by Wellington and Sequoia. In that reprint, Kalshi states more than $1 billion in non-sports annualized revenue, annualized trading volume above $1 trillion, more than 30 million accounts, and more than 75% of prediction-market trading volume. Kalshi also states commodities fees above $100 million annualized in three months, crypto volume of $140 billion annualized, politics volume above $8 billion annualized, international retail fees approaching $100 million annualized after one year in more than 140 countries, and perpetual futures at $83 billion of volume with more than 500,000 traders. These figures appear only as this newsletter's reprint of the Kalshi blog. This page does not check them.

For a prediction-market and sports-betting reader, the same post tracks state limits and the mix of volume. It says Utah brought criminal charges against Kalshi's co-founders. It says DraftKings Predictions then removed sports, novelty, political, and certain other event contracts for customers in Utah. It says FanDuel Predicts, from 10 a.m. ET Friday, removed sports, politics, and novelty contracts in Utah, left economic and financial contracts up, and kept open positions through settlement. TickerTracker figures in the post, for Wednesday, Oct. 28, put Kalshi volume at $3.6 billion, with parlays at 69.7% of volume, sports at 15.6%, and crypto at 11.3%. Trailing seven-day volume for the week ending Oct. 28 is Kalshi $25.8 billion, Polymarket US $4.04 billion, DraftKings DKeX $1.36 billion, Novig $845.5 million, Underdog Exchange $556.3 million, CDNA $528.5 million, Rothera $336.8 million, and ProphetX $70.5 million. The newsletter is the only source here for those TickerTracker numbers.

The roundup also passes on other single-source claims. The Wall Street Journal, as cited, says at least $2.6 million left Polymarket user accounts on Thursday after a website flaw. Polymarket paused the international exchange and said affected traders would be made whole. A Coinbase release, as cited, says prediction markets passed $200 million in annualized revenue, with 82% quarterly compound growth since launch and daily traders up more than 60% quarter over quarter. Robinhood, as cited, agreed to stop new sports event contracts in Ohio by Nov. 2. Black Basin Outdoors, as cited, will give store credit equal to the merchandise price if Kalshi's "Midterms: Congress Balance of Power?" market resolves to Republican control of both chambers.
