## Narrative

The author is explicit about his standing: **"I am not a lawyer. I look at the litigation around prediction markets and I see valid points on both sides."** That framing makes the piece useful — it is a practical objection from outside the legal argument, not a claim about the law.

### The objection

> **"It's hard to believe that Congress intended to allow sports betting in 50 states."**

He concedes the counter-argument before making his own: **"there is a material difference in how prediction markets work vis-à-vis sportsbooks."** That is the industry's position, and he accepts it as true.

Then the turn: **"the activity that the vast majority of users are engaging in is pretty clearly identifiable as sports betting."**

That is the argument in one line, and it is an argument about **substance over form**. A contract can be legally a commodity derivative and behaviourally a sports bet. The litigation is about which description governs.

He cites attorney Daniel Wallach and declines to rule on the legal merits, which is honest — the practical point stands separately from whether it wins in court.

### The "blindsided" claim

The second thread: **a lot of gambling regulators and operators were blindsided** by the rise of sports event contracts.

That is supported by the record in this wiki. The 2026-11-02 batch recorded Fanatics shutting its Utah product, DraftKings and FanDuel ending Utah contracts, and **37 state attorneys general** filing an amicus supporting Utah. Operators did not see this coming and are now reacting state by state.

### Why it connects to the volume data

The same wiki has the number that makes the objection concrete: **73.9% of Kalshi's Saturday volume was parlays.** If three quarters of the business is parlay-shaped, the "material difference from sportsbooks" argument is being made about a product mix where the difference is smallest.

**Caveat.** Opening sections only, single source, author is not a lawyer and says so.
