## Narrative

Event Horizon says prediction markets were a large part of Robinhood's Q3 earnings. The post says Robinhood plans to move the vast majority of its prediction-market trading to exchanges in which it has an ownership interest. CFO Shiv Verma said Rothera will be the primary exchange. He said Crypto.com and OG.com will be the main second exchange. He said Robinhood will get off most competitors by the end of the year into early next year. CEO Vlad Tenev said the firm cannot send all of its flow to one venue.

The post states these volume and revenue figures. This page has no second source for them. Robinhood saw $16.4 billion in trading volume in Q3. The post compares that with approximately $142 billion at Kalshi. About $68 billion of that Kalshi volume involved parlays. The post says Kalshi used to be very dependent on Robinhood retail flow and that this is less the case today. Event contracts generated revenue of $173 million, up over 6x YoY. Verma said non-sports contracts accounted for approximately 30% of trading in Q3, up from 15% in Q2. The post says June, during the World Cup, remains Robinhood's highest-volume month. The earnings release, as quoted, says Rothera reached over $100 million in annualized revenues in its first full quarter of operation, with over 6.4 billion contracts traded since launch. Verma said Robinhood received an initial 1% equity ownership stake in Crypto.com and OG.com with no cash outlay, and can earn up to 10% ownership.

These claims matter to a prediction-market and sports-betting reader because they describe where sports and non-sports event-contract flow may go. Tenev said Robinhood and SIG are significant equity holders in Rothera and that Robinhood does not operate Rothera. He said Rothera works with futures commission merchants and does not run a first-party customer business. He said an all-sports book will get commoditized. He pointed to growth in crypto contracts and commodities, and to work on earnings contracts for EPS, revenue, and company KPIs. Verma said Robinhood lowered customer pricing with a dynamic model when it bought Rothera. He said Gold members get better pricing. He said the firm did not impact its take rate.

The same post prints a volume table and attributes the data to TickerTracker. For Monday, Oct. 26, it lists Kalshi volume of $2.69 billion, parlays at 63.6% of volume, sports at 18.1%, and crypto at 14.7%. For the trailing seven days ending Oct. 26, it lists Kalshi at $25.5 billion, Polymarket US at $3.81 billion, DraftKings' DKeX at $1.38 billion, Novig at $858.1 million, CDNA at $571.3 million, Underdog Exchange at $569.8 million, Rothera at $316.3 million, and ProphetX at $67.9 million. These figures appear only in this newsletter here.

The briefing also collects items on the sports-betting line. It reports a Kalshi $2,500 monthly deposit limit on every account for 18- to 20-year-old traders. It reports a CFTC lawsuit against Utah, a New York State Gaming Commission petition to enforce subpoenas about Kalshi sports prediction markets, and an NYU Tax Law Center view that sports event contracts should face the same federal tax rules as sports gambling. It quotes Samuel Henry Lazarus at the Council on Foreign Relations: Americans have already staked more than $1.4 billion on prediction markets tied to the 2026 U.S. midterm elections, and that figure is expected to exceed $2 billion by election day. The author writes that contract structure can set prediction markets apart from a sportsbook, and that most people who use sports event contracts are still betting on sports. The author also writes that Kalshi has called its product sports betting on numerous occasions.
