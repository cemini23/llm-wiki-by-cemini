## Narrative

This Event Horizon briefing is dated 2026-10-29 in the inbox file. The lead item is a Kalshi email about hedging a late ski-resort opening. The email names Adam Farr of Ascend Properties in Big Sky. Farr bought "No" on Big Sky opening before November 22 in Kalshi "Mountain Opening" markets. The same market is live for Mt. Bachelor. The author, writing about Bend, Oregon, tried a trade that Mt. Bachelor would not open before Dec. 6. The author says Bachelor usually aims to open by Thanksgiving and lists a scheduled opening date of Dec. 4. The lack of liquidity and the slippage made that trade less appealing relative to the implied probability. The author says dates further out also lack enough liquidity at good prices. The author says few small businesses around Bend will find these trades useful in the current market. The email gives no instructions for resting an order. The author presumes Kalshi sent the note to addresses near a ski resort. The author calls that smart marketing and says Kalshi wants to be seen as a place to hedge real-world risk. The author also names a chicken-and-egg problem: traders need discovery and liquidity, and the market has to start somewhere.

The sports items matter to a prediction-market and sports-betting reader because they tie event contracts to league rules and state gaming enforcement. Front Office Sports reports that the NFL told Saquon Barkley to divest his Polymarket equity stake. Barkley said NFL players may not invest in or endorse gambling or prediction-market companies. The Michigan Gaming Control Board reports an agreement with QCX LLC, which does business as Polymarket US. Polymarket US will stop new sports-related event contracts for Michigan customers. It will close remaining open positions by the end of the day Eastern time on November 25, 2026. The board says similar agreements cover Coinbase Financial Markets, Inc. and Robinhood Derivatives, LLC. A state court issued a preliminary injunction against KalshiEX LLC. InGame reports that Robinhood CEO Vlad Tenev is less excited about sports event contracts than other players. CFO Shiv Verma said Robinhood is working on leaving Kalshi by late 2026 or early 2027 to focus on Rothera and Crypto.com.

The volume block is a single-source table in this newsletter. The post attributes the figures to TickerTracker for Tuesday, Oct. 27.

- Kalshi volume: $2.76 billion
- Parlays: 64% of volume
- Sports: 17.2% of volume
- Crypto: 14.8% of volume
- Trailing seven-day volume, week ending Oct. 27: Kalshi $25.2 billion; Polymarket US $3.88 billion; DraftKings' DKeX $1.38 billion; Novig $858.1 million; CDNA $571.3 million; Underdog Exchange $569.8 million; Rothera $322.7 million; ProphetX $67.9 million

Other numeric claims in the same post are also single-source here. InGame, citing a PitchBook report, says Kalshi may already be on course for adjusted earnings of more than $1 billion this year, which could put it ahead of DraftKings on that measure. A Wall Street Journal item compares a $1,000 stock buy with even-money event contracts at 50 cents each. That stake buys 2,000 contracts, before fees. The piece says Robinhood generated roughly 1.05 cents in revenue per contract in the quarter, so the take on that trade would be about $21, which it calls 175 times the stock buy. A Rutgers press release in the roundup says about 40% of those who bet on prediction markets, and 41% of social casino players, were classified as high-risk problem gamblers, a rate nearly four times as high as gambling on other activities. The Financial Times item says an analysis of data since April 2026 covered 170mn bets by 1.2mn retail accounts and names sports gambler "Swiss Tony" as Polymarket's top trader. The Atlantic item says Polymarket traders gave Francesca Hong about a 95 percent chance of winning her August Wisconsin primary until she lost.

The legal roundup calls the Utah case the most serious legal challenge Kalshi and the prediction-market industry have faced to date. It says Arizona brought criminal charges and a federal court stopped that state case. A quoted Matt Levine note says Utah sued Kalshi for illegal gambling and that Utah does not allow sportsbooks. Other items say Kalshi hired Obama adviser Eric Schultz, defended its Platinum program to Democratic lawmakers as different from sportsbook loyalty programs, and sued the Missouri Gaming Commission on the claim that only the federal government can regulate prediction markets. A CFTC press release sets a Dec. 15 roundtable on 24/7 trading. Rothera says the CFTC approved its first KPI swap, tied to Tesla (TSLA). The quote of the day, from InGame, is former congressman George Santos, whom the post says Kalshi banned: "Kalshi is gambling, and it's [unregulated] gambling, and it needs to be banned." New CFTC filings in the post include Kalshi election markets, Underdog NBA markets, Novig NBA awards, DraftKings DKeX college football awards, and a ProphetX withdrawal of Scorigami contracts.
