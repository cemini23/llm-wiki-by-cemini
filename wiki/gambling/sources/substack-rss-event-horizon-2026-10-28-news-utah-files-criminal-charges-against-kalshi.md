## Narrative

The Event Horizon says the fight over prediction-market legality escalated in Utah on Wednesday. It says Utah Attorney General Derek Brown brought felony and misdemeanor charges against Kalshi and its two co-founders. It attributes that report to multiple outlets. It says Salt Lake City media reported the news first, and it names ABC 4 and Fox 13. It says the Fox 13 article includes the charging documents.

The newsletter quotes Fox 13 on the charge list. Fox 13 says KalshiEx LLC and co-founders Tarek Mansour and Luana Lopes Lara face a second-degree felony for a pattern of unlawful activity, 12 counts of online gambling promotion (a third-degree felony), and one count of general gambling promotion (a class A misdemeanor). The figure of 12 counts appears only in that Fox 13 quotation in this file. The newsletter says the charges are allegations. It says the defendants have not been convicted of any wrongdoing.

The newsletter quotes Attorney General Derek Brown. He says gambling is illegal in Utah whether a person places the bet, runs the game, or promotes it. He says the label "prediction market" does not change the activity. He says federal registration does not place a company above the law. He says a federal court already ruled that Utah's gambling laws apply to Kalshi, and that Kalshi kept taking bets from Utahns.

The newsletter says the criminal charges follow a federal-court result in August. It says Utah prevailed against Kalshi. It says a judge ruled that federal commodities law does not preempt Utah's enforcement of its anti-gambling laws against Kalshi. It says Kalshi appealed that ruling to the Tenth Circuit. It says Utah sued Kalshi in state court last week. It says the Commodity Futures Trading Commission announced a lawsuit against Utah on Tuesday. That suit seeks to block the state's enforcement against federally regulated exchanges.

The newsletter quotes a Kalshi spokesperson. The spokesperson calls the case a political overreach and says the company will seek to stop it in court, as it did in Arizona. The newsletter says Arizona filed misdemeanor criminal charges against Kalshi in March, and a federal court injunction later blocked that prosecution. The file ends under a "Some context" heading. No further context follows that heading.

For a prediction-market and sports-betting reader, the source frames a state criminal case that treats Kalshi activity as gambling. It also reports a federal regulator suing the state, an appeal to the Tenth Circuit, and an earlier Arizona prosecution that a federal injunction stopped. Those claims all come from this one newsletter.
