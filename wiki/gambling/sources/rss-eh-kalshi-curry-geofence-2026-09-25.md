## Narrative

### The self-certification

Kalshi certified **new NBA player-movement markets**, including a **Stephen Curry "next team"** market, **despite past league objections**. Leagues have said markets about player movement and team transactions should not exist. Hundreds of millions of dollars traded on **LeBron James' next team** over the summer — a market the NBA said should not exist.

Curry market pricing at the time [Source: SI, 2026-07-30; Kalshi news, 2026-07-31]:

| Outcome | Price |
|---------|-------|
| Golden State Warriors or retires | 86% (later quoted 93%) |
| San Antonio Spurs | 8% |
| Miami Heat | 4% (later 3%) |
| Charlotte Hornets | 3% |
| Boston Celtics | 2% |

Settlement was set on whichever team Curry is with by **2026-10-21**.

Why it matters: the **CFTC advisory of 2026-09-22** (Letter 26-27) presumes the hardest cases — contracts settling on a named person's discrete conduct — to be readily manipulable. Player-movement markets sit in the same family. Kalshi is expanding the category while the regulator tightens around it and a possible **NBA–prediction-market deal** is reported to be near.

### New York sues Polymarket

The **New York Attorney General sued Polymarket** on Thursday 2026-09-25, alleging illegal gambling and seeking to shut down its New York operations, recover profits, and impose penalties.

Polymarket launched its **US platform in December 2025** through a CFTC-regulated exchange, and launched a **federal countersuit**. The litigation now spans **at least 20 states**. **New Jersey has asked the U.S. Supreme Court** to resolve conflicting appellate decisions on state authority over sports prediction markets.

The New York AG had already sued **Coinbase** and **Gemini** over prediction-market offerings in April, and secured an **$8M** settlement from a sweepstakes casino operator in September.

### Kalshi geofences California tribal lands

Kalshi executed its **first voluntary geofence**, blocking access on **California tribal lands**. This follows the **Ninth Circuit's approval on 2026-09-16** of a preliminary injunction sought by the tribes. The geofence resolves the next steps in the case for now.

### Trademark exposure

**FMF** sued Kalshi for **trademark infringement** — the first known case testing a prediction-market operator's right to use sports-league wordmarks and imagery. Kalshi is expected to argue **fair use**, the same defence DraftKings uses against an NCAA lawsuit over the association's trademarks.

### The bonus program

Kalshi's **"Trade $25, Get Up to $500"** promotional bonus drew regulatory comment: pre-defined performance metrics that give unequal, non-objective benefits to participants in the same category may run afoul of **Core Principle 2**. Reported as **"random bonuses appear to be back."**

### Public launches prediction-market AI agents

**Public.com** launched **AI Agents for Prediction Markets** on 2026-09-24 — announced with Kalshi. Per Max Crowley, Kalshi VP of Business Development: Public members can "use the probabilities our markets produce as a signal alongside stocks, bonds, crypto and other assets."

### The framing dispute

- **Yahoo/Benzinga:** former CFPB adviser **Lipton** said Kalshi's case is strongest when prediction markets look like financial products used by firms and sophisticated investors. Sports undermines that: a product built around "some guy on his couch placing a bet" "looks like gambling" and "really undermines the argument."
- **FAIR Canada (Jean-Paul Bureaud):** "An event contract may be a derivative in legal form, but in substance it can still be a wager."
- **Robin Hanson (George Mason):** defends prediction markets as information-gathering institutions using a "wolves and sheep" model — uninformed traders attract informed participants, and manipulation attempts create profit for corrective traders.
