## Narrative

**21 RSS items. Two carry a durable finding: the sweepstakes-ban push, and the exchange consolidation that prediction-market growth is funding.**

### 1. Sweepstakes bans are advancing in two states at once

| State | Instrument | Reach |
|---|---|---|
| **Wyoming** | Draft `27LSO-0098` | Online casino and sweepstakes become **professional gambling** — a felony, up to 3 years |
| **Pennsylvania** | **HB 2801** | Fines up to **$500,000 per violation**; session ends 30 Nov |

**The Wyoming draft is the one to read.** It reaches **service providers**, not just players: geolocation services, payment processing, server hosting, platform services, gaming content, data services, promotion, and advertising are all named. That is an infrastructure-level prohibition, and it is the pattern a prediction-market operator has to assume will spread.

**Asymmetry worth noting:** Wyoming has **no legal online casino industry to protect** — the ban is not operator-driven there. Pennsylvania does, and its bill is attached to a larger gaming package.

### 2. Prediction-market growth is now funding exchange consolidation

**Matchbook acquired Betdaq**, taking the UK's major betting exchanges from five to **four**. Matchbook's stated rationale is that **prediction markets have driven renewed demand for peer-to-peer pricing**, and it has been scaling both prediction-market and B2B activity since launching the **first UK prediction markets platform in December 2025**.

This is the clearest instance so far of prediction-market success reshaping the underlying exchange market rather than the reverse.

### 3. Michigan treats prediction markets as unlicensed gambling

The MGCB's 33 cease-and-desist letters target sweepstakes and offshore casino — but the report notes Michigan has **also targeted unlicensed operators beyond offshore sites, including prediction markets.** Cited scale: **$673.6bn/year** US wagering legal and illegal, illegal up **22% since 2022**, and **70%** of online bettors at illegal sites unaware.

The regulator's framing is **consumer protection** — specifically self-exclusion — not tax revenue.

### 4. The Seminole compact is being tested against Pick6

The Seminole Tribe's 72-page suit against **DraftKings, CEO Jason Robins, and GUS III LLC** tests whether a prediction-market product (**Pick6**) breaches **2021 compact exclusivity**. It includes a **RICO count against the CEO personally**, and seeks injunction, restitution, and disgorgement with no figure stated. DraftKings' defence is federal **CEA** compliance and a "peer-to-peer fantasy sports variant" characterisation.

**This is the same question as every other prediction-market action: is it a swap, or is it gambling?** The wiki's earlier CFTC material covers the federal side; this is the tribal-compact side.
