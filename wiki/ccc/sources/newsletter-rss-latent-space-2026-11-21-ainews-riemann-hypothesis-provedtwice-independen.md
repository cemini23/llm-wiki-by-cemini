## Narrative

Two mathematicians claimed independent proofs of the Riemann Hypothesis within hours of each other.

- **Levent Alpöge** (Anthropic) released a Claude-driven proof as a note with Lean code, about 1.3 GB and roughly 250,000 pages equivalent, not a full paper.
- **Alessandro Masullo** released a 940-page, fully human, work-in-progress proof he had worked on for 12 years.

The verification status is the part that matters. Elliot Glazer reportedly confirms the formalised statement is GRH and puts the chance of a Lean bug well under 1%. So if it compiles with no extra axioms, it is probably correct, and no human will ever read all of it.

The newsletter also gives the cautionary precedent: OpenAI's September Navier-Stokes claim was criticised because the Lean proof did not match the natural-language result. Both AI-claimed Millennium Prize results will take years to verify.

For CCC this is the strongest recent illustration of the faithfulness gap. A machine-checked proof is only as good as the statement it checks. Snippet-level trust in a passing kernel check is misplaced. Treat all three claims as [NEEDS VERIFICATION 2026-11-22]; no independent peer review exists yet.
