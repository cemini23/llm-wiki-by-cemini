## Narrative

Mercor raised $750M Series D at **$20B**, having crossed **$1B in February, $2B in June, and now $3B annualized**, while "surviving hacks". The company reports **5 million experts**, and frames its market as **800+ distinct US occupations** whose workflows models have yet to be evaluated against.

### The number worth carrying

Not the valuation. This, quoted from a company re: its own economics:

> **"We already spend 3x as much on inference as on employee salaries, and it pays for itself by making our team better at their jobs. If that ratio holds as AI spreads through the rest of the economy, it points to something closer to double-digit GDP growth over the next five years, nearly 3x the rate we're used to."**

That is the first hard ratio this workspace has seen on the inference-versus-labor substitution, and it is a **3:1 spend in favour of inference** — with the claim that it pays back through productivity rather than headcount reduction.

### Why the extrapolation deserves scepticism

A labelling company whose product *is* human expertise evaluated against models is the least representative firm for this ratio. Its employees are the input to the product, so inference spend relative to their salaries is structurally unlike, say, a logistics firm's.

The ratio is a real data point at one company. The leap to double-digit GDP growth is described by the newsletter itself as something the ratio "points to" — not a measurement.

**Caveat.** Opening section only.
