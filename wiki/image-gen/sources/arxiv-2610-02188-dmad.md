## Narrative

**The idea.** DMAD recasts the score-difference distillation of DMD as a *classification* problem. Two discriminator heads — teacher-vs-student and real-vs-student — sit on a shared backbone, and a linear loss on their logits trains the few-step student. Proposition 1 proves that at the discriminator optimum the logit gradient equals DMD's distribution-matching gradient. The practical payoff is that the auxiliary score critic and the online teacher both disappear.

**Engineering.** Balanced BCE per head; generator loss is minus the expectation of the weighted sum of the two head outputs. Gap-based reweighting uses the real head's EMA mean-logit gap between real and teacher samples per noise band (beta=0.99) to up-weight the teacher signal in low-gap bands. Teacher samples are cached, and the discriminator-to-student update ratio is 1:1 where DMD2 uses 5:1.

**Results.** ImageNet-64 1-step FID 1.24 against DMD2's 1.51 (1.04 with a projected discriminator). SDXL 4-step COCO-10K FID 14.47 against DMD2 19.32 and the SDXL teacher 19.36. Most relevant here: **Wan2.1 4-step VBench total 84.70 (1.3B) and 85.15 (14B), both beating DMD2, rCM and the 50-step teacher** (83.02 / 83.59). On MiniMax-H3 the 4-step student is preferred 79.1% over DMD2 and 84.6% over rCM in human preference. Per-generator-update training time drops about 4.2x on Wan-14B (162.9s to 38.5s).

**Phase-0 (2026-10-02).** Project page `yzmblog.github.io/projects/DMAD` claims code, models and demos, and says training/eval code "will publicly release" — **not yet out, and no licence stated**. Training used 8x H100 for the small cases and 64x H100 for Wan-14B, so this is not locally trainable; the operator waits for the *released student weights*. **Verdict: WATCH-full** — on the wiki's own Wan distillation track.

**How it relates to what the wiki already tracks.** It is a re-derivation rather than a new objective: it provably recovers the DMD gradient. The novelty is the estimator plus engineering (no critic, no online teacher, cheaper training, better sub-4-step quality). `@entities/models/longlive-plug.md` solves a different problem — it is an inference-time modular LoRA for long video — so the two are complementary, not competing.
