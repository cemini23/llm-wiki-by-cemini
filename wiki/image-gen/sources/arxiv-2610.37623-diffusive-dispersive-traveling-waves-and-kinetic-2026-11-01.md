## Narrative

The paper studies scalar hyperbolic conservation laws whose flux is smooth and non-convex. The flux may have an arbitrary finite number of inflection points. Earlier kinetic-relation theory, cited in the introduction, assumed a single inflection point. The authors extend the traveling-wave construction to this broader flux class.

They treat classical and nonclassical shock waves as traveling-wave limits of a linear diffusive-dispersive regularization. They classify these profiles in the phase plane. Within the speed range of the generalized Maxwell construction, the main result classifies the left outgoing profiles by their limiting equilibria. The construction uses a generalization of Maxwell's equal area rule. That rule supplies an invariant region in phase space.

In an elementary bowtie configuration, the authors prove nonclassical shock profiles with arbitrarily many oscillations. For nested configurations, they order the saddle connections on each entry interval and account for inaccessible saddles. They state that the number of oscillations in the non-classical shock depends on the diffusion-dispersion ratio. Along the elementary families of saddle connections, that number tends to infinity as the ratio tends to zero. The abstract gives no finite measured count, speed, or ratio. These limit claims are single-source statements from this paper.

The abstract does not discuss image generators, sampling, or multimodal models. Here "diffusive-dispersive" names the PDE regularization of a conservation law. A generative-image reader can use this page as a record of that shock-profile classification.
