## Narrative

Transferring atomic quantum sensors from laboratory physics packages to **compact, alignment-free sensor heads** for field and space deployment needs a structure that combines **mechanical, thermal, and optical** function in one manufacturable component.

The paper's answer is a platform built from **additively manufactured technical ceramics**, using **lithography-based ceramic manufacturing** to fabricate monolithic micro-optical structures.

### Why the ceramic choice is the argument

Most additive platforms are polymer. A quantum sensor head needs three things polymer does not give together:

- **dimensional stability** across temperature — the optics must stay aligned without a mechanism;
- **thermal conductivity** — heat has to leave the package;
- **low outgassing** — a sealed sensor head cannot have volatiles condensing on a surface.

Technical ceramic supplies all three. That is why a *manufacturing* paper sits behind a *sensor* result: the process is what makes the alignment-free design possible.

### Why it is filed here

This is a genuine additive-manufacturing application at the frontier of what printed ceramics can do, from a national institute. It is the class of work that defines the ceiling of the technique rather than the desktop-printer floor.

**Caveat.** Abstract only. No tolerance, shrinkage, surface-finish, or thermal figure reproduced.
