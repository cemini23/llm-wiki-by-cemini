## Raw Concept

Bambu Lab's entry-level open-frame line (A1 released 2024; A1 mini released 2023). Architecturally distinct from the [@entities/printers/x1c.md] / [@entities/printers/p1s.md] CoreXY siblings — the A1 is a **bed-slinger** (bed moves on Y; toolhead moves on X+Z). This is the cheapest path into the closed-loop Bambu ecosystem and the only Bambu line for readers who want a printer under $500.

## Narrative

### What you get for ~$400 (A1 bare) / ~$560 (A1 Combo)

[TENTATIVE 2026-05-08 — specs reflect late-2025 community knowledge; verify the current Bambu product page.]

- **Geometry**: bed-slinger — bed moves in Y, toolhead moves in X + Z. Different dynamics envelope vs CoreXY siblings (see "What this changes" below).
- **Build volume**: 256 × 256 × 256 mm (A1); 180 × 180 × 180 mm (A1 mini).
- **Hotend**: stainless steel nozzle; 300°C max; quick-swap (the A1 line ships with the easiest-swap hotend in Bambu's lineup).
- **Build plate**: heated; PEI textured.
- **Enclosure**: **none** — open frame. This is the disqualifying feature for [@entities/materials/abs.md] / [@entities/materials/asa.md] / engineering filaments that need controlled chamber temperature.
- **Sensors** (a different mix than P1S):
  - **Lidar** — yes; first-layer flow inspection + bed-flatness mesh.
  - **AI camera** — yes; same failure-detection classifier as X1C / P1S.
  - **Accelerometer** — yes; drives input shaping. **Bed-slinger resonance dynamics differ from CoreXY**: Y-axis resonance scales with build mass on a moving bed, so the empty-bed calibration drifts as the print grows. See [@concepts/input-shaping.md].
- **AMS Lite**: simpler 4-spool external changer (not the enclosed AMS unit on X1C / P1S). Cheaper, less robust, dries less effectively. AMS Lite is **not** compatible with the enclosed AMS, and vice versa.
- **Network**: WiFi only; LAN-only mode supported.

### A1 vs A1 mini

The A1 mini is the same architecture in a smaller box — 180 × 180 × 180 mm vs 256³, ~$300 bare vs ~$400. Same sensors, same AMS Lite compatibility, same open-frame disqualification for engineering filaments. Choose the mini when build-volume requirements are modest and price-per-printer matters; choose the A1 when you'll outgrow 180³ within a year.

### What this changes (bed-slinger trade-offs)

The bed-slinger geometry is meaningfully different from CoreXY in three ways:

- **Print mass affects resonance** — the bed (with the print on it) is on the Y axis. As the print grows taller, the moving mass increases, and the Y-axis resonant frequency drops. Active Tuning calibrated at print-start is less accurate by mid-print. CoreXY siblings (X1C, P1S) keep print mass stationary and don't have this drift.
- **Tall thin parts wobble** — at high speed, a tall print on a moving bed gets shaken back-and-forth. For tall parts, the A1 needs lower acceleration than its CoreXY siblings to maintain quality.
- **Footprint is wider** — a bed-slinger needs Y-axis swing room. The A1's full operating footprint is wider than the X1C's, even though both are nominally 256³ build volume.

These aren't deal-breakers — Bambu's tuning compensates for them — but they explain why the A1's marketed top speed is similar to the P1S/X1C while real-world quality-prints run at lower acceleration.

### What it does well

- **Cheapest closed-loop Bambu** — at ~$400 bare, the A1 is half the [@entities/printers/p1s.md] price and a third of the [@entities/printers/x1c.md] price.
- **Lidar included** — unlike the P1S, the A1 ships with lidar. First-layer reliability is comparable to the X1C.
- **Quick-swap hotend** — the A1's hotend is the easiest in the Bambu lineup to change. Useful when running multiple nozzle sizes or when a clog forces a swap mid-job.
- **Quiet operation** — bed-slinger architecture + careful tuning makes the A1 audibly quieter than the P1S in many environments.
- **Same Bambu Studio + MakerWorld experience** — slicer, profiles, AMS Lite workflow are productized to the same standard as X1C / P1S.

### What it doesn't do

- **No engineering filaments** — open-frame, no chamber control. ABS / ASA / engineering composites will warp, delaminate, or print poorly. PLA / PETG / TPU only [@concepts/filaments-baseline.md].
- **AMS Lite is downgraded** — fewer drying capabilities, less robust spool detection, no enclosed humidity control. For high-throughput multi-color work, AMS Lite limits production scale [@concepts/print-farm-operations.md].
- **Bed-slinger resonance drift** — print quality on tall prints degrades vs CoreXY at high speed.
- **No firmware modification path** — same closed-firmware lock-in as rest of Bambu line.

### When to choose A1 / A1 mini

- **Your budget is under $500 total** — A1 / A1 mini is the only Bambu in this band.
- **You only need PLA / PETG / TPU** — open-frame is fine for these.
- **You're printing decorative / hobby work** — AI-design-tool pipeline [@concepts/ai-design-tools.md] for Etsy / MakerWorld decorative items doesn't need engineering filaments.
- **You'll add a second printer later** — the A1 is an excellent starter; you can graduate to a P1S or X1C as a second machine when engineering work justifies the cost.

### When to NOT choose A1

- **You need ABS / ASA / composites** → [@entities/printers/p1s.md] (composites with hardened upgrade) or [@entities/printers/x1c.md] (composites + lidar by default).
- **Your prints are tall + must be high-speed-quality** → CoreXY sibling avoids the bed-slinger resonance drift.
- **You're scaling a multi-printer farm** → AMS Lite limitations make the A1 less practical at fleet size 4+.

### Day-1 setup priorities

1. Auto-bed-leveling + lidar calibration on first connect (Bambu Studio prompts).
2. Print [@entities/tools/kickstarter-autodesk-fdm-protocol.md] FDM Test V4 — per-machine baseline.
3. Bambu PLA Basic for the first 1-2 prints.
4. K-value calibration when switching to third-party PLA / PETG [@concepts/extrusion-control.md].
5. Re-run input shaping after moving the printer to a different surface (bed-slinger resonance is sensitive to the support stand).
6. Enable LAN-only mode in Settings if cloud isn't needed.
