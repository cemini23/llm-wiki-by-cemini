## Narrative

Google bans businesses from buying, selling, or manipulating reviews. A reader can report a review that breaks the rules. The report is a signal, not a decision. Google judges each case [Source: https://www.sterlingsky.ca/when-google-catches-business-buying-fake-reviews/ (retrieved 2026-11-26)].

**What the policy bans.** Reviews must come from real experiences and stay "honest, unbiased, and based on actual interactions". The article lists six banned practices:

1. **Bought or sold reviews.** Paying people or firms for positive reviews is banned, even when the reviews look real.
2. **Incentivized or biased reviews.** Discounts, freebies, or other rewards in exchange for a review are banned. So is paying a customer to change or remove a negative review.
3. **Fake customer experiences.** Reviews from people who never used the business are banned.
4. **Multiple accounts or coordination.** Posting or coordinating across many accounts to skew ratings is banned.
5. **Competitor attacks.** Fake negative reviews on a rival's profile are banned.
6. **Review gating.** Sending happy customers to a review form while steering unhappy customers away is banned.

The article states the sixth ban as a rule of its own. A business may ask all customers for honest reviews. It may not shape what they say or reward the good ones. The full rules sit in Google's "Prohibited and Restricted Content Policy".

**Report one review.** Do this in Google Maps:

1. Find the business listing.
2. Find the review.
3. Open the **three-dot menu** beside the review.
4. Select the report option.
5. Pick the reason that best matches the violation.
6. Submit the report.

**Document the pattern.** One review is weak evidence. The article advises that you save a file with these items:

- screenshots, dates, and review URLs
- messages that offer to sell reviews
- proof of paid or incentivized reviews
- sudden bursts of five-star reviews, near-identical wording, or suspicious accounts
- a pattern of removals followed by new spikes

**Pick the right channel.**

| Case | Channel |
|------|---------|
| One review | The Maps report flow, or the "Google Reviews Management Tool" |
| Systematic manipulation | The "Google Business Profile Help Community" for escalation guidance |

The **"Business Redressal Complaint Form" is the wrong channel for reviews.** It covers a fraudulent name, phone number, or URL only.

**Evidence standard.** The article sets no formal standard. It calls the signs "warning signs rather than proof", because a real business can also get a burst of similar reviews. Google makes the final call. A report does not guarantee removal.

**What can follow a report.**

- Google reviews the report and may remove the review.
- A temporary block on new reviews can follow. The article calls this "review jail".
- Ratings can drop when reviews are removed.
- A **"Suspected Fake Reviews"** warning label can appear on the profile. The label appeared in the UK from 2024.

The article gives no timelines. It says reports often must be repeated.

**One tracked case.** A single location moved through these review counts after a July 2022 removal:

| Point | Reviews | Rating |
|-------|---------|--------|
| Before removal | 60 | 4.1 |
| After removal | 18 | 3.0 |
| After a new purchase | 89 | 4.6 |
| October cut | 25 | 3.7 |
| After a new purchase | 37 | 4.1 |
| December cut | 20 | 3.4 |
| Later recovery | 100 | 4.6 |

The case shows two things. Google can remove bought reviews in waves. The profile can recover over time.

**Thresholds.** The article states no numeric threshold, no review minimum, and no reporting limit. It says a rank drop or a suspension after removal is possible but not certain.

**One dispute.** Commenters disagree on the ranking effect. One reader reports a rank collapse after removals. Another reader reports no change. Both effects stay `[TENTATIVE]`.
