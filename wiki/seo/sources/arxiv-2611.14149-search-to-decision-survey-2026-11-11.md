## Narrative

A survey organised around **six interrelated information-seeking capabilities**, covering the learning signals, optimisation methods, update loci, and credit-assignment mechanisms used to develop each.

The framing question: information seeking now involves decisions about **when** to acquire information, **where** to search, and **how** to evaluate and use evidence. Recent post-training improves several of these, but **their objectives and evaluations span different tasks and system settings** — which makes the results hard to compare.

### The organisation that makes it useful

Sorting by **capability** rather than by method is the right cut, because the methods differ less than the capabilities do. The survey reports gains in retrieval routing, query refinement, multi-tool exploration, evidence-grounded reasoning, and resource-aware search — and notes the scope of behavioural evidence varies.

**Why it is filed here.** It is a map of a field this workspace keeps meeting piecemeal: the metacognitive search-reward paper, the incomplete-relevance-judgment paper, the retrieval-exposure defence, DeliverBench's traceability finding. Those are five points on this survey's grid.

**Caveat.** Abstract only. The six capabilities are named but their treatment is not in the extracted text.
