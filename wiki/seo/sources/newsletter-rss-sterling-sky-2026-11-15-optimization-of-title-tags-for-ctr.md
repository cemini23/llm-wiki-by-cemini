## Narrative

The RSS body in the inbox truncates. The test results below come from the full published page, fetched 2026-11-22. Treat every percentage as a single-site test result from one agency.

Hawkins tested title-tag rewrites on pages that already rank well but get few clicks. She found such pages in Google Search Console, then wrote titles that address intent.

**Three tests.**
- Anonymous Google reviews page on Sterling Sky's own site: the new title explained how to do the task and what it does. Clicks rose 65%.
- Personal-injury law-firm page: the new title led with a direct answer and kept the main keyword and the "lawyers near you" phrase. Traffic grew 63%.
- Behavioral-activation page on a psychiatrist's site: the new title opened with a benefit ("Stop Waiting For Motivation"). Clicks rose 400%.

**Failures.**
- Removing the main keyword from a review-policy article caused a large click drop.
- When the displayed title did not match the H1, aligning the H1 with the start of the title tag helped.
- Sometimes the title is not the problem: one stronger title did worse because Reddit and Google-owned results ranked above it. Hawkins says to change back if the original performs better.

**Rules Hawkins gives.** Keep the top keyword, address intent, align the H1 with the title, and always test. She calls this a quick win for large libraries of older articles that still rank, because it needs no new content and no change in rank.

The lesson for local SEO: a good ranking is not the same as a good click rate. The title tag is a cheap tuning point, and the gain comes from intent match, not from more keywords.
