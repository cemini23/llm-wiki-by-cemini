## Narrative

LLM-generated explanations of a model sound fluent and are **not necessarily faithful to the model**. The paper names this the **faithfulness gap** and closes it with a loop.

Two-stage pipeline:

1. An **explainer LLM** produces textual descriptions at increasing levels of detail.
2. An **independent validator LLM** extracts a symbolic expression from the text, which is then compared numerically against the original model.

Framed as a discrete search over explanation space: **minimise complexity subject to a faithfulness constraint.**

Results over 250 runs at varying model complexity: **84.0% pass rate**, with most explanations converging at low complexity and only a small fraction needing detail.

### The mechanism worth copying

The validator does not judge the explanation's quality. It **extracts a machine-checkable artefact from it and compares numerically.** A fluent-but-wrong explanation fails the comparison even if it reads well.

That is the difference between asking a model "is this explanation good" and asking "does the expression recovered from this explanation equal the model". The second is falsifiable; the first is not.

The complexity-adaptive part follows: start simple, add detail only until the expression matches.

**Relevance.** Any place this workspace emits a natural-language justification — a prompt's rationale, a wiki page's claim — the same discipline applies: a claim that cannot be checked against source is not yet a claim.

**Caveat.** Abstract only.
