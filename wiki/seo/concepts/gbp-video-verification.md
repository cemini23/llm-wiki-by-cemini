## Raw Concept

This page answers one question: how does a business pass Google Business Profile video verification on the first try? The source is @sources/newsletter-rss-sterling-sky-2026-11-22-google-business-profile-verification-9-easy-step.md, a Sterling Sky practitioner article fetched 2026-11-25.

## Narrative

Video verification is a Google check that a listing is real. It is one method among several, but Google can require it, and it can stack methods. The clip must prove three things: **location**, **existence of the business**, and **management** of it [TENTATIVE — single agency source, consistent with Google help text].

**The rules.**

- Length: at least 30 seconds. 60–90 seconds is a safer target when uploads fail.
- One continuous take. No edits, no pre-recorded file.
- Filmed live on a mobile device.
- Upload through the Google Maps app, signed in to the account that manages the profile.
- Allow camera, location, and microphone permissions.
- No faces, no sensitive data, no private records.
- A branded email on an account with history is safer than a Gmail address [TENTATIVE].
- If the upload fails, retry several times before shortening the clip.

**The nine shots.** Group each shot under one of the three proofs.

| Group | Shot |
|-------|------|
| Location | The area around the business: street signs, intersections, nearby businesses, building numbers |
| Location | Permanent signage that matches the profile name. Indoors, point out of a window at a landmark or sign |
| Operations | The workspace or work site |
| Operations | Tools, equipment, or inventory in use or on site |
| Operations | Branded assets: uniforms, vehicles, cards, brochures |
| Operations | Open the vehicle or kit and show the tools inside (service area businesses) |
| Affiliation | Access to the location: unlock and enter, or unlock a vehicle |
| Affiliation | Official documents: licence or tax registration, held steady and readable |
| Affiliation | Access to a business system: CRM, accounting software, or MLS for realtors |

**After upload.** Review can take up to five business days. Do not send a second video to hurry it. Google may stay silent on failure, so check the Maps app daily. A "Review issues" warning shows the reason. "Get verified" with the video option again means a silent failure. "Get verified" without that option means success. A review longer than one week is a fault; use the verification status tool to open a support ticket.

**The usual failure causes.** Mismatched name, address, or phone across the site, listings, and documents. An address Google does not accept for the business type. A name with added keywords. A video that does not clearly show all three proofs. Missing real photos. Duplicate profiles. Frequent detail changes. A PO box, virtual office, or mailbox store. A different address on Yelp or Facebook. A minor policy violation.

**Why it belongs on a local-SEO page.** A failed verification blocks the listing. It also signals the same defects that hurt local ranking: inconsistent NAP, keyword-stuffed names, and thin proof of a real location. Fix the cause, not only the video. See @concepts/citation-building.md for the NAP half and @concepts/google-business-profile.md for the listing half.

**Caution.** The article says video is "the only method" in one place and then lists five other methods. Ignore that line. Google's help text says a profile may need more than one method.
