## Raw Concept

This page answers one question: when does more published content make a site weaker? The source is @sources/newsletter-rss-sterling-sky-2026-11-22-the-dangers-of-thin-and-scaled-ai-content.md, a Sterling Sky article fetched 2026-11-25.

## Narrative

Google calls the failure "scaled content abuse": many pages generated mainly to manipulate rankings, not to help users. The risk is not only that those pages do not rank. The thesis in this source is that the loss can reach the **whole site** [TENTATIVE — one agency, two case patterns, no controlled test].

**Two traits mark the risk.**

1. The content is unoriginal. It repeats what already exists, such as local news reports.
2. The content falls outside the site's area of expertise.

**The case pattern.** A personal injury law firm wrote one page per local crash reported in the news. Organic rankings fell sharply, and branded search reportedly was lost too. The drop appears tied to the May 2026 core update. A second firm lost ground with a single page. That weakens the idea that page count alone causes the loss.

**The topical-fit test.** Ask one question before publishing: would this audience expect this content from this site? People who search for one crash want news, and Google shows news sources. A law firm explaining what to do after an accident fits its expertise. Hundreds of crash summaries do not.

**Practical rules.**

- Do not scale pages that only restate an existing source.
- Check topical fit before production, not after.
- Publish what ties directly to the services and the expertise.
- Do not run experiments on how much low-value content Google will allow.
- Judge an update by organic position trends. Do not judge it by tool-estimated traffic.

**A supporting signal from search engineering.** An AI search relevance model is trained to separate neighbouring relevance grades, not just to accept or reject a page. See @sources/arxiv-larf-learning-aligned-reasoning-search-relevance-2611.32125.md. A page that is only partly on topic sits near a grade boundary. That is the least safe place to be. This link is this wiki's inference and is `[TENTATIVE]`.

**What this page does not claim.** There is no published count of lost pages, no traffic numbers, and no proof of causation. The source author states the same limit. Treat the mechanism as credible and the scale as unknown.
