## Narrative

Wu Blockchain's 31 October 2026 weekly roundup lists ten project updates. The post restates company statements and governance posts. Every figure below is a single-source claim from this newsletter.

Brad Garlinghouse, CEO of Ripple, says the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency has formally approved Ripple National Trust Bank. The post says this lets Ripple support stablecoin RLUSD on a federally regulated banking platform and offer digital-asset custody for enterprises. Ripple says RLUSD is live on Coinbase Prime, Coinbase Exchange, and retail applications. Those venues support 1:1 conversion of US dollars and other supported US-dollar stablecoins into RLUSD. RLUSD will later be available on Coinbase Global Exchange, including use as collateral. Deposits and withdrawals are supported on the XRP Ledger, Base, and Ethereum. Ripple will provide liquidity on Base.

Hyperliquid says Payward, parent of Kraken, plans to be the first CFTC-regulated deployer of HIP-3 markets. The stated aim is perpetual-contract trading for US customers on Hyperliquid infrastructure. The post says the plan is pending regulatory approval. Bitnomial, the CFTC-regulated entity under Payward, would create and administer the markets and would clear and settle contracts. NinjaTrader Clearing, a registered futures commission merchant, would manage customer accounts. Hyperliquid plans to ship HIP-3 in a network upgrade so deployers can open permissioned markets by on-chain whitelisting. The post says the feature is optional and leaves existing HIP-3 markets unchanged. HIP-3* is live on testnet, and the specifications remain subject to change. Pump fun says it has integrated Hyperliquid perpetual trading and that users need no cross-chain transfer. Coverage includes cryptocurrencies, US stocks, and commodities. The post quotes leverage of up to 40x on Bitcoin, 25x on Ethereum, and 20x on US stock contracts such as Tesla and NVIDIA. The stated platform fee is 0.05% per trade, plus Hyperliquid's standard fees. A Bybit line in the same section says WSOT 2026 runs from 29 October to 2 December, with five trading tracks and prizes of up to $10 million.

CZ restates "Multiple chains, one token" for a new BNB Chain layer-1. Cited BNB Chain documentation says the new chain will coexist with BSC and connect through a native cross-chain bridge. The post says no separate token will be issued, and BNB will still pay fees and serve staking and governance. The chain name is not set. Stated targets are a block time of 200 milliseconds and about 20 milliseconds for transaction pre-confirmation. An internal multi-validator devnet is running. Testnet is planned for the end of 2026 and mainnet for early 2027. Scroll's 30 October DAO summary restates a shift to a permissioned network and a migration of SCR and USX to Ethereum mainnet. A detailed proposal will go through governance later. Scroll is preparing positions.scroll.io so users can check positions and transfer or withdraw assets. Compass, Scroll's AI application, has paid subscriptions on iOS. The Android version and more fiat payment methods are still in development. ENS says ENSv2 is live on Ethereum mainnet with an updated ENS App and ENS Explorer. Each name can hold a separate registry for its subnames, with finer permissions so several parties can co-manage one namespace. The post says more than 40 teams have completed v2 integration, including 1inch, Aragon, Arbitrum, Jumper, Lighter, MetaMask, and Safe. Existing names stay valid. Eligible .eth names may upgrade to v2. ENS has also released a commemorative NFT.

Osmosis forum member JohnnyWyles proposes a transfer of 100 million OSMO from the community pool to a burn address. The post says that amount is about 12.6% of a total supply of 794 million tokens and about 5.8 million US dollars at the stated price. It describes the tokens as a prior "soft-burn" that governance could still reactivate. A permanent burn would close that path. After execution the community pool would keep about 19.3 million OSMO plus other assets. The proposal was under discussion, with an intended on-chain submission date of 30 October. The Optimism Foundation proposes sequencer and priority-queue fee rebates for partners that bring incremental volume to OP Mainnet. Rebates are denominated in ETH and funded only from new network fees those partners generate. The post says the mechanism uses no OP tokens and does not expand circulating supply, and that it still adds net incremental revenue to the Superchain OP buyback program. Terms include milestone thresholds, caps, and a fixed term. Individual agreement details stay undisclosed. Only an overall percentage range will appear in the annual budget report. Uniswap Labs has opened Token Jar fee tool TJAR to other applications, with Robinhood as the first partner. Teams choose the token in which they receive fee revenue. The post says Token Jar processed over $15.3 million in conversions in September. Robinhood has deployed TJAR through the Uniswap API on supported chains and receives fees in USDC and USDG. TJAR is live on seven chains. Its code and audit reports are public.

Sky Frontier Foundation's Q3 2026 report, as quoted, gives Sky Protocol total revenue of $99.29 million, up 14.2% year-on-year. Net revenue is $37.19 million, a 11.8% quarter-on-quarter increase, which the post calls the sixth consecutive quarter of positive net revenue. Total expenses fell 16% quarter-on-quarter to $62.10 million. Collateralized assets reached $11.41 billion. Sky spent $5.6 million on SKY buybacks in the third quarter. Protocol reserves were $85.08 million at quarter-end. For a quant-finance and macro reader, the useful claims are the US regulatory status of RLUSD, the proposed CFTC path onto Hyperliquid perpetuals, the quoted leverage and fee schedule, and the token-supply and protocol-revenue figures. The post supplies no second source for any of those numbers.
