## Narrative

### The turn

The industry has moved from "states do not matter, we are federally regulated" to "maybe we should work with the states". The trigger is a run of poor court outcomes. [Source: substack-rss-event-horizon-2026-11-24-can-prediction-markets-thread-the-needle-on-stat.md]

### Evidence of the pivot

- A New York bill backed by the industry would ban elected officials and policy advisers from trading prediction markets, require age verification, add gambling-harm safeguards, and place a **10 percent surcharge** on operators' net trading fee revenue from New York transactions. The Coalition for Prediction Markets, which includes Kalshi, Coinbase, Underdog and Robinhood, supports it.
- Polymarket chief executive Shayne Coplan said at DealBook that the company is "here and ready to engage" with states.
- The CFTC chair is less dismissive of states than before.

### Why state-by-state is hard

The author's argument is specific. State sportsbooks pay a 51 percent tax rate and paid $25 million for a licence. If a state authorises prediction markets at a much lower tax, a sportsbook can pivot to being a prediction market. That is a tax-revenue threat to the state, so legislators have a reason to keep the rate high or refuse.

New York is the hardest case: the relationship with the governor has been hostile, and the claimed $10 billion of state revenue from prediction-market taxes is described as a fairy tale. North Carolina is the opposite template: a nominal tax passed with little discussion at the end of a budget process, with about $12 million of annual revenue expected by 2031. Oregon may follow that model, but DraftKings is the state lottery's only legal online sportsbook there.

### Legal and market state

- Kalshi remained live in New York after a judge denied a temporary restraining order. A preliminary injunction and an under-21 ban on sports contracts remain possible.
- Kalshi paused operations in Utah, affecting more than 177,000 users, as its co-founders face criminal charges.
- The CFTC's final prediction-market rule is under White House review, received by OMB on 20 November.
- The CFTC issued a final rule on data reporting for fully collateralised event contracts, ending a no-action-letter patchwork.
- New Mexico: a federal judge ordered Kalshi to stop sports event contracts on the lands of four tribes.
- Norway is considering stricter restrictions.

### Market data

The parlay share is the structural fact. Kalshi's volume is mostly not event contracts in the political-information sense; it is sports parlays with a different regulator.
