## Narrative

The paper extends MAPPO with a Graph Attention Network (GAT) encoder for dynamic retail pricing: multiple retailers (agents) set prices for related SKUs on a competition graph, and the GAT encoder lets each agent's policy attend to its graph neighborhood. Experiments are simulated retail environments, not financial markets. Reports show the GAT-MAPPO variant outperforms vanilla MAPPO on revenue and convergence speed, with larger gains when the competition graph is denser.

The paper is methodologically reasonable but narrow: the retail-pricing simulation is handcrafted, there's no real-world retail data validation, and the "graph attention makes MAPPO better" result is not especially novel in the MARL literature. As a self-contained MARL paper it is fine; as a financial research item for Cemini it is tangential.

For Cemini the transferable idea is architectural — using GAT to encode a relationship graph before a MAPPO critic/policy — which could apply to (e.g.) a multi-strategy trading agent system where strategies have known dependencies. But the direct retail-pricing content is not relevant and the paper was miscategorized into the time-series cluster during partitioning.

## Key findings

- MAPPO + GAT encoder for multi-agent retail pricing; graph encodes competitor/substitute structure.
- GAT-MAPPO beats vanilla MAPPO on revenue and convergence in simulated retail environments.
- Gains scale with graph density — consistent with the motivation (graph must be informative).
- Pure simulation, no real retail-data validation.
- Misclassified: this is rl-marl (retail application), not time-series (despite sequential nature).
