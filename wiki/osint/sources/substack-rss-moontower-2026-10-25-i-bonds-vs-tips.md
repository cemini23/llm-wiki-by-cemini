## Narrative

The post opens on a trade the author had already described in a prior article. He says he "nearly bottom-ticked the TIPs market", meaning the entry came close to the low.

The useful part is the stated trade arithmetic. His estimate for the one-year expected return is about 8.50%, with about 7.50% volatility.

He then converts the annual figure to a weekly figure. The conversion divides by the square root of 52:

7.5% / √52 ≈ 1%

So the weekly volatility of the position is about 1%. The post stops at this point in the retrieved text.

The worked example is small but it is the standard volatility scaling rule. Annual volatility divided by the square root of periods per year gives period volatility. The rule assumes independent returns and constant variance, which the post does not discuss.

The 8.50% expected return and the 7.50% volatility are the author's own estimates for his own position. They are not a forecast this wiki endorses.
