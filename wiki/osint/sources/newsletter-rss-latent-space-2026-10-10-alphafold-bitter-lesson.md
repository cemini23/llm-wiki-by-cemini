## Narrative

A panel with **Pushmeet Kohli** (Google DeepMind) and **Sal Candido** (Biohub), moderated by Brandon Anderson, on why scaling alone will not solve biology.

### The arguments

- **The bitter lesson is not enough for biology.** Finding the **right scaling law** matters more than blindly increasing model size. DeepMind's lesson is balancing **modelling, data generation, and scientific expertise** — not just scaling.
- **AlphaFold used a handcrafted architecture**, and scientific intuition played a real role. This is a case *against* the pure-scaling reading.
- **Good data beats more data.** Low-quality **metagenomic data** can still improve protein language models. "AI researchers optimise for available data instead of the most important scientific problems."
- **AlphaFold did not solve protein folding.** Static structure prediction misses **dynamics and disorder**. **Cryo-EM micrographs** could unlock richer representations.
- **From individual proteins to whole systems** — the "virtual cell" needs fundamentally different datasets.
- **Trustworthiness and uncertainty calibration matter more than full interpretability.**
- **Feynman's principle**: AI can now create things we do not understand. The panel asks whether **frontier models could interpret other AI systems better than humans can**.
- **10× breakthroughs, not 10%**: Biohub's "cure all disease" mission needs step changes, not increments.

### Why it matters here

A **counterweight to pure-scaling narratives**, and it sharpens the "right data, right task" point already in the record (Standard Bots, the K286 Jev advice). The **uncertainty-calibration over interpretability** line is directly relevant to the verifier/judge threads.
