## Narrative

Short preprint proposing "harness engineering" as a named discipline: embedding machine-enforceable architectural constraints in the development toolchain. Preview claim: this practice has emerged independently across organizations navigating AI-driven software engineering but lacks a shared analytical vocabulary or formal definition. Paper offers an initial conceptual framing.

Honest assessment: at 11 pages and preprint-stage, this is a position paper / framework proposal rather than an empirical study. The framing is timely — the "harness" metaphor is indeed converging in the AI-engineering discourse of early 2026 (as the companion paper harness2-4-5 by Maynard also observes, from a linguistic/STS angle). Value is in whether the proposed framework supplies useful vocabulary or structure the field can adopt, not in measurable results.

Relevance to Cemini: the user is building a complex AI-driven trading stack, which is exactly the class of system where harness engineering arguments apply. Useful as conceptual scaffolding for thinking about how to embed constraints (e.g., risk-limit invariants, order-validation gates, state-transition checks) into the dev toolchain rather than at runtime. The companion Maynard paper (harness2-4-5) is the linguistic/framing half of the same conversation.
