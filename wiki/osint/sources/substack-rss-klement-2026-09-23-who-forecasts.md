## Narrative

Klement reviews a paper by **David Thesmar and Emil Verner**. The paper collects a long time series of expected equity returns from surveys across a wide range of constituents. It then compares past realised US market returns with expected returns for the following year.

The results split by respondent type:

| Respondent group | Survey | Behavior vs past returns |
|------------------|--------|--------------------------|
| Professional investors | Value Line (1956–2024) | Expect higher returns when past returns were lower |
| Professional investors | IBES (Thomson Reuters) | Same negative relationship |
| Professional investors | Shiller survey | Same negative relationship |
| Professional economists | Livingston Survey (Philadelphia Fed) | Almost no change in forecasts regardless of past returns |
| Individual investors | Nagel and Xu (NX) | Trend-following: higher past returns imply higher expected returns |
| Company CFOs | Graham and Harvey | Trend-following: same pattern |

The professional pattern is the standard valuation relationship: high past returns imply lower expected forward returns. Economists treat past returns as no indication of the future. Individual investors and CFOs extrapolate.
