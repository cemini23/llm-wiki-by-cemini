## Narrative

A short essay arguing that behavioral finance will remain relevant despite AI's growing dominance in trading. Makes five interconnected arguments, none empirically tested but all conceptually sound.

### Key arguments

1. **Human decision-making still matters**: Retail investors remain a significant force, swayed by fear, greed, and overconfidence. Even institutional investors exhibit cognitive biases during periods of high uncertainty [Source: trading 3 4:5.pdf p.1].

2. **AI reacts to human behavior**: AI models detect and exploit patterns rooted in human psychology (herding, loss aversion). AI's success depends on understanding the human behaviors it exploits — strengthening, not weakening, behavioral finance's role [Source: trading 3 4:5.pdf p.1].

3. **Feedback loops create new dynamics**: Interaction between human investors and AI-driven strategies creates complex feedback loops where each influences the other. Behavioral finance is essential for understanding these emergent dynamics [Source: trading 3 4:5.pdf p.2].

4. **AI's sentiment limitations**: NLP can analyze social media sentiment, but complex emotions (overconfidence, fear) are not easily quantifiable. Behavioral finance provides the framework to understand what AI misses [Source: trading 3 4:5.pdf p.2].

5. **Integration opportunity**: Incorporating behavioral finance theories (recency bias, overreaction) into AI models may outperform purely data-driven approaches — a more holistic framework [Source: trading 3 4:5.pdf p.2].

### Cemini relevance
