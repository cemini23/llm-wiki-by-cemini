### CoreWeave

Q2 revenue was **$2.575bn**, up **112%**. Backlog was about **$104bn**. But the quarter carried a **$626m net loss** and **$640m of net interest expense**. [Source: substack-rss-wublock-2026-11-09-wublockchain-weekly-outlook-us-cpi-and-ppi-test.md]

The newsletter's own bottom line is the right one: a $104bn backlog does not matter if delivery and financing cost consume it. Quarterly interest expense is about 25% of quarterly revenue.

### Applied Materials and Cisco

- **Applied Materials** revenue grew 25% to **$9.12bn** and the guide is about **$10.25bn**.
- **Cisco** revenue grew 18% to **$17.3bn**, with **$4bn** of quarterly AI-infrastructure orders and **$7.5bn** of related revenue expected in fiscal 2027.

### Why it matters here

This post supplies the two numbers that the Capital Flows thesis lacks: the backlog and the interest expense. Together they turn "AI credit risk" from a narrative into a ratio. The ratio to track is **quarterly net interest expense against quarterly revenue**, and the second is **backlog conversion rate**.

The macro calendar is also a GuruWatcher input. CPI, PPI, and claims are scheduled points at which a level can be tested against a prior print.

## Dead Ends

- **Reading backlog as revenue.** The backlog is undiscounted and undelivered. The interest cost accrues now.
- **Treating the calendar as a forecast.** The post lists prior prints. It does not model the next one.
