## Narrative

The post says long-term bond yields have increased substantially since 2025. That timing claim is single-source. The post says concern is rising that the rise reflects solvency fears for the United States, Japan, and other countries. The post says a country's fiscal position does not set its bond yields alone. A relative comparison with similar countries also sets yields.

The post cites an IMF analysis of long-term government bond yields for 80 emerging markets. The count of 80 is a single-source figure. The analysis covers factors besides the absolute debt level. The chart title in the file is "Drivers of sovereign risk spreads of emerging markets". The file credits Avila-Yiptong et al. (2026).

The post says local inflation rates matter most. The relative debt level versus similar countries ranks next. The post says that relative debt level matters more than market uncertainty, proxied by the VIX, and more than real GDP growth. These ranks are single-source claims. The file does not quote chart weights or a yield change in basis points.

The stored body ends with a bear analogy. A person escapes the bear by running faster than the slowest runner. For a quant-finance and macro reader, the usable claim is cross-sectional. In this emerging-market sample, local inflation and peer-relative debt outrank the VIX and real GDP growth as drivers of sovereign risk. The developed-market solvency worry is the frame. The file supplies the ranking. The file does not supply the chart numbers.
