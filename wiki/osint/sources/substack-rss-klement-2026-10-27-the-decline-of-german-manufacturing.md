## Narrative

The post covers the "second China shock". The term describes Chinese firms competing with European manufacturers in high-tech goods, both in European home markets and in third countries. The post states the effect hits European manufacturers hard, and German ones in particular.

The evidence comes from the Kiel Institute for the World Economy, reported in Chowdhry et al. (2026). The study measures the change in market share of Chinese and EU firms in third markets between the periods 2012–14 and 2022–24.

Two charts carry the result. The first shows that EU manufacturers lost third-market share while Chinese manufacturers gained it. The exceptions are a few goods: leather goods, mostly luxury items such as handbags, plus art and weapons. The second chart translates the share change into export growth rates and shows how much faster Chinese exports grew than European ones.

The sharper finding is the country breakdown. The study measures how much more German exports changed than a proportional change in line with the European average. German manufacturers lost ground in their core strengths. The named categories are cars, machinery, electrical equipment, and optical and medical equipment.

The investment reading is that the loss is concentrated in the products Germany was strongest in. That is a different problem from a general cost-competitiveness squeeze, because it erodes the high-margin end of the export book.

The underlying Kiel study is not read here. All figures are second-hand through Klement.
