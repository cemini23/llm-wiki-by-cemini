## Narrative

Wu Blockchain interviews Luuk Strijers, Coinbase’s Head of International Derivatives and former CEO of Deribit. The episode covers the Deribit integration, crypto options, and the product roadmap. The inbox file dates the post to 2026-10-29. The page says the transcript was generated with AI and may contain errors. Every figure below is a single-source claim from this interview.

Strijers says Deribit joined Coinbase for distribution. He states that about 80% of Deribit clients were institutional and about 20% were sophisticated active traders. Coinbase adds custody clients, retail users, and regulated entities. On 1 October the firm merged Coinbase International Exchange and Deribit into Coinbase Global Exchange. Perpetual futures came first. Options and other products follow. He says some integration work remains, and progress has been faster than the team expected.

He gives two market figures. Notional open interest returned to about $50 billion, near levels from the fourth quarter of last year. After the 1 October integration, open interest kept growing while the Bitcoin price fell. He says the integration is only a few weeks old, so the team should not treat the merger as the full cause. Trading fell across derivatives in the first half of the year and recovered around August and September. A matching-engine upgrade improved median round-trip latency by about 50–60 times, to about 75 microseconds. He also tracks P95 and P99 latency. He says faster quotes can tighten spreads and deepen the book. He says these latency figures are not directly comparable with CME or Eurex, because crypto venues run extra margin and risk checks inside the exchange.

The venue now lists perpetual futures linked to gold, oil, Tesla, equity indices, and pre-IPO companies. He says demand for traditional-asset perpetuals has been encouraging. Pre-IPO perpetuals have missed expectations. Open interest and volume stay limited on this venue and in the wider market. Deribit had about 175 employees. Coinbase has several thousand. Fee, product, and infrastructure decisions now take longer. He says retention has been healthy.

On capital, he says little new money entered crypto options over the past year. Some flow came through U.S. ETFs and new clients, and downturns brought withdrawals. Coinbase custodies hundreds of billions of dollars in crypto. The plan is to let clients use assets in custody for hedges and basis trades. As of the 20 October conversation, U.S. clients could not trade Deribit options directly. The stated plan was institutional access before the end of October and retail access in early November. The core liquidity pool sits in Dubai. The firm wants eligible clients on one global book where rules allow, and it is seeking more access in the United States and Europe.

For a quant-finance and macro reader, the usable snapshot is the positioning he describes. At the time of the talk, Bitcoin call strikes at $90,000 and $95,000 mattered, and October and December expirations were the active dates. The annualized futures basis was about 5.5%, above the risk-free rate. Investors still bought downside protection. He reads the set as a modest bullish bias and cautious optimism, with no strong directional consensus. He also says a trader can be right on Bitcoin’s direction and still lose on an option if implied volatility falls or time value decays.
