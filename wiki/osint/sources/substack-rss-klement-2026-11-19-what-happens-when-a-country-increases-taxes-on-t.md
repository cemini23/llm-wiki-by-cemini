## Narrative

The case is the Netherlands' 2012 reform of the "30% ruling", a tax break for skilled foreign workers that allowed a maximum 30% income tax rate for up to ten years. The reform added a wage threshold and removed eligibility for people living within 150 km of the border. About 55,000 people used the scheme in 2012, and some lost the benefit sooner than expected. [Source: substack-rss-klement-2026-11-19-what-happens-when-a-country-increases-taxes-on-t.md]

A study by Jose Giarola and colleagues finds a threshold effect. Users below the 90% income threshold stayed and paid the higher tax. The top 5% and top 1% earners were about 10% less likely to stay.

The fiscal arithmetic: leavers cost the government about €516.9m in taxes over five years. Stayers paid about €497.3m more. The net effect was an average annual tax loss of about €1.6m. Against 2019 income-tax receipts of almost €67bn, the authors call the reform cost-neutral. [Source: substack-rss-klement-2026-11-19-what-happens-when-a-country-increases-taxes-on-t.md]

The transferable point is the elasticity of the top tail, not the Dutch case. A tax change aimed at high earners can trade revenue from the many for exit of the few. Klement also cites Chris Rokos moving to Greece, with about £330m of annual UK tax at stake. Tag the Rokos figure `[TENTATIVE]`; it is a secondary claim.
