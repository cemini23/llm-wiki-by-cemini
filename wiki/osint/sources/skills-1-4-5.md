## Narrative

A short conference-talk paper arguing that generative AI is shifting workplace proficiency from **tool operation** (prompting ChatGPT, Gemini) toward a paradigm where individuals **manage AI systems like human subordinates**. Daly centers the emerging role of the **"Agent Boss"** (a term he attributes to Microsoft's 2025 research) — a professional who oversees, directs, and strategically delegates to autonomous AI agents — and proposes a *managerial AI Skill Stack* of skills for it.

The framing problem: AI automation is absorbing the routine, foundational tasks (drafting communications, summarizing meetings, background research, spreadsheet analysis) that historically served as career entry points for new graduates, so junior roles are contracting. The paper cites secondary survey evidence — PwC (56% of CEOs see AI boosting time efficiency), Deloitte (daily GenAI users save ~6.3 hrs/week), LinkedIn's 2025 Future of Work report (63% of leaders expect AI to automate most junior tasks), and Anthropic's projection that up to 50% of entry-level white-collar jobs could vanish within a decade — alongside elevated youth unemployment in Taiwan, Korea, and Japan. It traces AI's evolution through tool → multimodal → reasoning → agentic stages and concludes that graduates increasingly compete not with AI but with people who manage AI, calling on educators to redefine early-career readiness.

**Cemini relevance**: peripheral, secondary-survey-based context for the workforce/skills backdrop behind the `llm-agents` and `ai-ethics` clusters — the "Agent Boss" / oversee-direct-delegate framing echoes (in human-management terms) the agent-supervision and monitoring concerns that matter for Cemini's autonomous-agent governance. Maturity kept `draft`: a conference talk relying on press and vendor surveys, not primary research.
