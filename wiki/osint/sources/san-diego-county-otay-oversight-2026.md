## Narrative

San Diego County became the **first California county** to invoke a **2024 state law** allowing local public-health inspections of private immigration detention centers. After **February 2026** access was blocked or curtailed at the **CoreCivic-owned Otay Mesa Detention Center**, the county sued **DHS, ICE, CoreCivic**, and federal leadership in federal court.

**Feb 2026 access attempt:** Supervisors **Terra Lawson-Remer** and **Paloma Aguirre** turned away; county public health officer admitted but denied detainee interviews and medical records needed for a full inspection `[CONFIRMED media]`.

**Mar 10, 2026 complaint:** Seeks court-ordered access under state-authorized health oversight; argues federal block violated **Administrative Procedure Act** `[CONFIRMED clearinghouse/KPBS]`.

**Jun 4, 2026:** Judge **James Simmons Jr.** (S.D. Cal.) orders inspection **by Jun 17, 2026**; team of up to **four subject-matter experts** (supervisors excluded); CoreCivic must produce policies/procedures `[CONFIRMED KPBS]`.

**Jun 26, 2026:** County conducts **~8-hour** inspection (public health officer, environmental health director, two NCCHC consultants). **Report expected Jul 2026** `[CONFIRMED KPBS]`.

**Jul 2, 2026:** DHS completes **$739.2M** purchase of Otay Mesa from CoreCivic — **six days after** inspection, while litigation active and county report pending `[CONFIRMED 8-K + timeline]`.

**Thesis read:** Illustrates **local oversight warfare** on premium CA ICE sites; federal ownership + retained CXW management is the **structural escape** ICE pursued, but **headline risk** (county report, AB 103, advocacy) does not vanish on closing. Relevant read-through for **GEO Adelanto / Mesa Verde / Golden State** if sold to ICE.
