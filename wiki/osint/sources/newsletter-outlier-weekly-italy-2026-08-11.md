## Narrative

Founder-narrative piece that is, in effect, the **Atto origin story** published to a prediction-markets/geopolitics audience. The author (Italian American, NJ roots, Colledimacine/Abruzzo) describes the citizenship process as a **paperwork bottleneck**, not an emotional one: scanned PDFs in five places, birth spelling that doesn't match the marriage act, a missing civil record from a small comune, FamilySearch results that don't match aunt's paper copies, chat threads that can't remember the town name three days later.

Atto is described as a **local-first desktop kit for organizing Italian civil records and citizenship paperwork**: "Atto does not decide anyone's eligibility. It gives you a private, approve-only place for the documents you collect." Traced family line to the 1700s via family documents + careful online research.

**The product rule stated plainly**: "A fluent sentence is not a civil record." Judgment stays with the user (or retained counsel); software organizes documents, surfaces gaps, suggests the next place to look. The anti-pattern is the "magic genealogy bot" — upload everything, let the model decide — which the author tried and rejected after the model quietly normalized a name and upgraded a maybe into a fact.

Monetization context from the piece's framing: full-service professional help runs into the thousands; government fees are non-refundable; families want "a calmer way to organize the work they are doing themselves" — exactly Atto's kit + concierge positioning.
