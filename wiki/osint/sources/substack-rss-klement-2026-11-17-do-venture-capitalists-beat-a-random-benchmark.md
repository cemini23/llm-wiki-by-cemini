## Narrative

Klement summarises a study by Max Sina Knicker, Jean-Philippe Bouchaud and Michael Benzaquen. The study compares thousands of US venture-capital deals against a benchmark that picks investments at random, with performance measured as money multiple at exit. The answer is no: there is no statistical difference between the funds and the random benchmark. [Source: substack-rss-klement-2026-11-17-do-venture-capitalists-beat-a-random-benchmark.md]

The second finding is conditional. Deals entered at high multiples do worse than a random investment with a similar entry multiple. Deals entered at lower multiples do as expected or better. So funds overpay for trophy assets with high growth prospects, and that overpayment drags fund performance.

Klement's framing is his own and openly hostile to the profession; the study results are the citable part. Tag the result `[TENTATIVE]`: one secondary summary of one paper, with no effect size and no confidence interval given in the newsletter.

Why it matters here: the same overpayment pattern appears in listed markets, so it is a useful prior when a thesis depends on paying up for a "quality" asset. The random-benchmark test is also a good template: before crediting skill, compare to a randomised selection with the same entry conditions.
