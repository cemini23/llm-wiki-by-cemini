## Narrative

Gouker attacks the industry's rhetorical defence: **"prediction markets are not gambling because they do not act as the house."** He **concedes the factual part** — Kalshi takes fees, matches buyers and sellers, and **makes money regardless of who wins** — then rejects the conclusion. **Many things are gambling without a house:** poker (rake), pari-mutuel horse racing, jai alai, **lottery draws (pari-mutuel jackpots)**, bingo, sports betting exchanges. The **UK Gambling Commission licenses betting exchanges** — the exact structure prediction markets claim makes them different.

His substantive worry: **telling users they are "not gambling" distorts how they judge risk.** He notes **Kalshi has itself marketed as "sports betting" in the past.**

### Data points in the issue

**Public sentiment (Reuters/Ipsos):** Most Americans oppose election betting — **60% oppose** vs **only 13% support** betting on election results. **69% of Democrats and 61% of Republicans oppose** betting on election outcomes. Support for **sports** betting was notably higher.

**Volume forecast (Macquarie):** prediction-market **taker volume to reach $187B in 2026** — below the prior $190B forecast but above the earlier $169B, and about **8.5× growth** from 2025's **$22B**.

**Kalshi notional:** **$274B year-to-date**, of which **$114B (≈42%) is parlays**.

**Sunday 11 Oct volume — Kalshi $3,958,473,123 (a record).** Mix: **parlays 69.4%**, **sports 22%**, **crypto 7.9%**.

**Other items:** an **IGA-commissioned KlasRobinson report** estimates prediction markets could cost **tribal and commercial gaming $7.3B–$39.4B per year** (Gouker calls the upper bound unrealistic). The **Alabama-Quassarte Tribal Town** (about 400 members, casinoless) **launched a Kalshi app**, against opposition from **140+ tribes**. **WSJ**: courts now grapple with **betting on jury verdicts**. **Matt Levine/CNBC**: Gensler calls the Kalshi/admin position "frankly absurd"; Levine puts **Kalshi's Supreme Court chances at even money**.

### Why it matters here
Two things to keep separate. **(1) The definitional argument** — it is a messaging and reputational risk for the venue, not a price input. **(2) The volume data** — Kalshi's book is now **parlay-dominated** (69.4% of Sunday volume). That mix change matters for **`@concepts/kalshi-retail-lp.md`**: parlay flow is retail and taker-heavy, which changes who is on the other side.
