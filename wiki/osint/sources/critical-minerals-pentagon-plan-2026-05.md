## Narrative

The week of the Trump-Xi summit (Beijing, mid-May 2026) put rare earths back at the center of US-China tension. Key threads:

- **China's grip is intact.** Exports of yttrium, dysprosium and terbium remain **down ~50%** versus the 12 months before China's export controls, per customs data. China still holds ~90% of global refining capacity. Pricing in April 2026 reflects a *managed* market, not free price discovery.
- **A rare-earths "deal" holds, fragilely.** A US-China rare-earths arrangement remains in effect with an extension expected to be announced around the summit — but heavy REEs (Dy/Tb) are still being used as leverage.
- **Pentagon supply plan.** The Pentagon is accelerating a domestic critical-minerals plan — reported as run by a group of former Wall Street figures — ahead of a **2027 US defense ban on Chinese-origin rare-earth materials**. Framed by trade press as a "less than 8 months" deadline to stand up alternative supply.
- **Africa front.** China continues expanding its grip on African lithium and rare-earth supply, widening the gap the US/EU are trying to close.

This is direct, multi-source corroboration of the wiki's `@concepts/critical-minerals-thesis.md` and `@concepts/rare-earth-supply-deficit-thesis.md`: the structural Dy/Tb supply deficit and China's chokehold are not easing, and the policy response (defense ban + Pentagon plan) sets a hard 2027 demand catalyst.
