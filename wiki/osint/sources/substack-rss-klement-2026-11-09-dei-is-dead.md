## Narrative

Klement argues that US and European companies have cut public diversity, equity, and inclusion communication since Donald Trump returned to office. His claim is not that the programmes stopped. His claim is that companies **stopped talking about them**.

The evidence is a study by Ana Isabel Lopes and Joao Goncalves (2026). They read the social media feeds of the **20 largest US companies and 20 largest European companies**, classified each post, and measured the share focused on DEI topics. They compare 2024 to 2025. The shared chart shows a large fall. The article does not give the numeric values in text. [Source: substack-rss-klement-2026-11-09-dei-is-dead.md]

The article's mechanism is **government-contract exposure**. Alphabet has 191,000 employees. A federal lawsuit or a lost contract could remove thousands of jobs. Klement's line is that executives are not freedom fighters and have a lot to lose. The Google Maps naming example is decorative support for the same point: a firm renames a map label inside one country to avoid political exposure.

### Why it matters here

This is a governance and political-risk signal, not a market signal. Two usable observations:

1. **Disclosure is a choice, not a measure.** A fall in DEI post share is consistent with both programme cuts and programme silence. The study cannot separate them.
2. **Contract exposure is a channel.** Where federal revenue is large, public political positions carry a cost that is measurable in lost revenue. That is a testable governance input.

The article is opinion-forward. The empirical base is one working paper with a 40-company sample. Treat the direction as `[TENTATIVE]`.

## Dead Ends

- **Reading post share as programme activity.** Silence and termination look the same in the data.
- **Extrapolating from 40 large firms.** The sample is the largest 20 US and 20 European firms, not the market.
