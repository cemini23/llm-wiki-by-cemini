## Narrative

The EU rolls out its **Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM)** in 2026. Klement's argument: it will **reorder trade flows**, because the **EU Emissions Trading Scheme (ETS, 2003)** already did.

### The finding (de Santis et al., 2026)

The obvious expectation is that an ETS makes imports from non-ETS countries cheaper — so imports from outside should rise. **The opposite happened.** **Imports from within the EU rose** as the ETS rolled out, even though the ETS put a price on carbon. The proposed mechanism: **greater regulatory alignment and less bureaucracy beat the cost savings** from China, India, or elsewhere.

**Exports did not change** — no cost penalty for European exporters.

**The control:** comparing ETS-affected sectors against unaffected ones shows the effect is **not** just general EU integration — affected sectors increased intra-EU imports **much more**.

### Why it matters here

A **trade-flows insight for the macro lane**: carbon-pricing regimes can **increase** intra-bloc trade through alignment effects, against the naive cost prediction. Klement also nudges the UK, which is weighing EU alignment. **Wiki-only; no scp.**
