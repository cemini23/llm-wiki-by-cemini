## Narrative

### The hearing

The House Subcommittee on Higher Education and Workforce Development held a hearing titled "The Rise of Sports Gambling on College Campuses", reported on 2026-11-12. Rep. Michael Baumgartner (R-WA) led the session. Prediction markets were not the topic, but four witnesses criticised them.

Witnesses: Dr. Lindsey Mortenson (University of Michigan), Dr. Daniel Durkin (University of Mississippi), Jordan Maynard (Chair, Massachusetts Gaming Commission), and Cindy Clouner (Ohio State).

### The witnesses on prediction markets

| Witness | Claim |
|---|---|
| Mortenson | Sports betting and prediction markets are "not investments": no productive asset, negative expected return, zero-sum |
| Durkin | Kalshi admits traders at 18 while regulated sportsbooks require 21; Fliff offers a sweepstakes product at 18 |
| Maynard | Prediction-market firms offer sports wagers "and call them something else" to avoid state laws, taxes and regulation |
| Clouner | Anti-gambling messaging fails because students do not classify app betting, DFS, prediction markets or gamblified games as gambling |

Maynard added two lines worth keeping: "The CFTC is not equipped to regulate sports wagering to our standards," and "illegality is not innovation."

### The numbers in the testimony

- NCAA 2023 survey: **67%** of on-campus students had bet on sports; **16%** showed problem-gambling behaviour; **6%** lost more than $500 in one day.
- Massachusetts blocked at least **11,000** accounts from people under 21 through age verification.
- Rutgers data: among men 21+ who gambled in the past year, **12.5%** used a prediction market against **28.8%** who bet on sports.
- Ohio: only **1 in 3** higher-education institutions implemented gambling prevention.

All witness figures are testimony, not audited data. [TENTATIVE]

### The Novig ruling

The briefing reports a New York decision that cuts against the federal-preemption position. Judge McMahon denied Novig's motion for a preliminary injunction.

Her reasoning, as quoted:

- The Commodity Exchange Act does not preempt state gambling regulation of a designated contract market.
- It was inappropriate to defer to the CFTC's view that Novig's contracts are swaps while traditional sports bets are not.
- "Novig's sports event contracts are sports betting."
- The CFTC's order to exchanges "smacks of a collusive effort between a regulator and regulatee to generate a claim for conflict preemption."

This is a district-court opinion. It binds nothing outside its district, but it gives state regulators a quotable precedent. See @concepts/prediction-market-regulation-2026.md.

### The rest of the briefing

- **CFTC investigation of Kalshi self-certification** (CNBC, Politico): the probe concerns weather-related contracts. The CFTC warned exchanges on 24 July against one broad certification covering many contracts. A June proposal would add a 90-day review window.
- **CFTC state-access order**: on 5 November the agency said federally regulated prediction markets generally cannot block traders by state, with 30 days to comply or explain. Deadline in early December.
- **Polymarket layoffs**: viral claims of 30% cuts are inaccurate, per Front Office Sports. The company cut some full-time staff and many contractors, mostly in marketing, ahead of a possible go-public deal as soon as 2027.
- **Kalshi × Artnet**: Artnet becomes Kalshi's official art partner and supplies settlement data for art markets.
- **Texas**: Rep. Salman Bhojani pre-filed a prediction-market bill that stops short of a sports-contract ban.
- **Single-stock perpetuals**: the SEC filed a brief supporting the CFTC against an injunction. The SEC must decide by 31 December whether to overturn the exchange rules.
- **tZERO**: the CFTC designated tZERO DCM on 10 November, after the DCO registration on 16 October.
