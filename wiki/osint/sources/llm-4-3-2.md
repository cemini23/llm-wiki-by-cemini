## Narrative

**Problem.** Tabular foundation models (TabPFN, TabICL — Prior-Data Fitted Networks pretrained on synthetic tabular data) generalize strongly across heterogeneous datasets, but explaining their predictions is slow. SHAP / KernelSHAP feature attribution is computationally expensive (combinatorial in the number of features), making it unusable for interactive scientific workflows where researchers need real-time feedback while exploring hypotheses.

**Method.** The paper introduces **ShapPFN**, a tabular foundation model that integrates Shapley-value regression directly into the architecture so prediction and explanation emerge from a single forward pass. Built on nanoTabPFN's transformer (alternating attention over features and over datapoints), it adds two decoder heads: a **BaseDecoder** producing a global baseline and a **ShapDecoder** producing per-feature, per-class contributions ϕ. Final logits are explicitly additive — `f(x) = base + Σ ϕ_f(x)` — giving a SHAP-like decomposition for free. Training extends the ViaSHAP loss: a Shapley-consistency MSE term (Shapley-kernel-weighted, comparing the learned additive estimates against Monte-Carlo coalition estimates over masked inputs) is added to standard cross-entropy. Trained on 256,000 TabICL-generated synthetic datasets for 8,000 steps.

**Key findings.** ShapPFN matches competitive predictive accuracy on standard benchmarks while producing high-fidelity explanations (R²=0.96, cosine similarity 0.99 vs KernelSHAP) over 1000× faster — 0.06 s versus 610 s for KernelSHAP. Code is open-sourced (github.com/kunumi/ShapPFN).

**Cemini relevance.** Cemini works extensively with tabular market data; ShapPFN shows that fast, in-architecture feature attribution is feasible for foundation-model predictors, offering a path to real-time, auditable explanations for any tabular model used in ticker research or strategy signals — turning explainability from a slow post-hoc step into a forward-pass byproduct.
