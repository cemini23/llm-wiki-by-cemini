## Narrative

A teaser for a three-part report on how to trade a volatility spike. The free text is the framing, not the trades.

### The framework stated

Part 1 is the macro environment and asks when selling stops. Part 2 is levels and trades. Part 3 is execution and sizing through the dislocation. [Source: substack-rss-capital-flows-2026-11-23-when-is-the-bottom-in-for-equities.md]

The author states that the credit cycle and the liquidity cycle drive about 80 percent of returns. The argument runs: credit from AI capex was injected in the first half of the year. In the second half, capex expectations peaked and dollar liquidity contracted on higher rates, so equities chopped in a range. The midterms and a shift in AI regulation then created a source of selling inside a fragile environment.

### The load-bearing claim

Named catalysts are the Democratic sweep and the AI-regulation shift, the Jensen episode at NVDA, neocloud credit risk as compute prices fall, and the Anthropic IPO. The author's point is that these are not the cause. In a robust credit cycle they would have been bought. In a fragile one they become excuses to price risk that already existed. [TENTATIVE: one author, paywalled material behind the claim]

That is a causal claim worth flagging rather than adopting. The testable part is the credit-and-liquidity state, not the catalyst list.

### What is usable

No levels, no numbers, no trade structure in the free text. The value is a clean statement of the ordering: macro regime first, levels second, execution third. The regime read is already recorded in `concepts/inflation-risk-regime-2026.md`.
