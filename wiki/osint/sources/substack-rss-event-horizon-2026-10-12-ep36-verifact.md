## Narrative

### The CFTC's two Friday moves (the hard news)

**1. Casino products are not swaps.** An **Interim Final Rule** codifies the CFTC's position that **casino-style gambling products — sportsbook wagers and casino games — are excluded from the "swap" definition.** Chairman Selig: "Casino-style gambling products are not derivatives." **Why it matters:** some argued that if sports event contracts are swaps, sports bets could be swaps too — which would drag state-regulated sportsbooks under CFTC jurisdiction. This rule blunts that.

**2. Event contracts ARE swaps.** A **Notice of Proposed Rulemaking** would expressly **include event contracts — sports, politics, culture, weather — in the definition of "swap."** Selig: these are "commodity derivatives squarely within the CFTC's regulatory remit … and within the agency's exclusive jurisdiction."

**Read together, the two are a preemption play for the courts:** carve out casinos, claim the event contracts. **Kalshi filed for a Sixth Circuit rehearing on Oct 9, the same day**, citing the new swap rules.

### Verifact — the episode's subject

**Verifact Markets: markets on disputed facts about the past** — "a new category of markets, focused on topics of public interest where people genuinely disagree about what is true." CEO **Rodrigo Aquino**: "Making or amplifying a claim has never been easier. Resolving one has never been harder." **The open question the format raises:** who decides truth, and what happens when evidence arrives after resolution. (The wiki's K283 note records Verifact's non-appeal design.)

### Product news

- **Kalshi "protected" NFL player markets.** An **early-exit payout**: if a player takes at least one snap in the first half but none in the second half/overtime and has not already hit the target, the market settles at the **pre-kickoff fair price**. Only for **Yes** picks, only before the game, and it **reduces the payout**. This is the sportsbook-style injury insurance arriving in combos.
- **Polymarket now offers parlays up to 50 legs.**

### Regulation roundup

**The SCOTUS briefs grew:** the **NFL**, **145 tribes and tribal organizations**, **39 states**, and **Dodd-Frank co-author Christopher Dodd** — plus former CFTC Chair **Gary Gensler** — all filed supporting New Jersey. Dodd and Gensler both argue **Congress did not make the CFTC a gambling regulator** through Dodd-Frank. **Seminoles sue DraftKings**; DeSantis sided with the Tribe. **Kalshi hired a former FBI agent** to run its first AML team. **Blockchain.com** filed for two CFTC licenses.

### Insider-trading item

Kalshi is **investigating suspicious trades** on Trump's press-secretary pick (Katie Zacharia was ~1% before the news). The author's counter to "the trades were small": they were a **large share of the open interest** right before the move, and **investigations are the right response**.
