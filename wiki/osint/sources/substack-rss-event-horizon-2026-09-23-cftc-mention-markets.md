## Narrative

The **CFTC** issued a **Staff Advisory on Individual Mention, Attendance and Interaction Event** contracts. The advisory does not ban any market type. It warns that mention contracts carry a "heightened risk of manipulation" and signals that prediction-market operators should be more selective about which contracts they list.

Key points from the advisory:

- Settlement of a mention market may be controlled by a single individual, a small group, or persons with access to or influence over the named individual. CFTC DMO staff may therefore view mention markets as **presumptively readily susceptible to manipulation**, and expect a **heightened showing** in support of any submission to list such contracts.
- The advisory outlines the limited circumstances in which such contracts may be listed consistently with the Commodity Exchange Act and Commission regulations, with non-exhaustive factors for DCMs under Regulations Sections 40.2 or 40.3.
- It reminds DCMs of their **Core Principle 3** obligation to list only contracts not readily susceptible to manipulation, and stresses complete, contract-specific analysis under Part 40.

The article notes a high-profile case of inside information used to trade prediction markets: a former teleprompter operator for President Donald Trump. It observes that the CFTC has been permissive with prediction markets overall, so an advisory likely carries weight. It also notes that mention markets can provide genuine signal for speeches and earnings calls, but that some have devolved into the absurd.
