## Narrative

The file lists this post as published on 2026-10-28. Wu Blockchain reports that on 22 October 2026 Chris Dixon renamed a16z Crypto to a16z Frontier. The new fund mandate covers frontier technology companies, and Dixon said the team will still invest in crypto and support sensible crypto regulation. He gave three reasons: industry boundaries are blurring as Wall Street and fintech ship blockchain products; AI is creating new devices, products, and networks; and a16z needs a home for projects that sit outside its sector teams. Ben Horowitz made the structure point on an a16z podcast the same day. On 5 May the same team raised $2.2 billion for its fifth fund, down from $4.5 billion for the fourth fund in 2022. Paul Cafiero told The Block that the fifth fund would back only crypto founders. About five and a half months later the mandate widened. Sector allocations are not public. Each figure in this page is a single-source claim from this newsletter.

The post says large firms can still raise while early-stage funding shrinks. Paradigm announced a $1.2 billion fourth fund and now lists crypto, AI, robotics, and other frontier technologies. Framework Ventures announced a $400 million fourth fund, FVIV, in June, adding AI, robotics, energy, and fintech. Haun Ventures announced $1 billion on 4 May for financial infrastructure, new assets and markets, and the agent economy. CryptoRank reported 127 crypto venture deals and about $2.62 billion in disclosed funding in Q3 2026, down 59.6% and 40.4% year over year. Seed and pre-seed rounds were 40 deals and about $255 million. Institutions in those deals fell from 767 to 333. Galaxy Research’s Q2 report said company funding rose about 31% quarter over quarter, with about 78% going to later-stage firms, and that new crypto venture funds raised about $3.9 billion from five funds. CoinGecko reported total crypto market capitalization up 39.6% quarter over quarter in Q3, while CryptoRank recorded a 41.9% drop in disclosed venture funding. The post says the two reports use different methods and quarters, and that LP fundraising is separate from startup fundraising.

Token exits are the next pressure point in the post. CoinGecko’s sample from 1 January through 22 December 2025 showed average returns of about -40.6% for Layer 2, -75.2% for gaming, and -76.7% for DePIN. The post says these figures measure token prices. A May 2024 Binance Research report found circulating supply in a set of new tokens as low as about 6%, with none above 20%, and linked high fully diluted valuations and later unlocks to selling pressure. CoinGecko put average daily memecoin volume at about $1.1 billion in 2023 and $9.7 billion in 2024, with market capitalization about $150.6 billion in December 2024 and about $47.2 billion by November 2025. Galaxy Research’s Q2 2026 report said spot ETFs and digital asset treasury companies compete with crypto VC for institutional capital. In CryptoRank’s Q3 sample, disclosed IPO and post-IPO financing was about $3.63 billion, and 99.3% of that came from Strategy’s treasury financing.

CryptoRank said payments, exchanges, and brokerage businesses took 71.6% of disclosed venture funding in Q3 2026. On 24 September 2026 the Federal Reserve proposed two stablecoin rules on reserves, capital, risk management, and bank-subsidiary issuance. On 18 August the SEC proposed Regulation Crypto Assets, with a registration exemption and a conditional safe harbor. The post says both items remain proposals. Katie Haun argued that agents will need payments, trading, subscriptions, identity, privacy, verification, credit, and insurance, and that the shift will take time. For a quant-finance and macro reader, the post describes a split: listed crypto exposure through ETFs and treasury stocks sits next to a thinner early-stage venture pipe, and the remaining venture dollars cluster in payments and financial services. The post says long-term LP capital still depends on revenue, users after subsidies end, and realized distributions.
