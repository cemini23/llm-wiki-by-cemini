Commissioned after the rearmament defense report, as the next-week follow-on. The operator wanted a long thesis on Swarmer (NASDAQ: SWMR), the Erik Prince drone-swarm company, with the Prince roll-up as the main angle and the Ukraine combat-proven software as the substance.

## Narrative

Swarmer is a Ukrainian-founded, Austin-headquartered software company that sells a drone-swarm autonomy layer used in Ukrainian combat since 2024. Erik Prince is non-executive chairman and holds no stock, only unvested performance options. The thesis is that he consolidates Ukraine's combat-proven drone and robot makers into a US-listed platform.

The note is written long, with the risks up front. The lead risk is a revenue round-trip: Swarmer pays the same counterparty group $4.9M against $3.9M of consideration, and $2.4M of services could not be substantiated at fair value, so it reduces revenue. Other risks include 99 percent customer concentration, heavy dilution, the company abandoning its vendor-agnostic model, the stronger competitor Auterion, and Prince owning no shares.

The first acquisition, Ratel Robotics, has audited FY2025 net sales of $18.6M and $86.6M of customer advances, so the FY2026 earnout is a delivery question. The trade is long, equity only, since there are no listed options.

Research relied on SEC filings (10-Q, DEFM14A, POS AM, PREM14A, 8-K), the WSJ via Ukrainska Pravda, Defense One, Auterion, and New York Magazine. A Grok bot dossier and two rounds of verification corrected several claims: Prince holds zero shares (the 1,774,725 figure is an option), and the Ratel dilution split is 91.6 / 8.4 at closing, not 74.9 / 25.1.
