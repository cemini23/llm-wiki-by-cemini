## Narrative

**Problem / scope.** A short reference entry surveying AI ethics for political-communication scholars. It is not original research — a concise, well-sourced introduction to the field — hence `maturity: draft`.

**Content.** It frames AI ethics as an interdisciplinary domain spanning philosophy of mind and the practical impact of AI on human values (autonomy, fairness, equal opportunity). It groups ethical concerns into **pervasive risks** — accountability gaps from automated decision-making, model opacity, and bias/discrimination inherited from training data — and **domain-specific risks**, notably deepfakes and generative-AI-driven misinformation that erode trust in public discourse. The entry then traces the rise of **Trustworthy AI** and **Responsible AI** as policy responses, anchored in the EU High-Level Expert Group's "Ethics Guidelines for Trustworthy AI" and the hundreds of similar principle documents (transparency, explainability, fairness, accountability, privacy).

**Key findings / critique.** It foregrounds the **principle–implementation gap**: critics (Mittelstadt 2019, Hagendorff 2020, Héder 2020) argue high-level principles cannot guarantee ethical AI — they lack translation methods, force lossy simplification of multidimensional ethical concepts, and are mostly not AI-specific. More radical critics (Munn 2023) hold the gap is inherent to mainstream AI ethics. The entry closes by framing AI regulation/standardization as a continuation of the ethics discourse.

**Cemini relevance.** Governance backdrop for institutional-acquirer due diligence: if CeminiSuite or its IP is positioned for acquisition, an acquirer's compliance review will test the trading stack against exactly these Trustworthy/Responsible-AI principles (transparency, accountability, bias), so the vocabulary and critique here inform IP-sale-readiness framing.
