## Narrative

A six-page practitioner essay arguing that periodic model retraining is a lossy approximation of Bayesian inference. In a proper Bayesian pipeline the model maintains a posterior over parameters and updates continuously as data arrive; retraining from scratch (or fine-tuning on recent data) discards the prior's accumulated information and incurs what Katz calls "learning debt" — the gap between what the model knows and what a correctly-updated Bayesian model would know. The essay makes the case for streaming/online Bayesian updates over discrete retrains, particularly for time-series forecasting in non-stationary environments.

This is an **opinion piece, not research**. No empirical content, no benchmarks. Its value is framing: the "retraining as approximate Bayesian inference" framing is pedagogically clean and useful when explaining why a quant system needs to budget for staleness between retrains. Katz has prior Bayesian forecasting publications, so the framing has a credible author, but this specific artifact is short-form editorial.

For Cemini: useful mental model when discussing retraining cadence trade-offs with non-ML stakeholders. Do not cite as technical evidence.
