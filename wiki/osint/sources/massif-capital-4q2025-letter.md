### "Closely Held Assumptions" — the central thesis

Letter's headline argument: the **post-GFC regime** (2009–2021) rewarded duration as hedge, elevated terminal value in DCFs, treated leverage as return enhancer. These were rational responses to **persistent monetary accommodation + implicit volatility backstop**. They are now inputs into a different set of constraints.

The new regime is characterized by:
- Inflation persistence (labor / energy / geopolitical fragmentation)
- **Higher real-rate regime that appears durable rather than transitory** — alters arithmetic of risk and return
- Policy intervention is weaker: inflation constrains monetary easing, fiscal is politicized, industrial policy channels capital selectively
- **Tail risk less likely to be socialized; volatility may persist rather than mean-revert**

Historical parallels: 1968–1974 and 1999–2003 — losses came not from sudden shocks but from **delayed recognition that widely shared assumptions had expired**.

### Sector-level assumption-decay

- **Materials**: Chinese demand no longer normalizes cyclically (policy-managed). Supply responses constrained by politics/permitting/capital discipline. Regional premiums becoming common.
- **Energy**: prevailing narrative assumes demand destruction + rapid substitution + elastic reinvestment. Reality: **supply governed by decline rates, not responsiveness**; transition is **addition, not transition**.
- **Heavy industry**: not purely cyclical — policy creating regional cost asymmetries, capital intensity rising, oligopolies forming
- **Infrastructure**: not a bond proxy — inflation resets values unevenly, regulation strategic-not-technocratic

### Wicked problems framework

> "Wicked problems are not difficult puzzles awaiting better tools or more data; they are problems whose parameters shift in response to attempted solutions. Each intervention alters the system itself, rendering prior answers obsolete." [Source: Massif+Capital+4Q2025+Letter+to+Investors.pdf p.2]

Used to argue that modern macro/financial systems increasingly exhibit wicked characteristics: efforts to stabilize inflation destabilize growth; industrial policy intended to secure supply chains distorts capital allocation; financial repression preserves near-term solvency at expense of long-term returns.
