## Narrative

A short macro note on a **BIS study** (Johns et al., 2026).

### The mechanism

A rate hike raises financing costs for households and firms — **and for the government, the biggest borrower of all.** In a high-debt economy, a hike forces a choice: let the **deficit rise** (and investors price **higher future inflation** to inflate the debt away), or **cut spending** (and investors expect **lower growth**). Either way, the same rate hike lands differently depending on the debt level.

### The result

The BIS charts the response of European countries to an **ECB monetary-policy shock**, stylised at **60%** and **120%** debt-to-GDP. The high-debt response differs from the low-debt one.

### Why it matters here

A macro-regime point for the trading lanes: **the effect of a rate move is state-dependent on the debt level** — a fiscal-dominance channel. It is the kind of regime term a macro-weather read should carry. **Wiki-only; no scp.**
