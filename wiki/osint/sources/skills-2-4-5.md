## Narrative

A UK Government rapid evidence review of the literature on **AI skills for life and work**, addressing three questions: what AI-relevant skills are needed, the extent to which the UK labour force has or lacks them, and what the UK can learn from international counterparts. It distinguishes *digital literacy* (a prerequisite) from *AI literacy/competence/skills*, and reviews how AI-skills frameworks have been translated into educational curricula in the UK and abroad.

Key findings: a significant share of the UK population holds only partial Essential Digital Skills (EDS), with attainment skewed by gender, age, education, income, and region — gaps that risk reinforcing existing regional economic inequalities as business AI adoption rises. UK policy has so far concentrated on the *supply* side via tertiary education; the review argues for AI-skills curricula at primary and secondary levels and warns that a STEM-subject take-up push alone cannot meet demand — the *underpinning* level of STEM skills across all subjects needs raising. Significant supply/demand AI-skills gaps have persisted for years and are expected to widen sharply over the next five years. With the average skills lifespan now under three years, the review puts growing responsibility on employers to build a continuous learning culture, and flags high future demand for skills in *strategic, ethics-compliant* business deployment of AI.

**Cemini relevance**: backdrop material for the wiki's `ai-ethics` governance posture — it documents the workforce AI-literacy gap and the rising premium on ethics-/regulation-compliant AI deployment skills, context (not a direct input) for how Cemini frames responsible operation of its own AI stack.
