## Narrative

The paper argues that urban screens in China did not follow the same development path as urban screens elsewhere. It names the Chinese path "Chineseness" and defines the term carefully. Chineseness is not a cultural essence. It is a relational, historically situated configuration of three linked elements.

The three elements are:

1. **Deep-rooted lineages of public communication.** The tradition of public messaging predates the electronic screen.
2. **State-steered technological leapfrogging.** Industrial policy and mega-events drive the pace of adoption.
3. **A regulatory paradox.** Regulation permits spectacular scale while it tightens operational control.

The evidence is a five-stage genealogy running from 1990 to the present, a chronology of representative screens, and five paradigmatic cases.

Three effects follow from the configuration. First, temporal and spatial compression: several technology generations arrive close together in time and space. Second, coexistence of multiple technological generations on the same street. Third, a preference for collective witnessing over device-centric interactivity.

The paper positions Chineseness as a globally relevant alternative to market-led models of media architecture, not as an exception to them.

The analytic value for open-source research is the three-element frame. It separates policy, industrial capacity, and regulation as distinct drivers. It also gives named tools, "temporal–spatial compression" and "collective witnessing", for reading non-Western media ecologies. The paper is a humanities argument with no quantitative estimates. Treat the claims as an interpretive framework, not as measured findings.
