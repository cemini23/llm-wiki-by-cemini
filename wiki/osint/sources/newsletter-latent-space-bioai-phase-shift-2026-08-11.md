## Narrative

Latent Space podcast with Chai Discovery cofounder Matt McPartlon and product lead Neil Patil. Chai Discovery is OpenAI-backed, valued at ~$4B, only 2 years old; at the January JPM pharma conference four big AI×Pharma tools deals were announced.

Core thesis: AI-for-pharma companies historically had to build their own drug pipelines because "convincing pharma to use your tool requires proof your tool works." That changed in January when **tools became good enough for drug design teams to trust** — structural models became **binding models** (binding-affinity prediction). Binding models unlock design, and good design tools enable things lab-based discovery can't do (e.g. bi-specific antibodies).

The step change is **not an efficiency argument**: "you're enabling things you just plain couldn't do." Chai's angle is getting good molecules right out of the gate (less lab iteration), which "turns science into engineering" and enables one-shotting molecules toward the clinic. For engineering problems, the best product tends to win; good tech is necessary but not sufficient.
