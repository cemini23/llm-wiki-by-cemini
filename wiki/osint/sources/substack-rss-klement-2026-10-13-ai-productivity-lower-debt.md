## Narrative

Klement relays a KfW study by **Stephan Bales** on how **AI productivity gains feed through to US government debt**. The mechanism is mechanical: **productivity → GDP growth → higher tax revenue + lower welfare spending → smaller primary deficit → slower debt growth**. In the best case, **debt-to-GDP falls**.

**Bales' arithmetic (the numbers to keep):**

| AI productivity boost | US primary deficit, 2056 | US debt-to-GDP, 2056 |
|-----------------------|--------------------------|----------------------|
| 0.0% (baseline) | 2.8% | 185% |
| 0.2% per year | 1.5% | 143% |
| 0.4% per year | ~0 (eliminated) | 113% |

**Klement's own view:** he sides with **Acemoglu and the CBO** — about **0.1% per year** of productivity gain. He notes the **timing** point from Bales: past general-purpose technologies (steam, electricity, the computer) had **years to decades of hype with near-zero measured productivity growth** before a killer application arrived.

### Why it matters here
The trade-relevant claim is Klement's: **buying 30-year US Treasuries today is an implicit large long bet on AI-driven productivity and GDP growth.** If AI productivity disappoints, **yields drift higher** on the rising debt burden. This is a **falsifiable macro stance** with a dated, numeric distribution — a candidate **GuruWatcher macro watch** (alert-only; **no order, no auto-arm**).
