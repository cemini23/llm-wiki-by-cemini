## Narrative

### The observation

The author was driving, interrupted a podcast, launched a voice assistant's voice mode, and asked it about TLT volatility — a market question. The bot is connected to `moontower.ai` data **via MCP**, plus the internet and its own training.

He reached the destination and **never switched back to the podcast**.

### The thesis

Podcasts have an infovore following. The question he poses: **can anything compete with an infovore discussing what they care about, asking their own questions, steering their own learning?**

His framing: "conversational AI custom media eats traditional media."

### The economics, which is the real argument

Publishers sell page views, minutes, and subscriptions — **quantities produced once and delivered at zero marginal cost**. Custom conversational media "runs on tokens and compute" instead.

That inverts the cost structure of media. Mass media got cheap by amortising one artefact across an audience. Personalised media does the opposite: every consumption event costs something.

### The "hollowing of the middle"

Attention is zero-sum, bounded by hours. If media becomes individualised, more properties get sucked into "the middle" — and the middle is where the economics fail. His example is Vice: it outgrew an edgy audience without reaching the scale its fixed costs needed.

The prediction follows: **own a hyper-niche or broadcast the Super Bowl; do not sit between.**

### The counterargument he concedes

Most people are consumers and lurkers. They want to scroll and channel-surf. They do not want to steer, if entertainment is escape rather than outlet.

His answer is honest rather than dismissive: that is valid, and it is also consensus. His bet is on friction removal — "weird things happen when we remove friction" — with the DoorDash analogy.

### The part worth keeping

He draws on C. Thi Nguyen's *Games: Agency as Art*: games are "the art form whose medium is agency itself". The designer shapes what you can do; the experience depends on what you bring.

That is the right frame for why this feels different from a better search box. A podcast gives you a fixed path; a conversational agent gives you a medium.

**Caveat.** An essay with one anecdote and no data. The author says plainly he is "not planting any new flags".
