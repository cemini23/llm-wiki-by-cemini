## Narrative

LDP (LLM Delegate Protocol) argues that the protocols connecting multi-agent LLM systems increasingly constrain them. Current agent-to-agent protocols — Google's A2A and Anthropic's MCP — deliberately omit model-level metadata: agents are identified by names, descriptions, and skill lists, but properties fundamental to good delegation (model family, parameter count, reasoning profile, quality calibration, context window, cost characteristics) sit outside the protocol surface. A router choosing between a 3B classifier and an 8B reasoner that both advertise "reasoning" cannot assess quality–cost tradeoffs.

LDP makes these properties first-class via five mechanisms: (1) rich delegate identity cards carrying model family, quality hints, and reasoning profiles; (2) progressive payload modes with automatic negotiation and fallback (verbose natural language vs. compact semantic frames); (3) governed sessions for multi-round delegation with persistent context; (4) structured provenance tracking confidence and verification status; and (5) trust domains enforcing security boundaries at the protocol level. It is implemented as a plugin adapter for the JAMJET runtime, registering alongside A2A/MCP adapters with zero host modifications.

The empirical study (local Ollama delegates, Gemini LLM-as-judge, vs. A2A and random baselines) is mixed but informative. Identity-aware routing achieves ~12× lower latency on easy tasks via specialization-aware selection but does **not** improve aggregate quality over skill-matching in a small 3-delegate pool (A2A even edges it 7.43 vs 6.80 overall, not significant). Semantic-frame payloads cut token count 37% (p=0.031) with no quality loss. Governed sessions eliminate 39% of token overhead at 10 conversation rounds. Most notably, **noisy provenance is harmful**: unverified confidence metadata degrades synthesis quality below the no-provenance baseline. Simulated protocol analyses show trust domains detecting 96% of unauthorized delegations (vs 6% for A2A bearer tokens) and fallback chains reaching 100% task completion (vs 35%) — but these are design-property simulations, not empirical attack scenarios. The author flags that routing advantages should grow with larger heterogeneous delegate pools.

**Cemini relevance**: LDP speaks directly to the conductor's routing problem — choosing which model/agent to delegate a research subtask to. Its identity-card and quality-aware routing model, payload negotiation, and the cautionary finding that confidence metadata needs verification all bear on how the Cemini conductor should expose and trust agent capabilities.
