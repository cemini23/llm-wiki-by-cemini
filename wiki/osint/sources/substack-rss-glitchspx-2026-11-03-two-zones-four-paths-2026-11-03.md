## Narrative

### The move

VIX went **17.20 on Wednesday → 21.15 on Monday**. SPX closed at **7,541**, which is **3.55% below** its October 6 high of **7,819**.

### The curve

The front has flipped: **9-day VIX at 22.46 is above regular VIX**. Regular VIX against 3-month VIX (21.60) is **0.98** — still upward-sloping, but higher than on **89% of trading days since 2009**. A full flip above 1 has happened on about **7.5% of days**.

Some of that is election-week protection.

### The statistic that reframes the signal

This is the part worth keeping. A **full curve flip has historically meant bigger swings in both directions**, not a reliable signal of more downside.

> Twenty sessions after past flips began, SPX was **up a median 2.6%** (67 episodes since 2009), but with a **2-sigma day** along the way in about **7 of 10 cases**.

So the flip predicts **volatility, not direction** — and specifically predicts that something violent happens in either direction. Reading it as bearish is the common error.

### Dealer exposure levels

The map shows where dealer exposure concentrates across SPX and SPXW, cross-checked against the full options chain as of the November 2 close.

- **8,000** — heavy positioning on both sides, **balanced**, so less likely to act as a wall. Just above it, a large block of year-end call positioning spread across **7,980–8,210**.

The balance at 8,000 is the notable detail: a level with equal exposure on both sides does not pin price the way a one-sided wall does.

**Caveat.** The exposure levels come from a proprietary dealer-positioning map (Skylit Heatseeker), cross-checked against the chain. Dealer-gamma mapping is a model of positioning, not an observation of it.
