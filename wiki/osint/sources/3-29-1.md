## Narrative

Single-author paper proposing a compute-modulation mechanism for asynchronous MARL. Core claim per the abstract fragment: standard MARL forces every agent to run full neural-net inference at every environment frame, which wastes compute when immediate action is not needed. The "dual-gated epistemic time-dilation" framing suggests two conditions (gates) that together permit an agent to skip or slow inference based on its own confidence/uncertainty state — "time-dilation" being the metaphor for variable inference cadence per agent.

Honest assessment: single-author, no clear venue signal in the metadata captured. The framing is conceptually interesting for constrained-compute deployments (edge, embedded, or cost-sensitive production inference) but the abstract alone does not reveal baseline comparisons or empirical scale. Treat as an idea-stage paper pending a deeper read.

Relevance: for infrastructure, the idea of per-agent asynchronous inference cadence maps directly to cost optimization in LLM-agent trading systems (why call a large model every tick?). For rl-marl, it sits in the async-MARL lineage. Market-microstructure tag is a stretch from the abstract but plausible if the paper uses trading as a case study (not confirmed).

## Key findings

- Targets the waste-of-compute problem in synchronous MARL where every agent infers every frame.
- Introduces a "dual-gated" decision rule for whether to infer now or defer — "epistemic" suggests uncertainty-triggered.
- 14-page scope; single-author; no visible venue in the metadata captured.
- Direct analog for Cemini: inference-cadence gating for LLM-based trading agents could materially reduce per-decision cost.
