## Narrative

A multi-institution position paper (grown out of a Spring 2025 CASBS workshop) proposing **"AI Behavioral Science"** as a new discipline at the intersection of artificial intelligence and behavioral/social science. The thesis: because AI training processes and code are complex, often proprietary, and "black-box," AI systems should be assessed the way behavioral scientists assess humans — by *observed behavior*, inferring implicit objectives, biases, and tendencies rather than reading internals.

The paper organizes the field around three mutually reinforcing pillars: (1) **assessing and guiding AI behavior** — applying decades-old behavioral-science diagnostic tools (Turing-style tests, game-theoretic probes) to measure and predict what an AI agent is implicitly optimizing for, especially as models from different developers carry different incentives; (2) **using AI to advance behavioral science** — simulating individuals, groups, and entire social systems cheaply and at new scale; and (3) **understanding AI–human interaction** — how AI reshapes belief formation, knowledge, and behavior as it mediates platforms, judges, doctors, and children's learning, with particular concern that for-profit designers internalize neither information externalities nor public-goods effects. It catalogs AI's enhanced capabilities (prediction, power, concentration, large-scale data digestion, coordination, designability, self-guidability) and frames AI's labor-market impact through the economics of complements vs. substitutes and "creative destruction."

**Cemini relevance**: this paper is the conceptual backbone for the wiki's `ai-ethics` cluster — its behavior-not-internals assessment framing directly informs how Cemini should evaluate and govern its own autonomous trading agents, where the model is a proprietary black box accessed via API.
