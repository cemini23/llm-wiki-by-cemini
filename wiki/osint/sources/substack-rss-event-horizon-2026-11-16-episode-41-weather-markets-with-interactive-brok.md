## Narrative

The lead story is a state-federal collision. A Louisiana state court issued an industry-wide temporary restraining order barring sports, election, and award contracts, naming Kalshi, Polymarket US, Robinhood, Crypto.com, Coinbase, Underdog, Novig, ProphetX, ForecastEx, and Gemini. The CFTC then sued Louisiana, calling the order "naked lawfare" and noting Louisiana is the twelfth state it has sued to defend exclusive jurisdiction.

Newsletter commentary adds the pattern behind the state strategy: a cease-and-desist that nobody obeys, an industry suit against the state, and therefore a shift to court orders that bind immediately. Arkansas is the eleventh state sued by the CFTC.

Volume, Saturday 14 November, from TickerTracker: Kalshi $5.32bn with parlays at 73% and sports 16.4%. Seven-day trailing: Kalshi $27.7bn, Polymarket US $3.73bn, DraftKings DKeX $1.46bn, Underdog $902.1m, Crypto.com/NADEX $857.8m, Novig $851.9m, Rothera $423.1m, ProphetX $51m.

Other items recorded: Kalshi reported in talks at a $65bn valuation; Meta testing an internal prediction app ("Takes") that uses non-cash "cred"; a House Oversight staff memorandum on insider trading by officials and clearance holders; Senator Cruz preparing legislation to require state or tribal approval for sports contracts; Underdog partnering with Plaee to white-label its exchange; and a CFTC advisory against broad template-style self-certifications, of which Kalshi's "EVENTCONFIRMED" template is an example used for the FAA ground-stop markets.
