## Narrative

Head-to-head empirical + theoretical comparison of **Evolution Strategies (ES) vs Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)** for LLM post-training, across 4 tasks in both single-task and sequential continual-learning settings. ES has recently been promoted as a scalable *gradient-free* alternative to gradient-based RL fine-tuning (Qiu et al. 2025; Korotyshova et al. 2025; Sarkar et al. 2025), with claimed advantages in memory (no activations to store) and parallelism (embarrassingly parallel perturbations).

**Core empirical finding.** ES matches or exceeds GRPO in single-task accuracy, and remains competitive in sequential continual-learning when iteration budget is controlled. But despite equivalent downstream accuracy, the two methods produce **geometrically very different** solutions:
- **GRPO** — narrow, high-gradient direction; precise, targeted, small-norm updates; stays close to the base model in parameter space.
- **ES** — broad, low-curvature plateau; diffuse updates that travel orders of magnitude further from initialization; much larger off-task KL drift.
- **Orthogonal update directions** but **NO loss barrier between the two solutions** — they are linearly connected in the loss landscape.
- Probing loss along each method's learned direction on held-out tasks: ES direction behaves much like a *random* direction, indicating substantial displacement in task-irrelevant dimensions.

**Theoretical contribution.** Derives an analytical decomposition of ES weight updates into:
- On-manifold component (loss-changing, task-aligned).
- **Off-manifold component (loss-invariant, random-walk)** with characteristic scaling: ||Δw_off||² ∝ α²dT/N where α = noise scale, d = parameter dim, T = steps, N = population size. In high-d loss landscapes with many flat directions, the off-manifold random walk dominates the total displacement while the on-manifold component still matches task progress → "accumulate large off-task movement while still reaching same accuracy."

**Practical implications.** (1) Two methods with *equivalent test accuracy* on the trained task can have *very different* forgetting profiles on held-out tasks — GRPO's localized updates preserve other capabilities better; ES's diffuse drift corrupts them. (2) Chose-your-update-geometry: use ES for task-accuracy-maximization when other capabilities don't matter; use GRPO (or small-learning-rate ES) for continual learning where preservation is critical. (3) Population size N is a direct knob on off-manifold random walk — larger N → less drift. Code: github.com/Bhoy1/ESvsGRPO.

Relevant to Cemini as the most rigorous current analysis of the gradient-free-vs-gradient-based training trade-off. Directly applicable because: (a) `captum-re-4-3-2.md` (SHARPEN) proposes CMA-ES (an ES variant) for neural repair — this paper suggests that ES-based approaches risk large off-task drift unless population size is large, which is a concrete risk for Cemini's MAPPO policy; (b) Cemini's catastrophic-forgetting concern in `rl-technical-audit-findings-3-9.md` (Gap 2 CRITICAL) is *exactly* the off-manifold-drift mechanism this paper formalizes — the MARS frozen-ensemble solution is motivated by the same underlying concern. Honest limits: (1) 4 tasks, one model family (presumably Llama-class — not verified in read); (2) continual-learning sequential setup is simple (no replay, no rehearsal); (3) theoretical decomposition assumes a specific noise model; (4) paper concerns LLM post-training, not RL from scratch — direct transfer to MAPPO-from-scratch on 28-feature obs is a generalization leap; (5) no prescription for the crossover point (when to prefer ES over GRPO) in terms of d/T/N — only the scaling law.

## Key findings

- ES matches or exceeds GRPO in single-task accuracy; stays competitive in sequential continual-learning with controlled iteration budget.
- **GRPO: narrow high-gradient, small-norm, localized updates; stays near base model.**
- **ES: broad low-curvature plateau, diffuse large-norm updates; orders-of-magnitude further from init; much bigger off-task KL drift.**
- Despite orthogonal update directions, the two solutions are linearly connected with NO loss barrier between them.
- ES direction on held-out tasks ≈ random direction — substantial displacement lies in task-irrelevant dimensions.
- ES update decomposition: on-manifold (loss-changing) + off-manifold (loss-invariant random walk).
- Scaling: ||Δw_off||² ∝ α²dT/N — larger population N shrinks off-manifold drift.
- Practical: for continual learning / capability preservation, prefer GRPO (or large-N ES); for single-task max accuracy, ES is OK.
- Directly informs Cemini's catastrophic-forgetting framing (`rl-technical-audit-findings-3-9.md` Gap 2) and the CMA-ES prescription in SHARPEN (`captum-re-4-3-2.md`).
- Code: github.com/Bhoy1/ESvsGRPO.
