## Narrative

The post is a short essay with no data and no tradeable claim. It argues that gambling products exploit a useful impulse rather than create a new one.

The argument starts from a Jared Dillian essay built on a Norm MacDonald quote. MacDonald was a heavy gambler. His therapist said he gambled to escape reality. MacDonald answered: "That's why everyone does everything."

The post then quotes MacDonald's 2016 memoir on why the bettor returns. Winning brings relief, and relief is not worth a life. The state worth returning to is the interval after the bet is placed and before it resolves. The post names that state as hope.

The author's use of it: risk is adaptive because it precedes growth. Risk allows failure, failure produces learning, and learning changes behaviour. Gambling is a loop where the lesson does not compound. The post calls it the insanity of repeating the same act and expecting a different result.

The post then makes the claim that matters for prediction markets. It says gambling is increasingly marketed as something more noble, and singles out prediction as the word doing that work. Its objection is not to gambling but to the reframing. Its closing analogy compares the added vitamins in cigarettes to the intellectual framing added to betting.

The post offers no numbers, no product, and no recommendation.
