## Narrative

Klement compares European and US start-up funding. The gap is not in early-stage money. It is at the top.

### The round-size gap

The largest European VC round ever is **Mistral's Series D at $3.5bn**. The five largest US rounds are:

| Company | Round | Date | Size |
|---|---|---|---|
| OpenAI | — | March 2026 | $122bn |
| Anthropic | — | May 2026 | $65bn |
| OpenAI | — | March 2025 | $40bn |
| Anthropic | — | early 2026 | $30bn |
| xAI | — | Q1 2026 | $20bn |

Mistral's $3.5bn round ranks **19th** on the combined list. [Source: substack-rss-klement-2026-11-10-the-start-up-challenge-in-europe.md]

### The OECD analysis

The OECD studied EU and US start-ups founded between **2000 and 2025** and measured which companies scaled. It splits them into two groups:

- **rising superstars** — eventual valuation above **$11bn**;
- **growth-oriented companies** — raised at least **$50m** in a lead round.

Two findings:

1. **Time to scale.** About **40%** of EU start-ups needed more than seven years from first patent filing to the funding level needed to scale. On average, EU start-ups took **1.5 years longer** than US start-ups. [Source: substack-rss-klement-2026-11-10-the-start-up-challenge-in-europe.md]
2. **The top end diverges.** Funding for ordinary growth-oriented firms rises at roughly the same rate in both regions. Rising superstars and future unicorns diverge. Five or six years after launch, US rising superstars attract much more capital than EU peers.

Klement says a follow-up post will cover a second study with another blocking factor.

### Why it matters here

The finding is shaped like a **distribution tail**, not a mean shift. A policy that lifts average funding helps the median firm and does nothing for the tail. That is a useful frame when a claim says "Europe underfunds start-ups": the right question is which percentile.

The round-size table is also a clean measure of how far the AI capex cycle has moved the top of the US funding distribution. Two companies raised $187bn across three rounds in about 15 months.

## Dead Ends

- **Reading the gap as an average gap.** The two distributions match for ordinary growth firms and diverge at the tail.
- **Comparing round sizes across years without a deflator.** The table mixes 2025 and 2026 rounds. The ranking is still valid; the levels are not directly comparable over long periods.
