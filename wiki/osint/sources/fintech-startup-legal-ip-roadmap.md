K7 is the **most operationally granular** of the three corporate-structure audits — it provides a phase-by-phase sequenced execution roadmap that K6 (3-10) sketches more abstractly and K5 (3-9) only references obliquely (in the v11.0 project-state context).

### Section 1: Entity Selection — LLC over C-Corp, Florida over Delaware

K7 paras 6–11 lay out the LLC-vs-C-Corp dichotomy and resolves it in favor of single-member Florida LLC for a solo-operator proprietary trading firm:

- **C-Corp catastrophic for solo prop trader**: 21% federal corporate tax + 20% qualified dividend tax (+ 3.8% NIIT) on distributed profits = "double taxation paradigm" (para 7) erodes algorithmic alpha. Punitive 20% Accumulated Earnings Tax (AET) if the firm tries to retain profits indefinitely instead of distributing (para 7).
- **LLC pass-through is structurally correct**: single-member LLC is disregarded for federal tax (para 8); operating losses during R&D phase offset other personal income same-year (para 8); C-Corp would force loss-carryforward, delaying fiscal benefit.
- **Florida over Delaware**: Florida statute explicitly protects single-member LLCs from personal creditors (para 11), comparable to Delaware. Florida has no franchise tax + no personal state income tax. A Florida domestic LLC for a Florida founder minimizes duplicate annual reporting + eliminates foreign qualification fees (para 11).

This is **convergent with @sources/3-10-algorithmic-trading-ip-sale-audit.md §2(a)** which makes the same Florida-over-Delaware case (and refines it to Wyoming for the HoldCo specifically); K7 doesn't yet introduce the Wyoming HoldCo distinction in this section — that arrives in Section 3.

### Section 2: Tax Optimization — §475(f) MTM endorsed; §174A R&E expensing restored 2025

K7 paras 13–19 cover three tax instruments:

- **§475(f) Mark-to-Market election** (paras 13–15): K7 **endorses** MTM on the same reasoning as K5 (3-9-pm-research para 124) — exempts the trading activity from the $3,000 capital-loss limit, removes §1091 wash-sale accounting (catastrophic for high-frequency intraday strategies), converts gains/losses to ordinary business income. The MTM election deadline is the unextended due date of the *prior* year's tax return — must be filed proactively at entity formation. K7 + K5 endorsement vs K6's rejection makes the §475(f) split **2-vs-1 in favor of endorsement**; the K6 rejection is now outvoted, though the K6 reasoning (don't flip an irrevocable election with IP-sale ordinary-vs-capital-gains object-of-the-tax conflicts) remains a valid object-specific consideration. See @concepts/ip-sale-corporate-structure.md §475(f) discussion for the reconciled framing.
- **§174 R&E expensing** (paras 17–18): NEW topic not covered in K5 / K6. TCJA required all domestic software-development costs to be capitalized + amortized over 5 years for tax years 2022–2024 — devastating cash-flow impact for early-stage tech firms. **2025 legislation restored full domestic expensing under §174A**, with retroactive amendment + acceleration provisions for taxpayers with average annual gross receipts under **$31 million**. Cemini sits comfortably under that threshold. See dedicated @concepts/section-174-rd-expensing.md.
- **Florida R&D Tax Credit (Statute 220.196)** (para 19): 10% credit on qualified research expenses exceeding a base amount. **Restricted exclusively to C-Corporations** in targeted industries (incl. Information Technology). For a single-member LLC, claiming this credit requires Form 8832 election to C-Corp tax status, re-introducing the double-taxation problem from Section 1. K7 explicitly recommends *against* this trade-off ("the federal pass-through benefits and MTM trading efficiencies of an LLC vastly outweigh the restrictive state-level R&D credits").

### Section 3: IP Protection — Trade Secrets over Patents; Dual-Entity HoldCo/OpCo

K7 paras 22–30 lay out the trade-secret-vs-patent doctrine and the dual-entity prescription:

- **Trade secrets over patents for AI** (paras 23–25): Patent registration mandates exhaustive public disclosure of mechanics. Post-*Alice Corp. v. CLS Bank Int'l*, mathematical algorithms + abstract software concepts face severe patentability challenges. USPTO guidelines emphasize that AI-generated inventions without significant human ingenuity may not qualify at all. Trade secret law (federal: **Defend Trade Secrets Act, 18 U.S.C. § 1839**) provides indefinite protection for formulas, methods, techniques, processes if (a) economic value derives from secrecy and (b) reasonable documented measures are taken to maintain secrecy. Neural network architecture, training-dataset composition + cleaning methodology, hyperparameter configurations, and weight values all qualify (para 24). K7 para 25 cites *Neural Magic, Inc. v. Facebook, Inc.* on the requirement to prove the trade secret derives **actual economic value from secrecy** — a meticulous-documentation burden.
- **Dual-Entity HoldCo / OpCo** (paras 27–30): K7 prescribes the same Wyoming (or Delaware) IP HoldCo + Florida OpCo structure that K5 / K6 corroborate. K7 specifically frames the architecture's three benefits: (1) liability segregation — algorithmic trading inherently risks margin calls, flash crashes, malfunctions; OpCo creditors cannot reach IP held in HoldCo; (2) easier valuation + smoother structural transitions if the founder pivots from prop trading to licensing predictive models to third parties; (3) trade-secret confidentiality maintained via formal **Intercompany Intellectual Property License Agreement** that defines scope of use + royalty payments + confidentiality obligations. K7 makes Wyoming/Delaware HoldCo a coordinate alternative without choosing between them (same posture as K5; K6 is the source that picks Wyoming specifically).

### Section 4: Regulatory Thresholds

K7 paras 33–53 cover four federal/state regulatory regimes:

- **SEC Dealer Definition Expansion (Rule 3a5-4)** (paras 35–40): Effective April 2024 with compliance May 2025. Expanded statutory definition catches algorithmic firms acting as de facto market makers. Triggers: (a) regular two-sided trading interest at/near best available prices, (b) revenue primarily from bid-ask spread capture or maker-taker rebates. **Carve-out path**: directional / momentum / mean-reversion / statistical-arbitrage strategies that don't systematically capture the bid-ask spread on both sides of the order book avoid dealer classification. Meticulous algorithmic-execution-logic monitoring is required to avoid inadvertent crossing into unregistered market-making activity. This is the **first cited statement** of the 3a5-4 carve-out logic in the corpus that K6's @concepts/ip-sale-corporate-structure.md SEC §475(f) section now references.
- **Large Trader Reporting Rule 13h-1** (paras 41–45): Quantitative threshold — **2 million shares OR $20 million FMV per single calendar day**, OR **20 million shares OR $200 million FMV per calendar month**. Filing Form 13H assigns a **Large Trader Identification Number (LTID)** which the firm provides to all executing broker-dealers; brokers maintain time-stamped records + report to SEC on request. K7 specifically notes "active algorithmic startups can trigger these volume thresholds rapidly, even with modest capital, due to high turnover velocity" (para 45) — a structural risk not present for traditional buy-and-hold firms.
- **Investment Adviser Registration + Software Provision** (paras 47–50): If the firm manages external capital, registration is mandatory (state-level if RAUM <$100M; under Florida Securities and Investor Protection Act, registration triggered at **6+ Florida residents within 12 months**). K7 specifically cites SEC enforcement against "AI washing" (Delphia + Global Predictions settlements). For SaaS — historically the **Lowe v. SEC publisher's exemption** (impersonal non-tailored financial software) provided shelter, but if software analyzes user-personal financial data to generate tailored trade execution, it crosses into actionable regulated investment advice. **July 2024 amendment to the Internet Adviser Exemption Rule 203A-2(e)** allows automated providers to register directly with the SEC instead of navigating state-by-state regulators — but **eliminates the prior 15-client de minimis exception**, requiring zero non-internet clients + exclusively operational interactive website. This is new substrate that K7 contributes to @concepts/publishers-exclusion-iaa.md (which previously had only the Cemini-relevance framing).
- **CFTC Oversight + Florida Fintech Sandbox** (paras 52–53): Commodities/futures/swaps trading triggers CFTC. CTA registration exemption at **CFTC Rule 4.14(a)(10)** (fewer than 15 clients + does not hold out as CTA publicly). NEW: **Florida Fintech Sandbox under Chapter 559.952 of Florida Statutes** — supervised testing program with rule-waivers + control-person disclosure + capped maximum-consumer count during testing. See @concepts/florida-fintech-sandbox.md (K7 promotion).

### Section 5: Banking + Brokerage Infrastructure

K7 paras 56–66 are the **most operationally granular** treatment of trading-firm financial plumbing in the corpus:

- **Corporate Banking — Brex over Mercury** (paras 57–59): Both are fintech-banking-platform alternatives to legacy banks (which subject algorithmic startups to "arduous compliance reviews, delayed onboarding, primitive digital interfaces"). Mercury = fast setup, basic features, treasury yield gated by $250K cash balance or annual fee. **Brex = enterprise-grade**: AI-powered spend management, granular policy enforcement, multiple investment options for idle treasury, sophisticated API access for internal accounting reconciliation, programmatic fund transfers between Florida OpCo + IP HoldCo (intercompany royalty payments), venture debt access. K7 prescribes Brex for both HoldCo and OpCo. See @entities/platforms/brex.md (K7 promotion).
- **Brokerage — IBKR for institutional, Alpaca/QuantConnect for development** (paras 61–66):
    - **Alpaca + QuantConnect** = developer-tier alternatives — multi-asset API + backtesting environments. Good for prototyping; sub-institutional liquidity / routing.
    - **Interactive Brokers (IBKR)** = "preeminent industry standard for quantitative startups" (para 62). API tiers: **Web REST API** (websocket streaming for account management + non-latency-sensitive trading); **TWS API** (Java/Python/C#/C++; 100+ advanced order types; routing protocols; conditional algorithms); **FIX CTCI** (lowest latency; bypasses TWS desktop gateway; routes directly to exchange matching engines via leased lines or extranets).
    - **Professional Subscriber classification**: under exchange regulations, **any corporate entity / trust / LLC is categorically classified as Professional Subscriber** — substantially higher monthly fees for real-time market data across all exchanges (para 64). Operational-cost-model implication: Cemini's recurring data overheads will be at Professional rates regardless of trading-volume tier.
    - **Minimum Thresholds**: $500 corporate equity balance to activate market data subscriptions; **FIX CTCI minimum $1,500/month** primary FIX session commission (para 65).
    - **Legal Entity Identifier (LEI)**: 20-character alphanumeric global identifier mandated post-2008 for institutional brokerage onboarding. IBKR (and any institutional broker) **strictly requires active LEI** to onboard an LLC or C-Corp + grant trading permissions (para 66). LEI obtained via Global LEI Foundation (GLEIF). See @entities/platforms/interactive-brokers.md (K7 promotion) for the full IBKR-specific stack.

### Section 6: Prioritized Legal Execution Roadmap (3 phases / 8 numbered steps)

K7 paras 69–82 are the canonical sequenced execution roadmap. The phases are conceptually equivalent to but **more granular than** @sources/3-10-algorithmic-trading-ip-sale-audit.md §4 implementation sequence:

**Phase 1: Entity Formation, Local Licensing, Asset Protection**
1. **Incorporate IP HoldCo** (Wyoming or Delaware LLC) — holds title to AI models; no business operations; no major financial accounts; no trades.
2. **Incorporate OpCo** (single-member Florida LLC, Davie FL) — operational trading entity; pass-through income; no state corporate income tax.
3. **Local Municipal Compliance** — Broward County + Town of Davie Local Business Tax Receipt (BTR); Home Occupation BTR if dev work happens in home office (zoning compliance).
4. **Draft Intercompany IP License** — formal written agreement: HoldCo as Licensor, Florida OpCo as Licensee; non-exclusive right to use algorithmic models; data security protocols; royalty payment structure.

**Phase 2: Tax Optimization + Legal Identifiers**
5. **Acquire EINs + LEI** — separate EINs for HoldCo and OpCo from IRS; LEI for Florida OpCo via Global LEI Foundation (GLEIF) — do this immediately to ensure brokerage-onboarding readiness.
6. **File §475(f) Election** — drafted + submitted to IRS for the Florida OpCo by the unextended due date of the **prior year's** tax return.
7. **Implement Trade Secret Protocols** — source code, training datasets, RL models reside on isolated encrypted servers legally owned by IP HoldCo; OpCo accesses only via secure API keys.

**Phase 3: Financial Infrastructure + Regulatory Monitoring**
8. **Establish Corporate Banking** — Brex for both HoldCo + OpCo (working capital, automated royalty transfers, R&E expense management).
9. **Brokerage Integration** — IBKR Pro corporate account under Florida OpCo, using LEI + EIN; provision API keys (REST or TWS); subscribe to Professional market data feeds.
10. **Implement Volume Monitoring (Rule 13h-1)** — hardcode tracking logic into execution algorithms. If turnover approaches 2M shares OR $20M daily, **immediately pause operations**, file Form 13H, obtain LTID, submit to broker.
11. **Monitor Dealer Triggers + Expansion Paths** — continuously audit algorithmic strategy. If models evolve into passive bilateral liquidity provision (capturing bid-ask spread), prepare for SEC Dealer Registration. If commercializing software to retail users, prepare for state IA registration or SEC Internet Adviser registration.

(The roadmap text uses paragraph numbering rather than explicit step-numbering; the reconstruction above counts 11 distinct actionable steps across 3 phases.)

## Snippets

### S1 — LLC over C-Corp for solo-operator prop trader (para 13)
> "However, for a proprietary algorithmic trading firm operating solely on founder capital without immediate plans for external institutional equity financing, the C-Corporation structure presents catastrophic tax disadvantages. The most critical drawback is the imposition of double taxation. A C-Corporation pays corporate income tax on its trading profits at the federal rate of 21%, in addition to any applicable state corporate taxes. Subsequently, the owner is taxed a second time at the individual level when those retained profits are distributed as dividends, subject to capital gains rates of up to 20% plus the 3.8% Net Investment Income Tax (NIIT). For a high-volume quantitative trading firm generating substantial liquid profits that must be continuously reinvested to compound returns, this dual tax burden rapidly erodes algorithmic alpha."
>
> *— K7 para 7. Entity-selection thesis statement. Convergent with @sources/3-10-algorithmic-trading-ip-sale-audit.md §2(a).*

### S2 — §475(f) MTM endorsement (paras 14–15)
> "To circumvent these punitive limitations, a proprietary trading LLC must formally qualify for 'Trader Tax Status' (TTS) and execute a Section 475(f) Mark-to-Market (MTM) election. The MTM election fundamentally alters the tax treatment of the firm's trading activities. Trading gains and losses are converted from capital to ordinary business income, exempting the firm from the $3,000 capital loss limitation and allowing the full, immediate deduction of trading losses against other forms of income. Most importantly, the Section 475(f) election completely exempts the trading activity from wash sale rules, allowing the automated bots to trade at any frequency without generating complex deferred loss accounting adjustments."
>

### S4 — Trade-secret over patent for AI (para 23)

### S5 — Defend Trade Secrets Act + economic-value-from-secrecy burden (paras 24–25)

### S7 — SEC Rule 3a5-4 dealer-definition expansion + carve-out logic (paras 36–40)
> "However, the SEC has fundamentally altered the landscape by expanding the statutory definition of a 'dealer' and 'government securities dealer' under Exchange Act Rules 3a5-4 and 3a44-2. ... Effective April 2024, with compliance mandated by May 2025, entities that assume significant liquidity-providing roles must register with the SEC, join a Self-Regulatory Organization (SRO) such as FINRA, and comply with onerous federal securities laws, including net capital requirements. The expanded rules utilize qualitative standards to identify unregistered dealer activity. A proprietary firm triggers registration if it engages in a regular pattern of buying and selling securities that has the effect of providing liquidity to other market participants, specifically by: Regularly expressing trading interests at or near the best available prices on both sides of the market for the same security. Earning revenue primarily from capturing bid-ask spreads, or from capturing trading venue liquidity incentives (maker-taker rebates). If the proprietary reinforcement learning models utilize directional, momentum, mean-reversion, or statistical arbitrage strategies that do not systematically capture the bid-ask spread on both sides of the order book, the firm may avoid classification as a dealer."

### S8 — Large Trader Rule 13h-1 quantitative thresholds (paras 42–45)
> "The threshold for 'Large Trader' status is strictly quantitative. Upon filing Form 13H, the SEC assigns a Large Trader Identification Number (LTID). The firm must provide this LTID to all executing broker-dealers. ### S9 — Lowe v. SEC + Internet Adviser Exemption Rule 203A-2(e) July 2024 amendment (paras 49–50)
> "If the firm intends to operate purely as a software-as-a-service (SaaS) provider—licensing the algorithmic trading bots to retail users without directly holding their funds—the regulatory classification becomes highly complex.
