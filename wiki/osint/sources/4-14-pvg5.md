## Narrative

This paper attacks the cost structure of the dominant vector-search stack — **HNSW + float32 + cosine similarity** — and argues it forces an unavoidable RAM-cost coupling: one billion 768-dim float32 vectors need ~3 TB of RAM, and HNSW's greedy graph traversal requires the full graph resident in memory. The authors survey the two standard mitigations and show both trade something away: *storage disaggregation* (S3 Vectors, Turbopuffer) pays an I/O rehydration penalty (recall stabilizes at 85–90%, dropping below 50% under metadata filters); *vector quantization* below 4 bits/dim degrades recall sharply, with single-bit binarization historically treated as the accuracy-collapse threshold (LSH being the cautionary example).

The proposed alternative is an information-theoretic stack with three co-designed components: **Maximally Informative Binarization (MIB)**, a loss-minimizing conversion of float vectors to single-bit codes that preserves the most salient features (claimed ~32× memory reduction, unlike random-projection LSH); an **Efficient Distance Metric (EDM)** using native bitwise operations such as Hamming distance instead of float cosine; and an **Information-Theoretic Score (ITS)** that ranks by semantic content rather than geometric proximity and acts as a built-in reranker. Because codes are compact, the system does *exhaustive* linear scan rather than approximate graph traversal — yielding deterministic retrieval and no accuracy degradation under high query concurrency. The "lock-and-key" claim is that this stack is the first genuinely serverless-native vector search: compact codes fit in a Lambda function, bitwise ops suit bursty CPU, and stateless exhaustive scans scale via concurrency (1,000+ functions) rather than the QPS-vs-accuracy `ef` tuning incumbents need. Evaluation uses the MAIR benchmark (14 datasets, 10,038 queries) against Elasticsearch, Pinecone, PGVector, and Qdrant, reporting comparable retrieval quality with lower latency, constant throughput at high request rates, and a cost-per-query rather than cost-per-server economic model.

**Cemini relevance**: This is directly the K47 kb-server-retrieval-scaling problem — the HNSW-vs-binarization tradeoff bears on whether the librarian needs a large in-memory ANN index or can move toward exhaustive binary scan as the wiki corpus grows.
