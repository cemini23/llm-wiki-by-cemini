## Narrative

A short essay on risk-taking, built from a regular dinner with two retired or working options traders.

The core claim: looking back, the author and his former business partner agree they should have taken much more risk. He tests this against survivorship bias and keeps it. The argument rests on the SIG example. SIG tightened markets to win share, accepted slightly worse risk-reward per trade for far more absolute dollars, then used the cash to scale coverage, which let it price and hedge tighter. That is a flywheel: better product, more share, more proprietary data, repeat. [Source: substack-rss-moontower-2026-11-22-hillstone.md]

The contemporary contrast is Jane Street, whose balance sheet and coverage let it price risk with a near-linear utility of wealth, while the author jokes he would once have traded a half-dollar of edge and now fights for a penny.

The transferable rule: when you find an edge that others have not caught up to, and the edge can absorb capital, push it, because the edge will not last. Efficiency gains are good, but they compress the edge.

This is an opinion essay, not a measurement. Tag the SIG account `[TENTATIVE]`. The operating lesson applies to the operator's own tooling edges: a workflow advantage is a decaying asset, so size it while it exists.
