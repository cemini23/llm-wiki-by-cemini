## Narrative

### What it is

An edited transcript of Changpeng Zhao speaking at a Binance Life reader event in Hong Kong on 27 August 2026. It covers his book, the decision to sell his home for Bitcoin, Binance's regulatory setbacks, hiring, and where he thinks Web3 goes next. [Source: substack-rss-wublock-2026-11-26-cz-qa-bnbs-rocky-debut-startup-lessons-and-web3s.md]

Most of it is personal narrative and advice. Three sections carry market content.

### The DEX and KYC argument

Zhao's position: a decentralised exchange can now separate an international platform from a US one, and block restricted jurisdictions rather than require KYC from every user. He says his own case turned on an allegation that Binance's KYC was insufficient, at a time when KYC was already in place.

This is a former defendant describing the compliance bar he would like. It is an opinion, not a rule. Treat it as `[TENTATIVE]` and as an indicator of where product teams think the line is moving, not as legal guidance.

### Real-world assets, the part he says surprised him

He states that tokenised securities are growing much faster than he expected, and that he underrated the category. His reasoning is access: most people worldwide cannot open a foreign brokerage account, and a tokenised share removes that step. He cites Binance's user base above 300 million and notes that many national exchanges are thin — the Philippine market trading around $50 million a day, roughly one large New York trade.

He extends the argument to stablecoins, calling them an RWA category, with the dollar-denominated market at roughly $200bn to $300bn, and asks why other countries would leave that to the dollar.

The structural point is sound and testable: an issuer's addressable buyer set is a function of account access, and tokenisation lowers that cost. The size claims are platform figures.

### Where he puts the geography

Hong Kong first for Web3, on the strength of its finance talent and its access from the mainland. He is explicit that Hong Kong's advantage does not carry to AI, because power and data-centre costs are uncompetitive. Outside Hong Kong he names the UAE (Dubai and Abu Dhabi) and the US, where he says the policy environment has become more supportive.

That three-way ranking is a useful read on operator sentiment, and it lines up with the policy direction recorded in @concepts/asia-tokenisation-and-exchange-policy-2026-11.md.

### Startup material

The advice is conventional and mostly correct: ask what you can afford to lose before starting; keep five deep user conversations a month rather than surveying hundreds; hire for initiative; get genuinely good at one thing. Nothing here is new, and nothing here is wrong.
