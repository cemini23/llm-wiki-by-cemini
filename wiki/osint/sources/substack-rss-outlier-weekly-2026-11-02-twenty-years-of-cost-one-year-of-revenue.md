## Narrative

**A teaser, but the quoted sentence is the whole argument.**

Anthropic's S-1 says: *"Our revenue is primarily consumption-based, and much of it is not subject to long-term contractual commitments."*

The compute side is the mirror image. Per the teaser, some infrastructure commitments run **as long as twenty years, with annual escalators**.

That is a duration mismatch stated in the company's own filing: revenue that can stop next month against costs that escalate for two decades.

### Why the asymmetry matters more than the size

A short-duration revenue base against a long-duration cost base is the classic structure that works until it does not. It is fine while demand grows, because new consumption covers the commitment. It is fatal precisely when growth slows — which is when the commitment is still there and the revenue is not.

The annual escalators make it worse: the cost rises on a schedule that has nothing to do with demand.
