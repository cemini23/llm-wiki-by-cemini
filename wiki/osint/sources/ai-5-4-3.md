## Narrative

The paper proposes xCPD — a multivariate time-series forecasting architecture that uses graph spectral decomposition to explicitly route channel-patch dependencies. Where PatchTST and iTransformer treat channels independently (channel-independent patching) or mix them uniformly, xCPD builds a learned channel-dependency graph, computes spectral decomposition on it per forecasting step, and uses the spectral coefficients to gate which patches on which channels interact. The motivation is that real multivariate series (e.g., multi-asset price/volume blocks) have sparse, time-varying cross-channel structure that uniform mixing dilutes.

The paper reports SOTA on several multivariate forecasting benchmarks (ETT, Weather, Traffic, Electricity) with particular gains on high-channel-count datasets where the dependency graph is informative. Ablations show the spectral gating matters — removing it collapses performance toward PatchTST levels.

For Cemini the relevance is direct: multi-ticker, multi-feature forecasting over mid-to-large universes is exactly where uniform-mixing transformers underperform. xCPD is a candidate replacement for PatchTST/iTransformer in the multi-horizon forecasting block, especially for cross-asset signal generation. Computational profile (standard attention + spectral decomposition) is reasonable; should run CPU at batch cadence.

## Key findings

- xCPD = channel-dependency graph + spectral decomposition routing for multivariate TSF.
- SOTA on ETT/Weather/Traffic/Electricity benchmarks, largest gains on high-channel datasets.
- Ablations confirm spectral gating is the key component — not just "add another graph layer."
- Addresses a real failure mode of PatchTST/iTransformer (channel independence vs. uniform mixing).
- Direct candidate for multi-ticker cross-asset forecasting in Cemini.
