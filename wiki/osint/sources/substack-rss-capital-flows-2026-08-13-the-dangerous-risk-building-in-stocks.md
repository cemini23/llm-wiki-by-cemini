## Narrative

Livestream/teaser on the dangerous risk building in US equities. Core theses:

1. **Valuations are a sensitivity reading, not a sell signal.** High valuations alone don't cause crashes; crashes come from asset-liability mismatches when positioning gets too aggressive.
2. **Index returns are valuation-expansion, not earnings.** Decomposing S&P moves (last 2y, especially YTD) shows most came from valuation expansion driven by macro liquidity (quantity + price of money). The earlier-2026 pullback was valuation contraction → buying opportunity, not an earnings event.
3. **Hypersensitivity.** At record valuations the system is hypersensitive: a small macro-liquidity contraction that would barely register in a normal regime can now produce a 10% index move either way because the money base supporting prices is so excessive.
4. **Tech is the entire market.** S&P up ~20% over 6 months with 11 points from tech alone; NVIDIA pullbacks are market/sector-driven, not fundamental. Compute financialization (NVIDIA guaranteeing residual GPU value, owning the market around its product) deepens the monopoly.
5. **The carry trade is the quantity-of-money channel.** Falling yen funds yen-denominated leverage into US equities; AUD/yen + MXN/yen grinding higher confirm capital moving out the risk curve. Unwind → even a modest yen pop can drive a 10% equity drawdown regardless of CapEx/NVIDIA earnings; 2024 unwind took Nikkei −27% peak-to-trough.
6. **Cross-asset covariance falling** — market shakes off shocks but tails compress; VIX rising alongside ES new highs = short covering / hedges getting cooked, signalling increasing probability of an extreme move even as the index melts up.
