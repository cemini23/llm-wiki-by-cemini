## Narrative

The paper proposes an **LLM-guided, no-regret online-learning framework** for medium-to-long-horizon portfolio allocation that targets risk-adjusted (not raw) returns. Portfolio construction is modeled as a single-agent repeated game: at each rebalancing step the agent picks weights `a(t)` on the simplex (`Σ aᵢ = 1`, `aᵢ ∈ [0,1]`) and minimizes *hindsight regret* against the best fixed allocation. The policy update is a **follow-the-leader (FTL) variant** — rather than the multiplicative-weight mixtures of classical Hedge/Universal Portfolio, it greedily selects the single highest-performing allocation so far. The authors are explicit that this greedy choice **forfeits the sublinear-regret guarantee** of principled no-regret algorithms; it is a deliberate practical-performance trade. Candidate actions are generated by perturbing one asset's weight at a time around a baseline allocation (Algorithm 1), keeping the search tractable and interpretable.

Two LLM-driven overlays sit *upstream* of the regret loop, not inside it: (1) a **sentiment gate** using CNN's Fear & Greed Index that either permits rebalancing or forces liquidation to cash under sentiment extremes; (2) an **LLM hedging module** that proposes complementary sector-level hedges. Notably, the authors report LLM-driven dynamic *sector clustering* underperformed the full universe (~34% lower cumulative return, ~37% lower Sharpe), while LLM *hedging* reliably cut drawdown and lifted Sharpe (up to ~63% under quarterly rebalancing).

Evaluated on a 4-asset "COCKROACH" universe and a 51-ETF sector universe (2011–2024), the strategy beat SPY buy-and-hold by **+68.6% cumulative return, +119% Sharpe, ~+262% Calmar**, with max drawdown ~16% vs SPY's ~34% — all at quarterly rebalancing. Relevance to Cemini: this validates regret-minimization + sentiment gating as a low-turnover allocation layer, and provides a cautionary, well-quantified data point that LLMs are useful for *risk-offsetting hedge selection* but unreliable for *return-seeking sector picking* — directly informing how an LLM brain should be wired into the portfolio-construction pipeline.
