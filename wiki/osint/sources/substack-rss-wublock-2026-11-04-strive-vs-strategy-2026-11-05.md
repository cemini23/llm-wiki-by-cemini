## Narrative

Two Bitcoin treasury companies, opposite capital-structure moves, in the same week.

| | **Strive** | **Strategy** |
|---|---|---|
| BTC this week | **+6,182** | neither bought nor sold |
| Financing action | $630m SATS offering completed; up to **$1bn** new offering announced | ~**$204.9m** repurchasing STRC |
| Preferred stock / convertible debt as % of BTC holding value (at $80k BTC) | **61.1%** | **29.6%** |
| Annual dividends (or dividends + interest) as % of BTC holding value | **8.4%** | **2.2%** |

These ratios do not deduct cash or other assets.

### The SATS detail that matters most

**SATS carries a fixed 15% annual dividend.** At the $90 initial offering price, annual dividends equal about **16.7% of gross proceeds**.

The prospectus discloses a reserve covering the **first 18 months** of dividends on the initial issuance, and the company **expects to fund cash dividends primarily through additional financing**.

That is the structure to sit with. A 15% fixed obligation funded by more issuance is a promise whose service cost depends on continued access to capital. It works while the market is open.

### The two philosophies

**Strive** treats a higher preferred-capital share as a way to **outperform Bitcoin over the long term** — leverage on the asset.

**Strategy** has folded **buying back STRC at a discount** into its capital allocation, which **reduces future dividend obligations**. It is managing the liability side, not the asset side.

### Why the comparison is useful

Both are Bitcoin-holding vehicles. One is maximising exposure and paying for it with fixed obligations; the other is retiring obligations at a discount and accepting flat exposure.

At 61.1% versus 29.6%, Strive's preferred layer is roughly twice the burden relative to its holdings, and its annual distribution is roughly four times as large as a share of the same base.

**Caveat.** Ratios are as stated, at an assumed $80,000 BTC, with no deduction for cash or other assets. That assumption drives every percentage in the table.
