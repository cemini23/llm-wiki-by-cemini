## Narrative

An empirical study of how — and why — European firms move from responsible-AI (RAI) *principles* to *industrialized practice*, set against the incoming EU AI Act. Bughin surveys a panel of ~380 publicly quoted companies headquartered in the four largest EU markets (France, Germany, Italy, Spain) plus the UK, sampling RAI maturity at three discrete points (2018, 2021, 2024). The study deliberately treats RAI as a longitudinal *trajectory* — from early recognition, to principle design, to implementation — rather than a cross-sectional snapshot, and uses random-forest ML to rank the drivers of "RAI deepening."

Central finding: RAI deepening trajectories depend not on regulation alone but on an **ensemble of internal firm factors**, ranked by importance: (1) investment in AI technology and human-resource expertise in data/machine learning; (2) active AI-ecosystem play; and (3) robust MLOps practices. These factors are tightly coupled to a firm's overall **organizational AI maturity** — meaning RAI is largely a reflection of internal AI capability, conceptualized through a dynamic-capabilities / resource-based lens. The headline policy implication: "securing 'responsible AI' across firms may require more than a regulatory playing field." RAI adoption across Europe is spreading but far from uniform, with a clear bifurcation between fast/early adopters and a material portion of firms still struggling to operationalize principles. Bughin warns of "ethics washing" risk in public RAI announcements, and suggests regulators may need *incentive-based* mechanisms that promote the AI capabilities underpinning RAI — not just compliance mandates.

**Cemini relevance**: directly relevant to acquirer due-diligence framing — it shows that credible RAI posture is read as a *signal of AI maturity* (AI investment, ML talent, MLOps discipline), not a checkbox; Cemini's governance story should evidence the underlying capability stack, not just published principles.
