## Narrative

### The CFTC interpretation

On Thursday the CFTC issued an interpretation of Regulation 38.151(b), the **Impartial Access Requirements** for designated contract markets (DCMs). The rule is simple. A DCM must give competitive, open, fair, and impartial access to its members.

The interpretation says geography is not an excuse. Chairman Michael S. Selig: "Geographic access restrictions are inconsistent with those purposes and the Act's impartial access requirements." [Source: substack-rss-event-horizon-2026-11-06-cftc-to-prediction-markets-stop-blocking-states.md]

DCMs must do two things within 30 days:

1. Stop domestic geographic restrictions that violate the rule.
2. Make sure intermediaries that access the market are not blocking access.

If a DCM cannot comply, it must file an explanation with the Division of Market Oversight. [Source: substack-rss-event-horizon-2026-11-06-cftc-to-prediction-markets-stop-blocking-states.md]

The guidance reaches every operator, not only DCMs, because FanDuel and Robinhood access Kalshi markets as intermediaries. Robinhood also runs its own DCM.

### The open questions

The newsletter flags what the text does not settle.

- **What counts as an acceptable excuse?** Kalshi holds court orders in Nevada, Michigan, and Washington. It is not clear whether a court order is a valid excuse or whether the CFTC expects operators to ignore it.
- **What happens on non-compliance?** A footnote cites the CEA injunctive authority. The practical remedy is likely a CFTC lawsuit.
- **The operator's dilemma.** Comply with the CFTC and take state liability, or obey a state court order and face the CFTC.

- A federal judge issued the **first injunction banning Kalshi sports contracts on tribal lands**. The order covers the Blue Lake Rancheria and Chicken Ranch Rancheria of Me-Wuk Indians in California. Judge Jacqueline Scott Corley issued it after an appellate court reversed her earlier denial. [Source: substack-rss-event-horizon-2026-11-06-cftc-to-prediction-markets-stop-blocking-states.md]

### The Kalshi single-stock perpetual

Kalshi launched regulated **single-stock perpetual futures** on more than 50 NYSE and Nasdaq names. The company says users traded over **$300m** on the stealth-launch morning. The product has no expiry, no strikes, and no PFOF. Leverage sits under CFTC approval and well below offshore perp levels. [Source: substack-rss-event-horizon-2026-11-06-cftc-to-prediction-markets-stop-blocking-states.md]

The newsletter notes the CFTC is being sued over the product.

### DraftKings Q3

DraftKings added more than **one million** Predictions customers in the quarter. October annualized consumer volume was **$10.1bn**, up nearly 3x from July. Annualized total notional volume was **$56.8bn**. The company raised its 2026 Predictions investment to **$650–700m** and expects the 2026 cohort to pay back in about **20 months**. Take rate on DKeX was **11.1%** in September and October, of which DraftKings kept about two thirds. [Source: substack-rss-event-horizon-2026-11-06-cftc-to-prediction-markets-stop-blocking-states.md]

The stock fell more than 20% after hours. The company missed on adjusted EBITDA and lowered 2026 guidance.

### The Nate Silver dispute

Nate Silver, an adviser to Polymarket, attacked Kalshi and CEO Tarek Mansour over the post-election victory lap. His argument: prediction markets converged to model prices, not the other way around. He published a chart showing that the prices Kalshi held on election morning had been in the models for days or weeks. [Source: substack-rss-event-horizon-2026-11-06-cftc-to-prediction-markets-stop-blocking-states.md]

The newsletter's own framing is the useful one: Kalshi blames the crowd when a favourite loses and claims to be a truth machine when favourites win.

## Dead Ends

- **Reading the interpretation as settled law.** It is an agency interpretation, not a court ruling, and its enforcement path is unstated.
- **Treating the volume table as venue-equivalent.** Polymarket US and Kalshi are DCMs. Novig and Underdog trade on other structures. The newsletter labels its table "exchanges" but does not normalise.
