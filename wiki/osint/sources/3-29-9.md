## Narrative

A theoretical MARL paper (arXiv 2601.12518, Jan 2026) tackling cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning when communication is expensive. Standard cooperative MARL assumes frequent access to global information — team rewards, other agents' actions — typically via a shared replay buffer. In decentralized systems (multi-robot fleets, sensor networks, traffic control) this is unrealistic: acquiring global state at every timestep incurs heavy communication cost. The goal is to **minimize the number of communication rounds** while preserving convergence.

The standard workaround is **importance sampling (IS)**: reweight old data collected under a previous ("base") policy to estimate gradients for the current policy. But IS becomes unstable when communication is sparse — the missing-data probability is high, so the base policy is badly outdated and IS variance blows up. The paper's key technique is **base policy prediction**: rather than keeping the base policy fixed, it uses *old gradients* to predict a sequence of upcoming policy updates, then collects samples for that whole predicted sequence of base policies within a single communication round. This shrinks the gap between base and current policy without extra communication.

Theoretical results: the algorithm converges to an ε-Nash equilibrium in **potential games with only O(ε⁻³/⁴) communication rounds** and O(poly(maxᵢ|Aᵢ|)·ε⁻¹¹/⁴) samples — improving prior state-of-the-art communication cost, and crucially removing the *exponential* dependence on the joint action space size that earlier work (e.g. Liu et al. 2021's ∏|Aᵢ| terms) suffered. Results extend to general Markov Cooperative Games, finding an agent-wise local maximum. Empirically, base policy prediction is tested in simulated potential games and inside **MAPPO** on complex environments: it significantly cuts communication cost while matching the no-constraint baseline, whereas standard algorithms fail under the same communication budget.

**Cemini relevance**: Communication-constrained cooperative MARL is directly relevant to Cemini's 5-agent MARL — agents that must coordinate without paying full global-information cost every step. Base policy prediction offers a concrete recipe for keeping a MAPPO-style stack stable when inter-agent synchronization is throttled.
