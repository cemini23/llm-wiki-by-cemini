## Narrative

A podcast summary with Chris Yin, co-founder and CEO of Plume, on real-world assets.

### The thesis

**Stablecoins are among the most successful RWAs** — they make dollar-denominated assets accessible worldwide, and they did it by being useful rather than by being tokenised.

For the broader market, **"the biggest challenge is no longer simply tokenizing assets, but distributing them to users and building sustainable liquidity."**

That is a shift in where the work is. Tokenisation was a technical problem and it is largely solved. Distribution and liquidity are commercial problems, and they are harder.

### The risk disclosure, which is the useful part

RWA products offer **12%–15% annualised yields**. The CEO names where the extra return comes from, beyond smart-contract risk:

- **underlying credit exposure**
- **custody**
- **asset maturity**
- **redemption restrictions**

That list is the honest version of a yield conversation. A 12–15% on-chain yield is not a technology premium; it is a bundle of credit, custody, duration, and illiquidity that the buyer is being paid to hold.

**Why this is worth filing.** Any comparison between an on-chain RWA yield and a risk-free rate is meaningless unless the four risks above are priced. The quoted figure without them is a headline, not a yield.

### Outlook

Banks, asset managers, and exchanges becoming more active in RWAs; over two to three years, evolution beyond tokenised traditional assets into on-chain derivatives, structured products, and blended portfolios.

**Caveat.** Podcast summary; the CEO is describing his own market.
