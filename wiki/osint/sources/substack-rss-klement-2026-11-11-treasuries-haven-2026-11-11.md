## Narrative

A follow-up to a March post, *"Trust the dollar, not the Treasury"*, which argued that investors are losing faith **in the US Treasury and its paper** while keeping faith **in the dollar**. This post brings a study that tests it.

### The method

The Banque de France measured **correlations between assets during periods of heightened risk aversion** — the operational definition of a safe haven. Covered: government and corporate bonds, currencies, and alternatives including gold, silver, oil, and Bitcoin.

Correlation-during-stress is the right measure and it is also a demanding one: an asset that rises on average is not a haven; an asset that rises *when everything else falls* is.

### The findings

Two, and the second is the one to keep:

1. **Bitcoin, oil, and silver have no safe-have characteristic.**
2. **Gold does — but it ranks well below most government bonds or the US dollar as a safe haven.**

The author pre-empts the obvious objection by stating the gold result up front, which is unusually direct. Gold works; it is not the best.

The chart ranks government and investment-grade corporate bonds by average haven score, with per-episode scores beside the average.

### What "Treasuries lose their haven status" means precisely

Not that Treasuries fail. That their **rank has slipped relative to alternatives** — which is consistent with the March thesis: the dollar is still the haven, the paper is less so.

The distinction matters because the two claims are routinely conflated. A world where the dollar is trusted and Treasuries less so is a world where the reserve currency and the reserve asset have separated, and that is a different regime from a loss of dollar confidence.
