## Narrative

A systematic empirical benchmark of multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) algorithms for dynamic price optimization in competitive retail markets. Four algorithms are compared under the centralized-training–decentralized-execution (CTDE) paradigm: **MAPPO** (on-policy, clipped surrogate + GAE), **MASAC** (off-policy, entropy-regularized soft actor-critic), **MADDPG** (off-policy, deterministic, centralized critic per agent), and an **Independent DDPG (IDDPG)** baseline where each agent treats others as part of the environment.

The testbed is a custom `MarketplaceEnv` simulating N=3 competing sellers, parameterized from a trimmed UCI Online Retail dataset (~19,000 rows, top 50 SKUs). Per-SKU demand is modeled with CatBoost regressors (validation R²=0.6547), demand is allocated across sellers via a softmax market-share model (competition intensity β=10), and Gaussian demand noise (σ≈730) is added for realism. Each episode is T=24 monthly pricing decisions; actions are continuous price adjustments within ±30% of a reference price. All algorithms train for 400 episodes across 10 random seeds with identical hyperparameters where applicable (γ=0.99, lr=3×10⁻⁴, PPO clip ε=0.2, GAE λ=0.95).

Results (Table II, normalized): **MAPPO achieved the highest average profit (0.91), outperforming MADDPG by +16% and IDDPG by +47%**, with strong fairness (Jain's Index 0.94) and low cross-seed variance. **MADDPG produced the fairest outcomes** (Jain's Index 0.96, lowest Gini 0.11) at slightly lower profit. IDDPG showed the highest variability, confirming the need for CTDE coordination. MASAC failed to converge — negative average profit and a sharp early collapse — attributed to Q-value overestimation bias and entropy-coefficient sensitivity in the multi-agent setting. Practical takeaway: MAPPO is the reliable choice for profit maximization; MADDPG when equitable agent outcomes are the priority.

**Cemini relevance**: The MAPPO-vs-MADDPG head-to-head is directly relevant to Cemini's MAPPO stack — it independently confirms MAPPO's low-variance, reproducible behavior over deterministic-policy and independent-learner alternatives in a continuous-action, competitive economic environment, and flags MASAC's instability as a cautionary data point.
