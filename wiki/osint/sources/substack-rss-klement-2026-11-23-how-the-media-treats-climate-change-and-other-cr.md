## Narrative

### The study

Researchers at Lancaster University analysed the language of ten UK newspapers on specific topics between 2015 and 2024. Five are left-leaning: Guardian, Independent, iNews, Mirror, Observer. Five are right-leaning: Express, Mail, Sun, Telegraph, Times. [Source: substack-rss-klement-2026-11-23-how-the-media-treats-climate-change-and-other-cr.md]

### Finding

Newspapers disagree on what counts as a crisis. Left-leaning papers emphasise climate change as a climate crisis. Right-leaning papers emphasise the energy crisis and the cost-of-living crisis after the 2022 invasion of Ukraine.

They also disagree on which damage matters. Left-leaning papers focus on environmental damage and damage to lifestyle and property. Right-leaning papers focus on infrastructure damage, such as potholes, and on financial and business damage.

### Why it matters

The framing shapes what readers perceive as important, which shapes political debate and government action. Klement's own framing is that there are plenty of crises; the disagreement is about the label.

### Read

This is a media-content observation, not a market claim. The transferable idea for research work is narrow: a word like "crisis" or "damage" is a framing choice, so a keyword-based sentiment or risk count will inherit the outlet's politics. That matters when building a news-sentiment feed. Indexed in `concepts/media-crisis-framing.md`.
