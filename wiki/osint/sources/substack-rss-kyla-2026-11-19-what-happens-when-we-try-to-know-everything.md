## Narrative

A long personal essay, not a market letter. The argument is that an economy needs people who understand particular circumstances, not only systems that hold every fact. The author builds it from a week of conference travel, a run of infrastructure outages, and a claim that "morals don't matter if an economy works".

The market-relevant fragments are few:

- A run of outages in one week: Microsoft, the FAA grounding flights for four or more hours, Chase customers unable to move money, United, the NSA internal network, Apple, and a large hack. [Source: substack-rss-kyla-2026-11-19-what-happens-when-we-try-to-know-everything.md]
- A WSJ/NORC survey: among 18-to-34 year olds, 9% have "a great deal" of confidence in major companies, 40% "only some", and 48% "hardly any".
- Nvidia fell more than 8% after hours when the WSJ reported the board was preparing to remove Jensen Huang as CEO. He resigned, tried to return, and the board said no. Nvidia was down about 30% from its October high. [Source: substack-rss-kyla-2026-11-19-what-happens-when-we-try-to-know-everything.md]

The operational-trust angle matters for the operator stack: a single-venue or single-provider dependency is a real risk, and user trust falls fast when money cannot move. The Nvidia figures are single-source and should be tagged `[TENTATIVE]` if used.
