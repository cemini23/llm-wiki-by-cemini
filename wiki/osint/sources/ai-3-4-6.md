## Narrative

A practitioner-facing whitepaper from CFTE arguing that **AI literacy** is now an essential workforce competency — analogous to digital literacy — and a compliance obligation. The document is explicitly framed against the **EU AI Act's Article 4**, which (from February 2025) requires providers and deployers of AI systems to ensure "sufficient AI literacy" among staff, tailored to their role, technical knowledge, and the context of AI use. The paper traces the term to Long and Magerko's 2020 academic definition and offers its own: AI literacy is "the ability to understand, evaluate, and confidently use AI technologies, recognising both their capabilities and limitations."

The core framework decomposes AI literacy into **five components**: (1) Core Knowledge of AI Concepts; (2) Foundational Interaction with AI Tools (prompting, refining outputs); (3) Critical Evaluation of AI Outputs (cross-checking against trustworthy sources, recognizing bias and "black box" limits); (4) Awareness of AI Risks and Ethical Considerations (bias, misinformation, privacy, transparency, accountability); and (5) Comfort and Confidence in Engaging with AI — including resilience against "FOBO" (Fear of Becoming Obsolete). The paper proposes these as universal, scalable "minimum criteria," and recommends organizations implement them via structured training, critical-thinking development, ethical grounding, and continuous upskilling. It positions AI literacy as both a non-compliance-risk mitigant and a competitive advantage, noting AI adoption projected to rise from 45% (2022) toward 85% (2025).

**Cemini relevance**: directly informs the workforce-literacy dimension of Cemini's AI-governance posture — for institutional-acquirer due diligence, demonstrable staff AI literacy is now an EU AI Act Article 4 obligation, and this five-component framework gives a defensible structure for evidencing it.
