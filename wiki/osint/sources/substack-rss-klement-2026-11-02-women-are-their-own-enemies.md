## Narrative

A short write-up of one lab experiment on self-assessment and how evaluators respond to it. Single-source: Megalokonomou et al. (2026), cited but not otherwise described.

### The design

**350 participants** answered 20 STEM questions. Each then self-evaluated and guessed their own score. Those self-evaluations went to **1,750 different participants** acting as "career advisers", who recommended either a more ambitious next task or a less ambitious one.

### The result

True scores were nearly equal: men averaged **11 of 20**, women **10 of 20**. Guessed scores were not — men consistently guessed higher than women at every level of actual performance.

The overconfidence carried into the recommendation:

| Condition | Men recommended | Women recommended |
|-----------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Baseline (self-report only) | **44%** | **31%** |
| Objective (true score also given) | **47%** | **37%** |
| Nudge (advisers told women under-promote, men over-promote) | **37%** | **47%** |

Two things happen in the first two rows. The baseline gap is 13 points. Giving advisers the true score **raises both** numbers but narrows the gap to 10 — the correction helps women more than men, but does not close the gap. Only the explicit nudge flips the ordering, and it overshoots: women end 10 points above men.

### The author's reading

Klement declines to endorse the flip. His position is that neither the objective setting nor the nudge is fair, and that the underlying finding is about **self-promotion**, not about evaluator bias alone: the women in the study were their own worst advocates.

His policy suggestion is narrower than the experiment: **anonymise more objective tests** so the adviser sees the score and nothing that hints at gender.

### What it does and does not show

The experiment measures a self-reporting effect on a synthetic task with a self-selected adviser pool. It does not measure hiring, promotion, or pay. The author is careful about that and so is this page.

The transferable idea is mechanical, not moral: **when an evaluator sees only a self-assessment, they are ranking confidence as much as ability.** Supplying the objective measure improves the ranking without changing the underlying behaviour.

## Dead Ends

- **Reading this as a hiring or pay finding.** The study measures task recommendation in a lab, not employment outcomes.
- **Citing the effect size outside this design.** It is one experiment with 350 subjects and a synthetic task.
