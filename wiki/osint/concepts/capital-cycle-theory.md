## Raw Concept

What prompted this page: Massif Capital's Q4 2025 letter references capital-cycle theory (Marathon Asset Management lineage) as the framework underlying their real-assets allocation. Concept page synthesizes the framework for cross-reference.

## Narrative

### Origin

**Marathon Asset Management** (London-based; Edward Chancellor's *Capital Returns* book is the canonical popularization, 2015) developed capital-cycle theory in the 1990s-2000s as a counter-cyclical supply-side investment framework.

### Core mechanism

The capital cycle has four phases:

1. **High returns** in a sector attract capital inflow
2. **New capacity** is built (capex spike)
3. **Supply outstrips demand** → returns compress → equity prices fall
4. **Capital flees** → capex collapses → eventually demand catches up → returns rise → cycle restarts

**Key insight**: most analysts focus on demand; capital-cycle investors focus on **supply**. Supply is more forecastable (capacity takes years to build, capex announcements are public) and the leverage points are at the inflection points of capex.

### Why it works

- **Long capacity lag**: mines, refineries, power plants take 5-15 years to bring online. Underinvestment phases are slow to reverse → durable supply deficits.
- **Behavioral asymmetry**: capital flees a sector at the bottom (when fundamentals are worst but forward returns are best); chases at the top (vice versa).
- **Falsifiable**: capex data is observable. The thesis breaks cleanly when capex rises.

### Massif application

Massif applies capital-cycle theory to **real assets** (mining, energy, materials):

- **Setup**: 2014-2020 saw deep underinvestment in mining capex (post-China-supercycle bust) → durable supply deficits emerging 2024-2027
- **Trigger**: post-2022 commodity demand recovery + deglobalization-induced onshoring + critical-minerals supply-chain panic
- **Positions**: gold/silver miners (EQX, GMIN), copper, lithium, rare earths, uranium — supply-constrained metals where capex collapse 2014-2020 limits 2025-2030 production response
