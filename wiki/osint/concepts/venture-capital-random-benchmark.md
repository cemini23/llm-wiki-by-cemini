## Raw Concept

Question this page answers: do venture-capital funds select better than random, and what does that say about the AI capital cycle?

Built from Klement's summary of Knicker, Bouchaud and Benzaquen (2026).

## Narrative

**The test.** Take thousands of US venture-capital deals. Compare realised money multiple at exit with a benchmark that picks investments at random. If skill exists, funds should beat the random benchmark.

**The result.** No statistical difference. The funds do not beat random selection. [TENTATIVE: one secondary summary, no effect size or interval in the free text]

**The second result.** Deals entered at high multiples do worse than a random investment with a similar entry multiple. Deals entered at low multiples do as expected or better. The pattern matches listed markets: paying up for trophy growth assets destroys the return.

**Why it matters for the AI cycle.** A large share of AI funding runs through venture capital. If fund selection is close to random and high-entry-multiple deals underperform, then the AI capital boom is not evidence of superior allocation. It is evidence of capital abundance. That supports the bubble-side reading in the AI-bubble post: analyst and investor optimism can be broad without being informative.

**The design lesson.** The random benchmark is the right null for any skill claim. Apply it whenever a track record is offered without a matched random baseline: same vintage, same entry multiples, same exit conditions.

**Caution.** Venture returns are illiquid, self-reported and slow to realise. A money-multiple comparison across vintages is noisy. Treat the finding as a prior, not a precise estimate.
