## Raw Concept

What this page answers: where inflation risk is building in 2026 and how to model it. From Capital Flows 2026-08-12.

## Narrative

Model view: inflation = every source of nominal demand vs every source of real output; M2 velocity is "worthless" — real-time card data, savings balances, retailer pricing power are the live signals. State: Core CPI 2.9→2.6 YoY, near 2% by year-end at current deceleration; ~5.8% real-GDP nowcast with fixed investment accelerating; ~40% of S&P returns tied to AI. **Bonds won't bid until AI CapEx ends or its credit collapses.** Factor rotation: steels/metals +43% YTD, ags +16% YTD; short-end inflation swaps Japan→UK show pressure building. Warning stack: falling inventory-to-sales vs rising government spend; real rates at highs but not restrictive enough.

### The nominal-demand-minus-policy-rate spread (2026-11-13)

The November livestream teaser restates the thesis in one number. Nominal demand runs near **6%** against a **4%** policy rate. On that reading the policy rate is below nominal growth, so policy is still accommodative in real terms, and the spread amplifies rather than brakes price pressure.

The teaser also claims the inflation swap market still prices a deceleration the data does not support, that factor flows show equities positioned for AI momentum rather than macro risk, and that more than half of this year's nominal return has been eaten by inflation.

Only the spread is checkable without the paid material. It is a simple monitor: nominal GDP growth minus the policy rate, updated each quarter.

### The sign of the equity reaction depends on the shock (2026-11-22)

A Bank of Italy study, summarised by Klement, finds that the equity reaction to an inflation surprise flipped after 2020. Before 2020, a positive surprise was negative for share prices, and worse for firms with more long-dated debt. Since 2020 it is positive, and better for heavily levered firms. The proposed reason is the shock type: demand-driven surprises can be cooled by a rate hike, supply-driven surprises cannot. A persistent supply shock devalues nominal debt in real terms. If the shock becomes large enough, profits suffer and the sign flips back, as in 2023. ### The hiking-cycle path (2026-11-20 to 2026-11-21)

Capital Flows argues that a hiking cycle is coming, because the Fed must raise rates or let long-end rates blow out and weaken the currency. The observable part is a cross-asset ordering after the Anthropic IPO: ES broke below 7400, VIX rose, FX volatility stayed low, and bond volatility rose only a little. If equities enter a hiking cycle in that position, the author expects a larger decline once rate volatility does rise. [Source: @sources/substack-rss-capital-flows-2026-11-20-anthropic-ai-flows-and-the-coming-hiking-cycle.md] [Source: @sources/substack-rss-capital-flows-2026-11-21-the-ai-repricing-and-what-bonds-are-not-confirmi.md]

The monitorable part is the divergence, not the forecast: equity volatility elevated while rate and FX volatility are not. A widening gap is a candidate alert. The hiking-cycle claim itself is `[TENTATIVE]`.
