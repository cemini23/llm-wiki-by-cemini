## Raw Concept

What this page answers: the Capital Flows (Jaymes) thesis that record US equity valuations make the market *hypersensitive* to macro liquidity — and why that changes how a small macro-liquidity contraction or a yen-carry unwind should be read.

## Narrative

**Valuations are a sensitivity reading, not a sell signal.** High valuations alone don't cause crashes; crashes come from asset-liability mismatches when positioning gets too aggressive. The level tells you how *fragile* the market is, not which direction it breaks.

**Index returns are mostly valuation expansion, not earnings.** Decomposing S&P 500 moves over the last two years (and YTD 2026) shows most came from valuation expansion driven by macro liquidity — the quantity of money in the system and the price of money. That makes the earlier-2026 pullback a *valuation contraction* (a buying opportunity) rather than an earnings event.

**Hypersensitivity.** At record valuations the money base supporting prices is so excessive that a small contraction in macro liquidity — one that would barely register in a normal regime — can now produce a ~10% index move in either direction. This is the operational takeaway: watch the liquidity channel, not earnings beats.

**Tech concentration.** S&P +20% over 6 months with ~11 points from tech alone. NVIDIA pullbacks are market/sector-driven, not fundamental; compute financialization (NVIDIA guaranteeing residual GPU value and owning the market around its own product) deepens the monopoly. Staring at GPU credit risk misses the macro-liquidity question.

**The yen carry is the quantity-of-money channel.** A falling yen funds yen-denominated leverage into US equities; AUD/yen and MXN/yen grinding higher confirm capital moving out the risk curve. On unwind, even a modest yen pop can drive a 10% equity drawdown regardless of CapEx or NVIDIA earnings. Reference: the 2024 unwind took the Nikkei down ~27% peak-to-trough.

**Tail compression.** Cross-asset covariance falling (market shaking off shocks) with tails compressing; VIX rising alongside ES new highs is short covering / cooked hedges — signalling *increasing probability of an extreme move* even as the index melts up.
