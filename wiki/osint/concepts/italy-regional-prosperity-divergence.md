## Raw Concept

When did the north–south economic gap in Italy start, and what does the evidence say about trade as a growth cause?

## Narrative

### The conventional account

The north traded with Europe, the Middle East, and Asia. The south traded less. The usual start date is the rise of Venice as a trading hub in the 1300s.

### The revision

Salvo and Weisdorf (2026) use **church construction** as a prosperity proxy. A community with surplus builds large buildings. They measure churches per square kilometre and floor area per square kilometre across Italy.

Finding: from about the year **1000**, church floor area per unit area grows much faster in the north and centre than in the south and islands. The authors date the divergence about **300 years earlier** than the Venice account. [Source: substack-rss-klement-2026-11-06-the-economic-divide-in-italy.md]

### The two reversals

The northern advantage falls twice, around **1300** and around **1500**. Both drops fit plague arrivals. An open trade economy imports goods and also imports pathogens.

That is the paper's second claim: openness raises both the level of prosperity and the variance. A closed region has less upside and smaller drawdowns.

### The long-run result

The regions that stayed closed to trade grew less for centuries. Over a long horizon the level effect dominates the variance effect.

### Limits of the evidence

- **The proxy is ordinal.** Church construction measures surplus that a community chose to spend on a durable public building. It does not measure output.
- **The proxy has a selection bias.** A wealthy community that spent surplus on walls, roads, or palaces instead of churches looks poor.
- **The sample is one country.** The external validity is limited.

`[TENTATIVE]` for the causal claim. `[CONFIRMED]` that the two papers exist and that the construction divergence is measurable — the chart is in the source.

### Why it matters here

The wiki has a large Italian genealogy section for Atto. That is a different subject. This page is economic history and has no archive or record content. Keep the two separate.

The transferable idea is narrow: **openness raises mean and variance together**. Any claim that a region or a firm should open up more needs both halves stated.
