## Raw Concept

What this page answers: is Swarmer (NASDAQ: SWMR) a speculative long on Erik Prince consolidating Ukraine's combat-proven drone base? Operator desk 9 Oct 2026. Observational. CFTC Rule 4.41. Draft, pending audit.

## Narrative

**Thesis:** the software is real but small. The bet is that Prince turns Swarmer into the US-listed roll-up vehicle for Ukraine's combat-proven drone and robot makers before the war ends and the consolidation window shuts.

Swarmer is software only (Trident OS, MINAS, STYX), founded May 2023 by Serhii Kupriienko and Alex Fink. It went public 16 March 2026 at $5.00, spiked to $83.30, and closed 8 October 2026 at $13.13, a market cap near $218M on $298K of trailing revenue.

Erik Prince became non-executive chairman on 8 December 2025. He holds **zero shares**; his pay is unvested options (943,350 at $6.2711 pre-split, 1,774,725 at $3.3334 post-split). Sixty percent vest on milestones: $10M of revenue he sources, or a $500M market cap for 20 of 30 days. He is paid to consolidate.

The first deal is Ratel Robotics, a Ukrainian combat-UGV maker with **audited FY2025 net sales of $18.6M**. It has $86.6M of customer advances for undelivered UGVs, so the FY2026 earnout ($77M revenue) is a delivery question, not impossible.

The risks, stated first in the note: a revenue round-trip (Swarmer pays a counterparty group $4.9M against $3.9M of consideration, with $2.4M of services whose fair value it could not substantiate, booked as a reduction of revenue); 99 percent customer concentration; heavy dilution (a $146M equity line, a September lock-up expiry of about 59 percent of the float, up to 5.49M more Ratel shares); the company abandoning the vendor-agnostic "Microsoft model" for hardware integration; the stronger competitor Auterion; and Prince holding no stock. The "100,000 combat missions" claim is company-only and loose, and real combat swarms are three to eight drones.

The trade: long, equity only (no listed options, so no LEAPS), sized as a speculative special situation, with the Ratel vote (2 November 2026) and the termination date (7 January 2027) as catalysts.

### Falsifiers

- The Ratel earnouts fail and the deal becomes a dilutive acquisition of a going-concern-challenged business.
- A ceasefire cuts Ukrainian demand.
- The equity line and Ratel shares swamp the float.
- The round-trip counterparty does not pay, or revenue is restated.
- Auterion, or a prime, wins the US and NATO slots first.
- Prince leaves, or the performance milestones lapse.
