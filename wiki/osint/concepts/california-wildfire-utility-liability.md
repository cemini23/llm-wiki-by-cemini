## Raw Concept

The question this page answers: how does California law assign cost for wildfires started by utility equipment, and which findings decide who pays?

## Narrative

California holds its large investor-owned utilities (PG&E, Southern California Edison, San Diego Gas & Electric) financially responsible for wildfires caused by their equipment. Three mechanisms matter.

**1. Inverse condemnation (strict liability).** Under the California Constitution, a utility is liable for property damage from its equipment even without negligence. Fault is not required for the property-damage claim. This is the base layer.

**2. The California Wildfire Fund (AB 1054, 2019).** The state created a fund, backed by utility contributions and a state loan, to pay wildfire claims. A utility must hold a valid **safety certification** to draw on it. The fund lets a utility pay claims without immediate insolvency.

**3. The administrator's finding.** The **Wildfire Fund Administrator** reviews the utility's conduct. Two findings matter:
- **Prudence / imprudence** — did the utility act reasonably? An imprudence finding shifts cost back to the utility (and its shareholders) but is the milder outcome.
- **Willful disregard** — did the utility consciously disregard public safety? A willful-disregard finding can require the utility to **reimburse the fund**. This is the severe outcome.

**De-energization (PSPS).** A public safety power shutoff cuts power to lines during high fire risk. Regulators treat this as a mitigation tool. Failing to consider de-energizing a high-risk line is the conduct that Muddy Waters alleges against SCE for the Eaton Fire (@sources/muddy-waters-edison-international-eix-2026-10-13.md).

**Why it matters for trading.** Utility wildfire names discount a range of outcomes. The gap between **imprudence** and **willful disregard** is often the largest swing factor, and it is decided by an administrative process, not by the market. That makes it a binary legal catalyst with its own timeline.
