## Raw Concept

### Statutory framework

**Chapter 559.952 of the Florida Statutes** establishes the Financial Technology Sandbox, administered by the Florida Office of Financial Regulation (OFR). The program permits fintech innovators to test new products and services in a **supervised, flexible environment** with rule-waivers from specified general laws and rule requirements that would otherwise prevent the product's launch.

The program is part of a broader US state-level trend (Arizona, Wyoming, Utah, others have similar sandboxes) that emerged in the post-2018 wave of state regulatory-innovation legislation, parallel to the UK FCA Sandbox and the Hong Kong SFC Sandbox. Florida's program operates under the OFR's Division of Consumer Finance.

### Application requirements

K7 para 53 enumerates three substantive applicant requirements:

1. **Identify the specific regulations** preventing the product's launch — applicants must articulate which Florida statutory or rule provisions would be barriers absent the sandbox waiver.
2. **Disclose control persons** — full transparency on entity ownership, key officers, decision-makers.
3. **Cap the maximum number of consumers** during the testing phase — the program is a *supervised pilot*, not a license to deploy at scale. The consumer cap negotiates between the OFR and the applicant per program approval.

The sandbox does **not** waive federal-law requirements (SEC, FINRA, CFTC, FTC, etc.) — applicants remain fully subject to federal regulation. The waiver scope is specifically Florida state-law and OFR-rule provisions.

### Cemini-side relevance — P3 / future pivot pathway

K7 frames the sandbox as a "strategic pathway if the firm eventually pivots to retail algorithmic product offerings" (para 53). Under the current Cemini posture (solo-operator proprietary trading on founder capital), the sandbox is **not yet relevant** — the firm has no consumer-facing product, no external advisees, and trades only its own capital, so there are no Florida-state-law barriers to identify in an application.

The sandbox becomes a relevant pathway under three pivot scenarios:

1. **Retail SaaS commercialization** — if Cemini later licenses the algorithmic engine to retail users, Florida state-law and OFR-rule provisions on automated investment advisers, robo-advisers, or consumer financial software may apply. The sandbox could permit a supervised pilot before full state-level registration.
2. **B2B managed-account product** — if Cemini takes on external capital under a managed-account model (≥6 Florida residents triggers state IA registration per the Florida Securities and Investor Protection Act, per K7 para 48), the sandbox could permit a supervised pilot before full IA registration is in place.
3. **Innovative product structure outside existing OFR rule taxonomy** — if Cemini productizes something genuinely novel (e.g., AI-driven prediction-market signal SaaS, novel structured-product offerings), the sandbox provides legal certainty during the period when OFR rule-making catches up.

### Sandbox vs federal IAA Internet Adviser Exemption — pathway comparison

K7 covers two distinct registration pathways for retail SaaS products in different sections; they are mutually-substitutable for some product configurations:

| Pathway | Authority | Use case | Trade-offs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Florida Sandbox (Ch. 559.952)** | Florida OFR | Florida-resident consumer pilot with rule-waivers | State-only; consumer-count capped; supervised pilot only — not production at scale |
| **SEC Internet Adviser Exemption Rule 203A-2(e) (July 2024)** | SEC | Nationwide automated SaaS advisory, no human-directed services, zero non-internet clients | Federal registration with full IAA fiduciary duties + compliance manual; no consumer cap |
| **CFTC Reg 4.14(a)(10) CTA exemption** | CFTC | Commodity/futures/swaps advisory with <15 clients + no public hold-out | Hard 15-client ceiling; no public marketing |

The sandbox is a **state-level pilot pathway** for product validation; the federal exemptions are **production-scale operating regimes** once the product is past validation. They sequence rather than compete: sandbox-as-pilot → federal exemption-as-production.

### Decision rule: when sandbox becomes actionable

The sandbox is a P3 ticket. It becomes a P2 pathway-evaluation workstream under any of:

- Cemini decides to commercialize the algorithmic engine as a retail SaaS / B2C product
- Cemini contemplates accepting external managed-capital from Florida residents
- A novel product structure surfaces during IP-sale negotiations that needs Florida-side regulatory clarity

Under the current proprietary-trading-only posture (which K7 + K5 + K6 all corroborate as the operational regime through the IP-sale event), the sandbox is **not actionable**. Documented here for completeness and for the future pivot-path optionality.
