## Raw Concept

What this page answers: the regulatory state of US event-contract markets in Aug 2026 — CFTC vs NY state-gaming-law fight, the false wildfire-betting narrative, and venue volume trends. Synthesized from Event Horizon 2026-08-12.

## Narrative

**Wildfire betting is a narrative without a market.** Senators' letters and local-news panic claim prediction markets offer wildfire contracts; no exchange currently does. Kalshi says it hasn't and won't; Polymarket's international site doesn't. Polymarket offered some in 2025. The lesson cuts both ways: media inflated a non-existent market *and* the episode shows venues can list almost anything if they choose to.

**Kalshi × NY AG is the structural story.** NY AG Letitia James filed July 31 in state court seeking a nationwide TRO on *all* Kalshi event contracts plus >$36B in damages. **CFTC responded with emergency authority**, ordering KalshiEX to keep operating under CEA Core Principles — Chairman Selig: NY wants event-contract derivatives to "waste away under its iron curtain of state gaming laws." CFTC is directing Kalshi to continue serving NY even against a court order. This is a live federal-vs-state preemption fight with direct operational risk for any Kalshi-based bot (pm-kalshi lane).

**Venue data.** Kalshi volume Aug 10: $956M (sports+parlays 70%, crypto 27%); Kalshi tops $4B annualized revenue seeking $40B valuation; Polymarket's World Cup comedown −56% weekly volume from June high, Kalshi −25%. FlightAware v Kalshi voluntarily dismissed after Kalshi changed flight-market data usage.

**For pm-bot ops:** NY-state exposure is a legal tail risk on the Kalshi lane; monitor the CFTC emergency-authority litigation. Seasonal demand lull (post-World Cup) lowers venue liquidity into the fall.

**K239 mention markets + WA.** Federal review of mention markets (NPR unnamed sources); Kalshi delisted sports mentions ahead of NFL. King County final order forces a WA geofence (Aug 19 IP+residency / Sep 2 multi-source) covering sports, elections, politics, entertainment, culture, tech/science, and mentions. Advertising those products in WA banned. Do not treat mention markets as a live Kalshi bot surface. [Source: Event Horizon 2026-08-14]

**K236 — responsible trading + incentive scrutiny (2026-08-13).** Three regulatory threads converged:

1. **Novig's responsible-trading framework** (Chapter 14 of the Ludlow Exchange rulebook) — a 21+ nationwide age standard (vs the 18+ industry norm at Polymarket/Kalshi), member deposit/loss/exposure limits, cooling-off + self-exclusion, responsible-marketing bans, trading-incentive restrictions, and risk-based monitoring, all codified as exchange-side obligations rather than optional customer tools. A benchmark for what responsible-participation rules look like when embedded in rulebooks.
2. **CFTC DMO advisory on incentive programs** (Reg 40.5/40.6 self-certifications) — explicitly flags payments exceeding what's reasonably necessary, unlimited rebates / "risk-free" trades / market-maker stipends (Rule 1.56 guarantee prohibition), hidden one-off/preferential arrangements, secret discount codes, non-cash prizes, VIP/early access without disclosure, and chance-based sweepstakes rewards (Core Principle 2). Directly relevant to Kalshi's chance-based bonus offers and to any market-maker/LP incentive program on the retail lane.
3. **NYC Council investigation** (Speaker Julie Menin) into deceptive/predatory marketing — letters to Kalshi, Polymarket, Coinbase, Gemini Titan; influencer-marketing and fabricated-wager concerns; subpoena power. **Baltimore** separately sued Kalshi + Polymarket for unlicensed sports betting + consumer-protection violations. Kalshi volume Aug 11: $1.27B (sports+parlays 78.6%, crypto 18%); raise talk at a $40B valuation (Sequoia/Wellington); Kalshi referred 32 insider-trading cases to the CFTC.

Net for pm-bot ops: retail-facing incentive/marketing programs are now a regulatory target — an awareness item for the Kalshi lane, not a code change. The responsible-trading framing (21+, loss-chasing detection) is also the plausible direction future federal oversight takes.

**K242 — onshore/geofence, not offshore (2026-08-19).** Event Horizon argues the refrain “if you ban prediction markets the action goes offshore” overstates elasticity. Sports-betting legalization created **new** activity rather than only moving existing offshore books onshore. If US event contracts vanished, payments friction, app-store absence, ad bans, crypto-only UX, and **CFTC tools against US-serving offshore venues** (Polymarket 2022) would leave a large share of volume **extinct, not relocated**. Kalshi’s legal product scaled even though Polymarket international already existed. Comp: post-UIGEA poker players often stopped instead of going offshore. Ops: **Kalshi WA geofence is 2026-08-19**. Mentions markets remain a tiny volume share under CFTC review. No pm-bot scp from this steal — awareness only. [TENTATIVE] single-author argument, not a measured demand elasticity. **K283 — Ohio cease-and-desist ×10, CFTC perpetuals no-action (2026-10-06).** Two hard regulatory events. (1) **Ohio** sent cease-and-desist letters to **10 prediction platforms** — Coinbase, Gemini Titan, MooMoo Financial, Novig, Plus500US, Polymarket, Prophet X, Robinhood Derivatives, Underdog Predict, WeBull Financial — giving 14 days to respond and shutter, one week after the **Sixth Circuit** let Ohio enforce its gambling rules against Kalshi. The Ohio letter to Polymarket explicitly calls its event contracts "online sports gaming." (2) The **CFTC** Division of Market Oversight issued a **no-action letter** letting DCMs convert perpetual-*style* broad-based security index futures into **true perpetuals** (remove expiry), subject to customer-protection conditions; the position **expires 2026-10-20**. Kalshi separately won approval for **S&P 500 perpetual futures**. Also: a House bill would bar federal candidates from trading markets on their own elections; Kalshi argues in the Second Circuit that the CEA pre-empted state law in a 1981 "Potato War" case. Kalshi set a record **$3.68 B** Sunday volume, ~76% of the weekend's $9.37 B across venues. **Awareness only — no pm scp.** [Source: @sources/substack-rss-event-horizon-2026-10-06-religion-prediction-markets.md]

**K284 — CFTC proposes CTX/CAM; Connecticut withdrawals (2026-10-07).** After the Senate failed to advance the CLARITY Act, the **CFTC proposed Regulation Crypto Asset Transactions (CTX)** and **Regulation Crypto Asset Markets (CAM)** via an **ANPRM** (comments due within 60 days). Registration is **optional** — the CFTC cannot compel it without Congress — but registered venues may offer **retail margin/leverage**, which state-licensed venues cannot. Separately, **Connecticut** saw **ProphetX, Gemini, and Webull withdraw**; the DCP is in contact with six more (Polymarket, Coinbase, Crypto.com, Robinhood, Novig, Underdog). Also: **Kalshi US 500 index perpetual futures** launched; **Kalshi offered to cap its affiliate desk at 5% of quarterly volume** during the CFTC conflict-of-interest comment period; **Polymarket Protocol V2** (smart-contract rebuild); **EDGE Markets** announced **agentic capital allocation** — pre-authorised agents with capital limits. **Awareness only — no pm scp.** [Source: @sources/substack-rss-event-horizon-2026-10-07-kalshi-election-push.md]

**K285 — 39 states file at SCOTUS; Michigan halts Coinbase (2026-10-08).** **39 states filed an amicus brief** backing New Jersey's SCOTUS petition to rule that federal law **does not preempt state gambling law** for sports event contracts. **The NFL also sided with New Jersey.** The states' argument: markets cannot strip states of sovereign power through **"relabeling and window-dressing"**; certiorari grounds are state sovereignty, the **major-questions doctrine**, citizen protection, and a **circuit split**. **Ten states did not join** (TX, FL largest). **IAGR/NAGRA** filed too, on **IGRA / Class III** tribal-gaming grounds. Circuit picture: the **Third Circuit** ruled for Kalshi vs New Jersey, but Kalshi **lost in the Ninth and Sixth**. Separately, **Coinbase agreed to halt sports event contracts in Michigan by 2026-10-10** (after Robinhood Derivatives and a KalshiEX injunction), and **Kalshi launched tribal prediction apps**, dividing Indian country. **Awareness only — no pm scp.** [Source: @sources/substack-rss-event-horizon-2026-10-08-39-states-scotus-brief.md]

**K289 — the CFTC carves out casinos and claims event contracts (2026-10-12).** Two moves the same Friday. **(1) An Interim Final Rule** codifies that **casino-style gambling products — sportsbook wagers and casino games — are excluded from the "swap" definition.** Selig: "Casino-style gambling products are not derivatives." The purpose is to blunt the argument that if sports event contracts are swaps, ordinary sports bets are too (which would pull state sportsbooks under CFTC jurisdiction). **(2) A Notice of Proposed Rulemaking** would expressly **include event contracts — sports, politics, culture, weather — in the definition of "swap"**, and asserts the CFTC's **exclusive jurisdiction**. **Read together it is a preemption play for the courts.** **Kalshi filed for a Sixth Circuit rehearing on Oct 9, the same day**, citing the new rules. The SCOTUS briefs grew to include the **NFL, 145 tribes, 39 states, Christopher Dodd, and Gary Gensler**. Also: **Kalshi "protected" NFL player markets** (early-exit payout at the pre-kickoff fair price; Yes only, pre-game only, reduces payout), **Polymarket 50-leg parlays**, and **Verifact** — markets on **disputed facts about the past**. **Awareness only — no pm scp.** [Source: @sources/substack-rss-event-horizon-2026-10-12-ep36-verifact.md]
