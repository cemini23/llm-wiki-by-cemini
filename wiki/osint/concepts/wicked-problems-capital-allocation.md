## Raw Concept

What prompted this page: K41 ingest of Massif Capital's Q4 2025 Letter to Investors — the central thesis is that post-GFC assumptions are now invalidated, geopolitics has become a primary value determinant, and capital allocation needs to be re-grounded for a structurally different regime.

## Narrative

### Core claim

Three post-GFC assumptions that drove a 15-year asset-allocation playbook are now invalidated:

1. **Inflation is a tail risk, not the base case** — invalidated: structural inflation is back (deglobalization + onshoring + energy bottlenecks)
2. **Real rates stay low / negative** — invalidated: real rates positive, capital-cost regime shift
3. **Geopolitics is a discount factor not a price driver** — invalidated: geopolitics now determines which assets clear at what price (sanctions, supply chains, critical-mineral access)

Massif frames the resulting investment problem as "wicked" in the technical sense: problems with no clean specification, no stable solution, and answers that themselves change the problem.

### Assumption-triage discipline

The letter's operational core (not just narrative) is an **assumption-triage** practice:

1. List every load-bearing assumption underneath a position
2. For each, ask: is this assumption from the post-GFC playbook? Is it still valid? Under what regime does it break?
3. Decompose into (a) assumptions you can verify directly, (b) assumptions you must accept on faith, (c) assumptions that need monitoring
4. Re-do this for every position quarterly

### Why "wicked"

A "wicked problem" (Rittel & Webber 1973) has:
- No stopping rule
- Solutions are not true-or-false but better-or-worse
- Every implemented solution is a one-shot — no trial and error at production scale
- Every wicked problem is essentially unique
- The problem can be considered a symptom of another problem

Massif's claim: capital allocation in a regime-change environment has all of these properties.

### Cemini posture

- **Generalization target**: this is operationally the **same discipline** as Cemini's Phase-0 audit pattern (clone → verify license → verify maturity → audit failure mode → decide). Massif applies it to investment positions; Cemini applies it to tool adoption.
- **Portable to Cemini brief discipline**: every brief should have a "load-bearing assumption" section equivalent to Massif's triage; could become a brief-template addition
- **Cross-reference candidate**: connects to @concepts/regime-conditioned-evaluation.md (which generalizes the same idea for evaluation pipelines)
- **Track-record cred**: Massif's 28-quarter track record (15.6% net annualized inception) is the **outcome variable** validating the framework, not the framework itself. Useful as evidence the discipline doesn't kill returns.
