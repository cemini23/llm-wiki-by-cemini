## Raw Concept

What the CFTC's November 2026 interpretation of Regulation 38.151(b) requires, and why it puts operators between federal and state authority.

## Narrative

### The rule

Regulation 38.151(b) requires a designated contract market (DCM) to give competitive, open, fair, and impartial access to its members and to persons with trading privileges.

The CFTC issued an interpretation on 2026-11-05. Its position is that **geography is not an impartial basis for exclusion**. Chairman Michael S. Selig: geographic access restrictions are "inconsistent with the purposes" of the Commodity Exchange Act and with the impartial access requirement. [Source: substack-rss-event-horizon-2026-11-06-cftc-to-prediction-markets-stop-blocking-states.md]

### The two 30-day duties

Within 30 days, a DCM must:

1. Cease domestic geographic restrictions that violate the rule, or file an explanation with the Division of Market Oversight for why compliance is not feasible.
2. Ensure that intermediaries accessing its market do not impede access, or file the same kind of explanation.

The second duty is what pulls in brokers. FanDuel and Robinhood reach Kalshi as intermediaries. [Source: substack-rss-event-horizon-2026-11-06-cftc-to-prediction-markets-stop-blocking-states.md]

### The unresolved parts

`[OPEN QUESTION 2026-11-10]` — three points are not settled by the text.

- **Is a state court order an acceptable explanation?** Kalshi holds orders in Nevada, Michigan, and Washington. A federal judge also enjoined Kalshi sports contracts on two California tribal land areas in the same week.
- **What is the enforcement path?** A footnote cites the CEA injunctive authority: the CFTC may sue anyone "restraining trading in any commodity for future delivery or any swap". Litigation is the likely route.
- **Does compliance override a court order?** The interpretation does not say.

### The operator dilemma

The guidance puts every operator in the same position. Comply with the CFTC and risk state liability. Obey the state court and risk federal action. There is no reading of the text that removes the conflict.

### Reading

A plausible intent is **litigation support**. A national-access interpretation strengthens the federal argument that state regulation of event contracts is preempted. The CNIGA response reads it the same way and calls it an attempt to undermine court orders and tribal sovereignty. [Source: substack-rss-event-horizon-2026-11-09-episode-40-the-misery-index-with-adjacent-market.md]

### What to watch

1. The first DCM explanation filing. Its content sets the effective standard.
2. Any CFTC suit against a non-compliant operator.
3. Whether a circuit split appears between states with injunctions and states without.

## Dead Ends

- **Treating the interpretation as a final rule.** It is agency guidance. Courts decide preemption.
- **Reading "30 days" as a compliance deadline with a fixed penalty.** The penalty path is unstated litigation.
