## Raw Concept

What this page answers: the macro/cultural thesis that the US economy now rewards ownership over effort, and how that reshapes work, speculation, and politics. Synthesized from Kyla Scanlon's "How to Get Rich in America."

## Narrative

**The shift is structural, not cyclical.** Labor's share of gross domestic income fell from ~58% (1980) to ~51%; corporate profits' share rose 7% → 11.7%. The tax code migrated the same way: corporate income tax fell from >1/5 of federal revenue to <1/10 while the payroll tax (tax on work) grew to the second-largest revenue source. On a multi-trillion-dollar economy that's roughly $8k–$12k/year per worker.

**Wealth builds more wealth.** 42% of top-1% nonwage income is pure capital gains. Median household net worth roughly doubled in real terms since the mid-1990s while incomes grew ~30%; climbing from the bottom quarter to the top quarter of the wealth distribution went from ~20 years to ~40. This is why "get rich" increasingly means *nonwage income* — stocks, S-corp pass-through, home appreciation — not salary.

**Culture follows the payoff structure.** Passive-income demand is rational given the mechanics: >half of Americans (60% of Gen Z adults) say a full-time job won't meet their goals; 1 in 4 have a side hustle. The grifter layer (passive-income courses) sits on top of a real wealth-begets-wealth dynamic.

**The AI wealth effect is the newest installment.** AI enriches asset holders while threatening labor displacement (WEF: 92M jobs by 2030); consumption + state data-center revenue + AI-capex-order-book names (Caterpillar/Cummins trading like tech) all tie to it. Data-center construction ~$70B annualized in June while homes/hospitals outlays fell $100B. The backlash is legible: another thing promising growth that's plopped down to enrich a few.

**Demographics cement it.** 55+ hold 74% of US wealth (vs ~50% in 1989); under-40 fell 11% → 6.6%. Retirees drive >quarter of consumer spending. Fernández-Villaverde: an aging society's first-order problem is redistributing worker→retiree income through taxes (visible) or capital income (invisible, 401k-driven). This recasts "anger at corporate profits" as partly a worker-retiree conflict.

**Prospect theory at generational scale.** 80% Gen Z / 75% millennial are drawn to speculative investments because they feel financially behind; sports betting functions as a financial strategy for young traders; gambling and investing blur inside the same apps (Robinhood: IRA + event contracts). When a "normal life" is out of reach, lottery-like bets look rational. South Korea is the extreme case (~110M active retail accounts, home ownership out of reach).

**Illegibility and withdrawal.** Kludgeocracy + time tax + shadow work make systems uninspectable; Fed dropping forward guidance (Warsh) adds opacity. ANES: 2024 plurality is the "angry voter" (votes while believing it changes nothing); under-35 resigned voters hit 23%+. People don't stop caring — they optimize to the world as it exists.
