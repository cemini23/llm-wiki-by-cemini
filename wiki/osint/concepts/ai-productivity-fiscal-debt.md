## Raw Concept

**The question this page answers:** how much of the US long-end yield is an implicit bet on AI-driven productivity?

## Narrative

The transmission chain is mechanical and undisputed: **AI productivity → GDP growth → higher tax revenue + lower welfare spending → smaller primary deficit → slower debt growth → (in the strong case) falling debt-to-GDP**. The magnitude is what is contested.

### The KfW / Bales arithmetic

| AI productivity boost | US primary deficit 2056 | US debt-to-GDP 2056 |
|-----------------------|-------------------------|---------------------|
| 0.0% (baseline) | 2.8% | **185%** |
| 0.2% per year | 1.5% | **143%** |
| 0.4% per year | ≈0 (eliminated) | **113%** |

Lower deficits also mean **less interest expense** and, the study argues, **lower bond yields** — a compounding effect.

### The range of disagreement is enormous

- **Acemoglu / CBO camp (~0.1%/yr)** — Klement's own position.
- **Goldman Sachs** — considerably higher.
- The KfW scenarios use **0.2% and 0.4%**.

### The timing trap

Bales' historical point: **steam, electricity and the computer each had years to decades of hype with near-zero measured productivity growth** before the killer application appeared. If AI follows that shape, **the debt-improving payoff arrives decades out**, not in the current forecast window — and the market cannot price a payoff it cannot date.

### The falsifiable claim worth tracking

Klement states it plainly: **buying 30-year US Treasuries today is an implicit large long bet on AI-driven productivity and GDP growth.** The counterfactual is explicit — **if AI productivity disappoints, Treasury yields drift higher** on the ever-rising debt burden.

This is a **dated, numeric macro stance** with a clear disconfirming signal (long-end yields). Candidate **GuruWatcher macro watch** — **alert only; no order, no auto-arm**.
