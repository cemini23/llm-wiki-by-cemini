## Raw Concept

Question this page answers: which Asian jurisdictions are moving on tokenised instruments and exchange access, and in which direction?

From two Wu Blockchain issues (2026-11-15, 2026-11-16).

## Narrative

Three distinct postures, often confused in headlines.

China separates technology from assets. Shanghai encourages blockchain and privacy-preserving computation for scientific-data sharing under its Trial Measures, and states that this does not cover crypto trading or issuance. The CAC simultaneously bars "virtual currency", "exchange" and "ICO" from blockchain filing names. Reading the encouragement as a crypto signal is a category error.

Kazakhstan builds regulated plumbing. A tokenised tranche of 91-day central-bank bills priced at a 15.72% weighted average yield with 1.4x subscription, on a KASE platform reportedly using Solana and BitGo custody, with tokenised government bonds planned from Q1 2027.

Russia opens a dated window. Exchanges may trade crypto from 22 November 2026 once rules are agreed with the regulator, with participant registration, trade monitoring and price calculation required.

Thailand tests a consumer use case. A tourism payment app converts stablecoins to baht through PromptPay inside an SEC sandbox.

The pattern: tokenisation of sovereign and institutional instruments is progressing inside licensed venues, while retail crypto access moves at a different and slower speed.

### Operator voice — CZ in Hong Kong, August 2026

A separate transcript adds the operator view rather than the regulator view. Changpeng Zhao, speaking in Hong Kong, ranks three jurisdictions for Web3: Hong Kong first, then the UAE, then the US, and says the US policy environment has become more supportive. He is explicit that Hong Kong's advantage is finance, not AI, because power and data-centre costs are uncompetitive. [Source: substack-rss-wublock-2026-11-26-cz-qa-bnbs-rocky-debut-startup-lessons-and-web3s.md]

He also argues that a DEX can separate an international platform from a US one and block restricted jurisdictions instead of requiring KYC from every user. That is the position of a former defendant, not a rule. `[TENTATIVE]`

The useful part is the flow direction: tokenised real-world assets, including stablecoins, are the category operators now expect to grow fastest, and the stated driver is account access rather than yield. The dollar stablecoin market is put at roughly $200bn to $300bn, undated.
