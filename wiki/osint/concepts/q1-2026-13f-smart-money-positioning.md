## Raw Concept

The question: across nine notable managers' Q1 2026 13F filings, where do they *agree*, where do they *diverge*, and what positioning themes emerge? Built directly from `@sources/13f-q1-2026-hedge-fund-holdings.md`.

**Caveat — read first.** 13F data is long US-listed equity only, as-of 2026-03-31, disclosed ~2026-05-15 — six weeks stale and silent on shorts, cash, bonds, non-US holdings, and derivatives. Patterns below are *positioning signals*, not trade recommendations, and not a complete view of any fund's risk.

## Narrative

### 1. Alphabet — the quarter's consensus accumulation

Five of the nine funds are long Alphabet, and the *direction of travel* is the story:

- **Berkshire** opened/expanded GOOGL **+204%** (now 5.9%) — notable as Buffett historically avoided big tech beyond Apple.
- **Tiger Global** holds GOOGL as its **single largest position** (13.4%).
- **TCI** added GOOG **+17%** and bought GOOGL.
- **Baupost** added GOOG **+9%** (6.6%).
- **Appaloosa** holds GOOG at 8.4% (a minor −3% trim).

The lone dissenter is **Pershing Square**, which cut both share classes ~95% — a near-complete exit. Read with care: some of the cross-fund GOOG-vs-GOOGL split is share-class housekeeping, not conviction change. Net: Alphabet was the most broadly *accumulated* mega-cap in this batch.

### 2. Microsoft — the sharpest divergence

MSFT splits the group cleanly:

- **Pershing Square** initiated a **new ~15% position** ("Buy").
- **Tiger Global** cut it **−54%**, **TCI −84%**, **Appaloosa −82%** (the latter two to residual stubs).

One high-conviction new buyer against three managers slashing it — the clearest disagreement in the batch.

### 3. Amazon — the broadest high-conviction long

AMZN is a top holding for four funds and was *added* by three:

- Pershing Square 17.4% (+19%), Appaloosa 15.2% (**+98%**), Baupost 12.7% (+47%), Tiger Global 9.1% (flat).

No fund in the batch was selling Amazon. It is the closest thing to a consensus long.

### 4. China-tech de-risking

**Appaloosa** — the batch's long-standing China bull — cut its China book hard: BABA −33%, PDD −49%, JD −69%, KWEB −77% (BIDU the lone small add). No other fund here carries meaningful China-tech. The signal: the most committed China-tech manager materially reduced exposure in Q1.

### 5. AI-electricity / power

Independent of the China cut, **Appaloosa** grew power generation: Vistra (VST) **+114%**, NRG +6%. Tiger Global holds GE Vernova (GEV, 3.7%). The "AI demand → electricity" theme shows up as accumulation in independent power producers while traditional oil is trimmed (Berkshire CVX −35%).

### 6. Managed care — contested

UnitedHealth was cut by **Tiger Global** (−17%) and **Appaloosa** (−55%). Meanwhile value buyers sit in the *rest* of managed care: Baupost holds Elevance (ELV, 7.3%) and Molina (MOH); Greenlight holds Centene (CNC, +3.5%) and Cigna (CI). The group is trimming the sector leader while value funds hold the cheaper names.

## Dead Ends

- A true "consensus buy list" cannot be built from nine funds — the sample is small and style-skewed (value-heavy). Treat overlaps as *interesting*, not statistically meaningful.
- Third Point / Daniel Loeb was intended for this batch but not supplied (see source page); any "smart-money consensus" here is missing that data point.
