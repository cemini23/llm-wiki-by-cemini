## Raw Concept

What does a recurring meeting cost? Klement cites a survey of more than 9,000 US workers that prices meeting attendance by industry. This page records the estimate and the calculation method.

Source: @sources/substack-rss-klement-2026-11-13-meetings-are-a-waste-of-money.md.

## Narrative

### The estimate

| Sector | Share of wage bill paid to people in meetings |
|---|---|
| Finance and IT | about 20% |
| Retail | about 10% |

The gap has two causes. Managers attend more meetings than non-managers. Finance and IT pay more per hour than retail.

### The survey findings

- Managers spend far more time in meetings than non-managers.
- Post-pandemic, more meetings are online than in person.
- Men, older workers and college graduates spend more time in meetings. Klement reads this as the manager gap showing through.
- Finance and IT hold more meetings per week than public administration.

### The calculation

A meeting cost is a product:

```
cost = attendee_count × loaded_hourly_wage × duration_hours
```

The useful part is the word "loaded". Salary alone understates the cost by the benefits and overhead loading, which is where the 20% figure comes from.

### Why it is filed here

The method applies to any recurring operator ritual. The workspace runs several: the federation morning summary, the daily research digest, and the per-domain sweep cadence. Each has a computable cost.

The rule that follows is simple: if a recurring ritual cannot be shown to change a decision, its cost is pure loss.
