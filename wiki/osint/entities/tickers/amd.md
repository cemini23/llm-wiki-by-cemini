## Advanced Micro Devices, Inc. (AMD): Comprehensive Equity Research Report

### 1. Business Overview

Advanced Micro Devices, Inc. (AMD) operates as a premier global semiconductor enterprise, specializing in the design, development, and commercialization of high-performance computing, graphics, and visualization technologies. The company has undergone a profound structural and strategic transformation over the past decade, evolving from a secondary, often deeply discounted supplier in the personal computer processor market into a dominant, dual-source powerhouse within the enterprise data center and artificial intelligence (AI) accelerator ecosystems.1 The fundamental operating model of the enterprise is strictly fabless; AMD engineers its silicon architectures and intellectual property (IP), but relies entirely on third-party foundries—predominantly Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSMC)—for the physical manufacturing of its advanced process nodes.3 This deliberate approach minimizes the immense capital expenditures associated with maintaining proprietary fabrication facilities, allowing the company to direct massive resource allocation toward research and development, software ecosystem expansion, and system-level engineering.

At the onset of fiscal year 2025, AMD restructured its financial reporting segments to more accurately align with its end-market strategies and operational management, merging the previously distinct Client and Gaming divisions into a single reportable segment.4 Consequently, the business is now evaluated and managed across three primary pillars: Data Center, Client and Gaming, and Embedded.6 For the fiscal year ended December 27, 2025, AMD generated a record $34.64 billion in total net revenue, representing a 34% year-over-year increase from the $25.79 billion recorded in 2024.4 This revenue mix underscores a definitive structural shift toward enterprise and hyperscaler infrastructure.

The Data Center segment has emerged as the primary engine of both margin expansion and top-line growth, accounting for 48.0% of total revenue in 2025. Generating a record $16.64 billion in 2025 (up 32% year-over-year), this segment encompasses EPYC server central processing units (CPUs), Instinct accelerators (including the MI300 and MI350 series GPUs), data processing units (DPUs), and adaptive System-on-Chips (SoCs).6 The extraordinary growth within this division is directly attributable to hyperscaler AI capital expenditures, sovereign AI infrastructure investments, and widespread cloud enterprise adoption.9

The newly combined Client and Gaming segment represented 42.1% of total revenue, generating $14.6 billion in 2025 (up 51% year-over-year).8 This segment includes Ryzen desktop and notebook processors, Radeon discrete graphics processing units (GPUs), and semi-custom SoCs utilized in premier gaming consoles such as the Sony PlayStation and Microsoft Xbox.4 The Client sub-segment alone accounted for a record $10.6 billion, driven by robust architectural adoption of the "Zen 5" platform and the burgeoning hardware upgrade cycle catalyzed by AI-enabled personal computers. Meanwhile, the Gaming sub-segment provided $3.9 billion, a figure that is currently heavily influenced by the mature lifecycle dynamics of the current console generation.8

The Embedded segment contributed 10.1% of total revenue, generating $3.5 billion in 2025 (a marginal decrease of 3% year-over-year).4 This segment comprises Field Programmable Gate Arrays (FPGAs) and adaptive computing products, which were largely integrated into the portfolio following the transformational 2022 acquisition of Xilinx. While inventory normalization in the industrial and automotive end markets has created near-term revenue headwinds, the segment remains highly profitable and is strategically critical for edge AI deployment, aerospace, defense, and high-speed networking.12

From a geographic exposure standpoint, AMD maintains a highly globalized revenue base. In 2025, international sales represented 67% of the company's total net revenue, a slight increase from 66% in 2024.14 A geographical breakdown of the 2025 revenue highlights the strategic importance of both domestic and Asia-Pacific markets: the United States accounted for $11.36 billion, China (including Hong Kong) contributed $7.75 billion, Taiwan represented $5.18 billion, and Singapore accounted for $4.28 billion.5 Notably, the company reported that no single customer accounted for more than 10% of consolidated net revenue in fiscal years 2025 and 2024, a significant diversification improvement from 2023, when a single Client and Gaming segment customer represented 18% of total revenue.5 However, qualitative assessments and capital expenditure data strongly imply that massive hyperscalers—specifically Microsoft and Meta Platforms—represent a substantial, highly concentrated portion of the forward-looking Data Center pipeline.8

The economic engine and profitability mechanics of AMD rely fundamentally on a heterogeneous compute architecture, colloquially known in the industry as the "chiplet" model. By separating what would traditionally be large, monolithic semiconductor dies into smaller, functionally distinct chiplets connected via advanced packaging technologies (such as TSMC's Chip-on-Wafer-on-Substrate, or CoWoS), AMD drastically improves semiconductor manufacturing yields and exponentially lowers costs compared to monolithic designs. This architectural superiority directly feeds into the company's margin expansion capabilities. The margin profile is highly sensitive to product mix; the Data Center segment commands significantly higher average selling prices (ASPs) and gross margins than the semi-custom console business within the Client and Gaming segment. As the high-margin Instinct MI350 AI accelerators and high-core-count EPYC CPUs comprise an increasingly dominant percentage of the overall sales mix, the underlying gross margin floor of the enterprise naturally elevates, a dynamic that is central to the company's long-term profitability thesis.

### 2. Competitive Landscape

The global semiconductor sector is characterized by intense capital requirements, rapid technological obsolescence, complex geopolitical supply chains, and deeply entrenched software ecosystem moats. AMD operates in a duopoly or highly concentrated oligopoly across almost all of its target end markets, primarily contesting Intel Corporation in the legacy x86 CPU space and Nvidia Corporation in the discrete GPU and AI accelerator markets.

According to market intelligence from Mercury Research covering the fourth quarter of 2025, AMD has achieved extraordinary and sustained market share gains, largely driven by structural supply constraints, manufacturing execution missteps, and architectural stagnation from its primary rival, Intel.16 In the desktop CPU market, AMD captured 36.4% of the unit share and an impressive 42.6% of the revenue share.17 In the highly lucrative server CPU market, AMD secured 28.8% of the unit share and a record 41.3% of the revenue share.17 In the mobile and laptop CPU segment, AMD held 26.0% unit share and 24.9% revenue share.16 Overall, across the total x86 CPU landscape, AMD reached a record 29.2% overall unit share and 35.4% revenue share.17

The structural disparity between unit share and revenue share is a critical second-order insight for investors. AMD's significantly higher revenue share relative to its physical unit volume in the server and desktop markets indicates immense pricing power and superior ASPs. The enterprise and hyperscaler markets are clearly willing to pay a premium for the superior power efficiency, thermal dynamics, and core density of the EPYC processor line. This dynamic has effectively dismantled the historical, decade-long narrative of AMD serving merely as a low-cost, budget alternative to Intel, cementing the company as the premium performance leader in the data center CPU space.

AMD’s most durable structural moat stems from its symbiotic partnership with TSMC and its absolute mastery of chiplet packaging architectures. By exclusively utilizing TSMC’s most advanced fabrication nodes (such as the 3nm and 4nm processes), AMD ensures hardware performance parity—and often superiority—relative to vertically integrated competitors that struggle with internal foundry yields.19 Furthermore, the unique combination of CPU (Zen architecture), GPU (CDNA and RDNA architectures), and FPGA (Xilinx) intellectual property allows AMD to offer heterogeneous, customized rack-scale compute solutions that massive hyperscalers increasingly demand to optimize total cost of ownership (TCO) and power efficiency.19

Conversely, the most fiercely contested moat lies at the software layer of the AI ecosystem. Nvidia’s Compute Unified Device Architecture (CUDA) is a deeply entrenched, proprietary programming ecosystem that has served as the industry standard for parallel computing and machine learning for over a decade. CUDA represents a massive switching cost and a formidable barrier to entry for AI developers. AMD’s open-source alternative, the Radeon Open Compute platform (ROCm), has historically lagged significantly in both performance optimization and developer stability.20

However, recent analysis indicates that the ROCm software ecosystem is rapidly and aggressively closing this historical gap. Throughout late 2025 and early 2026, ROCm reached full production-ready status for major AI workloads, specifically integrating seamlessly with PyTorch and vLLM frameworks.21 While CUDA still reportedly outperforms ROCm by 10% to 30% in highly specialized, general compute tasks, ROCm’s cost-to-performance ratio has become highly compelling for enterprise clients.22 For memory-bound Large Language Model (LLM) inference tasks—which constitute the bulk of the economic value in generative AI deployment—the performance gap is now negligible, allowing hyperscalers to deploy AMD MI350X hardware at a 15% to 40% lower capital cost compared to competing Nvidia hardware.22 The strategic $665 million all-cash acquisition of Silo AI—Europe’s largest private AI laboratory—completed in August 2024, further accelerates the optimization of open-source LLMs specifically tailored for AMD silicon, bridging the software deficit with an influx of over 300 world-class AI scientists and engineers.23

Beyond direct competition from Nvidia and Intel, AMD faces emerging threats from adjacent industries, disruptive new entrants, and fundamental technology shifts. First, massive hyperscalers—such as Amazon Web Services (AWS) with its Inferentia and Trainium chips, Google with its Tensor Processing Units (TPUs), and Meta with its proprietary MTIA chips—are increasingly designing and deploying proprietary custom silicon.26 While AMD currently partners heavily with these firms, the long-term threat of hyperscalers substituting merchant merchant silicon with internal Application-Specific Integrated Circuits (ASICs) poses a latent risk to terminal growth rates in the data center. Second, startups such as Cerebras, MatX, and Groq are pioneering novel semiconductor architectures that embed massive amounts of Static Random-Access Memory (SRAM) directly on the processing die.27 These SRAM-first spatial architectures circumvent the high-bandwidth memory (HBM) bottlenecks inherent in traditional GPU designs. If the broader market for AI inference workloads pivots heavily toward these spatial architectures, the traditional HBM-dependent GPU moat upon which AMD is currently building its AI franchise could be structurally impaired. Third, the proliferation of ARM-based CPUs in the data center poses an ongoing threat to the historical dominance of the x86 instruction set held collectively by AMD and Intel.

Despite these competitive pressures, a powerful macroeconomic and technological counter-trend has emerged in early 2026: the rise of "Agentic AI." As artificial intelligence models transition from simple, prompt-response generative chatbots into autonomous agents capable of executing multi-step, state-dependent logic and interacting dynamically with external environments, the strategic importance of traditional CPUs in the data center is being aggressively re-rated.28 CPUs are intrinsically superior for the dynamic interaction, sequential processing, and complex task management required by AI agents. This shift is currently catalyzing a massive surge in demand for AMD's high-density EPYC server CPUs, as hyperscalers build "greenfield" CPU-heavy data centers to complement their dense GPU deployments.19

#### Capital Allocation Strategy

AMD’s capital allocation framework is heavily weighted toward strategic, bolt-on acquisitions and shareholder returns via share repurchases. The company currently does not issue a dividend and has explicitly stated it expects not to pay dividends in the foreseeable future, preferring to reinvest capital into research and development.34

Share Repurchases: In fiscal year 2025, AMD returned $1.3 billion to shareholders by repurchasing 12.4 million shares of common stock in the open market. As of the end of 2025, a massive $9.4 billion remained authorized and available for future stock repurchases, providing a significant floor for the equity during periods of macro volatility.4

Mergers & Acquisitions: The company has utilized its robust balance sheet to execute targeted M&A.

ZT Systems ($4.4 Billion): Closed on March 31, 2025, this transaction was a transformative acquisition designed to internalize rack-scale AI systems design capabilities.5 Crucially, AMD immediately entered into an agreement to divest ZT Systems' low-margin, capital-intensive data center infrastructure manufacturing business to Sanmina.10 This highly strategic maneuver allows AMD to offer hyperscalers fully optimized, rack-level reference designs without degrading its corporate gross margin profile by becoming a low-margin hardware assembler.

Silo AI ($665 Million): Closed in August 2024, this all-cash acquisition brought in Europe's largest private AI lab to accelerate the development of the ROCm software stack and bespoke open-source language models running on AMD hardware.23

#### Balance Sheet Health and Liquidity

AMD possesses a fortress balance sheet, characterized by massive liquidity and highly manageable debt obligations. As of December 27, 2025, total cash, cash equivalents, and short-term investments stood at $10.6 billion, more than double the $5.1 billion recorded at the end of fiscal 2024.4

The debt profile is structurally sound, consisting entirely of unsecured senior notes with a total principal amount of $3.25 billion.32 The debt maturity wall is exceptionally well-laddered, presenting virtually zero short-to-medium-term liquidity risk:

2030: $750 million (2.375% Senior Notes)

2032: $500 million (3.924% Senior Notes)

With $10.6 billion in immediate liquidity and billions in annual free cash flow generation, AMD can easily retire the 2026 and 2028 tranches organically using cash on hand, while seamlessly funding ongoing capital expenditures and its expansive share repurchase program. Furthermore, the company maintains an untapped $3.0 billion unsecured revolving credit facility, providing an additional layer of financial flexibility.6

#### Regulatory Shocks and China Export Controls

The most material, and highly disruptive, operational event of the trailing twelve months was the abrupt enforcement of United States government export controls regarding high-performance AI accelerators destined for the People's Republic of China. During the second quarter of 2025, AMD was forced to record an $800 million write-down on work-in-progress inventory related to the AMD Instinct MI308 GPU, a product specifically designed for the Chinese market.10 This regulatory intervention fundamentally distorted Q2 margins, temporarily masking the underlying health of the business.

However, management executed a successful mitigation strategy. By the fourth quarter of 2025, AMD had successfully navigated the complex Department of Commerce licensing regime, began shipping compliant products, and subsequently reversed $360 million of the previously recorded inventory charges.14

Note: A highly unusual, circumstantial, and single-sourced claim has emerged in the context of these export approvals. It is reported that U.S. government officials have expressed an expectation that the U.S. government will receive 15% of the revenue generated from licensed MI308 sales to China.14 While this remains a circumstantial claim, if formalized, this unprecedented quasi-tax on export licenses represents a profound regulatory burden and sets a highly concerning precedent for gross margin compression for U.S. technology firms operating in foreign jurisdictions.

#### Strategic Shifts and Product Launches

Operationally, AMD effectively accelerated its AI accelerator release roadmap to match Nvidia's aggressive annual cadence. The company successfully launched the Instinct MI350 series in 2025, heavily targeting the AI inference market with validated claims of matching or exceeding the performance of the competition's B200 architectures at significantly lower capital costs and complexity.10 Concurrently, the rollout of the 5th Generation EPYC ("Turin") processors drove record enterprise and cloud adoption.10 The overarching strategic shift over the last 12 months has been the explicit pivot from acting strictly as a merchant silicon provider to operating as a "comprehensive AI compute provider" capable of delivering end-to-end, fully integrated rack-scale infrastructure.19

#### Management Changes

While Dr. Lisa Su remains securely at the helm as Chair and Chief Executive Officer, driving the company's overarching vision, the executive ranks have seen highly targeted additions stemming from recent mergers and acquisitions. Most notably, the integration of Silo AI CEO Peter Sarlin into the AMD Artificial Intelligence Group brings crucial European software leadership directly into the corporate hierarchy, reporting to AMD Senior Vice President Vamsi Boppana.24

#### Regulatory and Legal Developments

Beyond the Department of Commerce export controls, AMD operates within a complex web of broader legal and regulatory developments. In early 2026, the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) and the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) signaled increasingly aggressive postures toward technology firms. The SEC has heightened scrutiny on how public companies disclose AI capabilities to prevent "AI-washing," while the FTC implemented, and subsequently faced legal challenges regarding, major changes to the Hart-Scott-Rodino (HSR) Act reporting requirements, which impose significantly greater burdens and expenses on filing parties for M&A activity.36 For AMD, which actively acquires software startups (e.g., Silo AI, Nod.ai), the shifting HSR landscape poses a latent friction cost to future inorganic growth.

### 5. Forward Catalysts (Next 6–12 Months)

#### Near-Term Earnings and Consensus Expectations

AMD is scheduled to report its fiscal first quarter 2026 financial results on May 5, 2026.3 Market consensus expects non-GAAP EPS of $1.23 on revenue of approximately $9.84 billion.3 Management’s internal guidance aligns closely, anticipating Q1 2026 revenue to be roughly $9.8 billion (±$300 million), which would represent robust 32% year-over-year growth.38 However, investors must monitor profitability metrics closely; sequential gross margin is expected to contract by approximately 200 basis points to 55%. #### Product Roadmap and Execution Milestones

The next 6 to 12 months represent a highly critical execution window for AMD's data center ambitions, featuring several massive, stock-moving milestones:

The MI450 "Helios" Launch: Slated for the third quarter of 2026, the launch of the MI450 series features the highly anticipated Helios rack-scale AI platform.9 This system architecture connects up to 72 GPUs per rack, offering a claimed 10x generational performance leap for advanced frontier models.10 The successful volume production, bug-free software integration, and physical deployment of Helios is the single most critical execution milestone for the company in 2026.

Meta Platforms' 1-Gigawatt Deployment: AMD has secured a multi-year, multi-billion-dollar, 6-gigawatt AI infrastructure agreement with Meta Platforms.3 Initial physical shipments for the first 1 GW of MI450 GPUs and EPYC CPUs are scheduled to commence in the second half of 2026.39 Progress updates on the physical build-out of this deployment will serve as a vital bellwether for AMD's ability to scale operations at the absolute highest tier of the hyperscaler market.

"Venice" CPU Launch: The next-generation "Venice" server CPUs are expected to launch broadly with OEM partners later in the year. These architectures are designed specifically to address the dense compute, high-memory bandwidth demands of emerging "Agentic AI" workloads, positioning AMD to capture a new vector of CPU growth.9

#### Sector and Macro Events

Hyperscaler Capex Signaling: Forward capital expenditure guidance from the "hyperscaler hypersquad" (Microsoft, Meta, Alphabet, and Amazon) throughout the 2026 earnings cycles will heavily dictate the multiple assigned to AMD's stock. Any signaling of a plateau, delay, or digestion phase in AI infrastructure spending will disproportionately and negatively impact AMD's valuation.

Geopolitical and Macro Developments: Global semiconductor output remains acutely sensitive to cross-strait tensions in Taiwan. Furthermore, the stabilization of global macro events, such as the indefinite U.S.-Iran ceasefire implemented in April 2026, generally serves as a powerful tailwind for high-beta, risk-on semiconductor equities.40

#### The Bull Case

The positive thesis for AMD rests on three foundational pillars:

The Agentic CPU Renaissance: The technological pivot toward "Agentic AI" structurally elevates the importance of high-density CPUs for state-dependent logic. AMD's EPYC line stands to capture massive greenfield data center builds tailored specifically for complex AI agent processing, expanding the TAM beyond pure GPUs.19

Accelerating Software Parity: The rapid maturation of the ROCm ecosystem, combined with the industry-wide embrace of open-source frameworks like PyTorch and Triton, fundamentally diminishes Nvidia's CUDA software moat, allowing AMD to compete purely on hardware TCO, memory bandwidth, and power efficiency.21

Mandatory Hyperscaler Diversification: Massive cloud providers (e.g., Meta, Microsoft, Oracle) are actively allocating billions to AMD infrastructure to prevent total vendor lock-in and pricing subjugation by Nvidia, essentially guaranteeing a vast, captive TAM for the MI350/MI450 accelerators.19

#### The Bear Case

The skeptical thesis highlights valuation limits and structural market risks:

Priced for Flawless Execution: Trading at over 56x EV/EBITDA, the stock prices in zero margin for error. Any deceleration in the AI capex cycle, manufacturing delays with the Helios platform, or TSMC CoWoS packaging bottlenecks could trigger a violent multiple compression.19

Structural Dilution Overhang: To secure gigawatt-scale infrastructure deployments with hyperscalers (e.g., historical deals with Meta and OpenAI), AMD has historically utilized volume-based stock warrants. Vesting events associated with these warrants through the second half of 2026 and 2027 pose a severe headline risk of EPS dilution, potentially obfuscating core operational profitability.27

Alternative Architectural Disruption: The rapid funding and development of SRAM-first custom ASICs by highly capitalized startups (e.g., Cerebras, Groq) presents a long-term existential threat. If the industry coalesces around lower-power, spatial architectures for inference, AMD's massive sunk investments in HBM-dependent GPUs may yield lower terminal returns.27

### 8. Risks

#### Company-Specific Risks

Execution on the Helios Platform: AMD's premium valuation is heavily tethered to the flawless technological rollout of the MI450 Helios rack-scale systems in Q3 2026. Any delays in engineering, bottlenecks in CoWoS packaging yields at TSMC, or integration bugs with the ROCm 7 software stack will result in missed delivery windows to hyperscalers, permanently destroying revenue opportunities as clients pivot back to Nvidia hardware.

Customer Concentration: A massive portion of AMD's Data Center growth relies on a handful of mega-cap technology firms (e.g., Microsoft, Meta, Oracle). A shift in procurement strategy by any single entity—either toward internal custom silicon or back exclusively to Nvidia—would severely and immediately impact AMD's revenue trajectory.8

Dilution Overhang: As noted in the bear case, the historical issuance of volume-based stock warrants to secure gigawatt-scale infrastructure deployments creates a persistent, structural drag on GAAP earnings per share. Vesting events associated with these warrants through 2026 and 2027 pose a headline risk of EPS dilution, potentially masking the core operational profitability of the firm to algorithmic traders and retail investors.27

#### Sector Risks

The Cyclical Hangover: While the Data Center segment is currently booming, the Client and Gaming segments remain historically cyclical. The Gaming segment is already experiencing severe deceleration due to mature console lifecycles, and a broader global consumer spending pullback could stall the momentum of the AI PC upgrade super-cycle, creating a drag on roughly 40% of the company's revenue base.

Technological Obsolescence: The accelerating shift from generalized GPUs to highly specialized AI inference ASICs threatens to commoditize AMD's core hardware advantage. If the industry successfully coalesces around lower-power, SRAM-first spatial architectures for inference tasks, AMD's massive sunk investments in HBM-dependent GPUs may ultimately yield lower long-term returns on invested capital.27

#### Macro Sensitivities

Geopolitics and Export Controls: The U.S. government's unpredictable expansion of advanced technology export restrictions to China remains a highly volatile risk. While AMD successfully navigated the MI308 blockages, the specter of the Department of Commerce withholding future licenses—or enforcing unprecedented revenue-sharing requirements (as rumored with the 15% claim)—structurally limits AMD's total addressable market in Asia.14

Macroeconomic and Duration Sensitivities: Trading at peak earnings multiples, AMD's equity is highly duration-sensitive. Any resurgence in global inflation that forces the Federal Reserve to hold or increase interest rates will disproportionately impact the present value of AMD's long-dated cash flows, causing violent multiple compression across the semiconductor sector.

Foreign Exchange (FX) Exposure: With 67% of total revenue derived from international markets, AMD is highly exposed to currency fluctuations. A sustained strengthening of the U.S. dollar against the Euro or Asian currencies makes AMD's hardware more expensive abroad, potentially dampening international demand and compressing reported U.S. dollar revenues.6

### 9. Valuation & Peer Context

As of late April 2026, AMD's stock trades dynamically in the $275 to $305 range, pushing its market capitalization decisively toward the $450 to $500 billion threshold.46 The valuation profile reflects absolute premium pricing, fully incorporating market expectations of multi-year, uninterrupted hyper-growth in the data center.

#### Current Multiples vs. History

Price-to-Earnings (P/E): The trailing P/E ratio hovers near an astronomical 107.5x, well above semiconductor industry medians and significantly higher than traditional value bounds.50 This figure is somewhat misleading on a GAAP basis due to the heavy amortization of intangible assets related to the $49 billion Xilinx acquisition, which suppresses GAAP net income. However, even on a non-GAAP basis, the earnings multiple remains highly elevated. For context, the end-of-year P/E was 105.8x in 2025, 108.0x in 2024, and 38.8x in 2022.50

Enterprise Value to EBITDA (EV/EBITDA): A more accurate reflection of operational cash generation, AMD trades at roughly 56.7x trailing EV/EBITDA.49 Over the fiscal years 2021 to 2025, AMD's EV/EBITDA averaged 50.9x, hitting a cyclical trough of 18.0x during the 2022 tech bear market and peaking at 77.2x during the initial AI frenzy in late 2023.51 The current multiple indicates that while the stock is not at its absolute peak "hype" valuation, it commands a meaningful premium to its historical five-year average.

Enterprise Value to Sales (EV/Sales): The trailing EV/Revenue multiple stands at approximately 12.7x, indicating that investors are paying nearly 13 dollars for every dollar of top-line revenue generated by the firm.49

#### Key Correlations

AMD's equity performance is virtually decoupled from traditional macroeconomic commodities (e.g., oil, gold) but is hyper-correlated to two specific vectors: AI Capex signaling and the cost of capital (interest rates). The blowout Q1 2026 revenue report from foundry partner TSMC (+35% year-over-year) served as a direct, highly correlated proxy for AMD's fundamental health, validating relentless end-market AI chip demand and instantly lifting AMD's stock in sympathy.3 Conversely, AMD trades with a high beta to the Nasdaq 100; any macro-driven rotation out of mega-cap technology equities or widening of corporate credit spreads will forcefully drag down AMD's valuation, as evidenced by the intense sensitivity of its EV/EBITDA multiple to broader market risk sentiment.19
