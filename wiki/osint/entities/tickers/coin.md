## Equity Research Report: Coinbase Global, Inc. (COIN)

### 1. Business Overview

Coinbase Global, Inc. operates as the foundational financial infrastructure layer for the global digital asset economy. Over the past decade, the enterprise has evolved from a rudimentary fiat-to-cryptocurrency onboarding portal into a highly diversified, regulated financial services conglomerate. In late 2025, the company officially crystallized its "Everything Exchange" vision, effectively expanding its product suite to include spot crypto assets, derivatives, equities, commodities, and prediction markets.1 This transition represents a structural pivot aimed at capturing total market liquidity rather than relying solely on the cyclical volatility of cryptocurrency beta.

#### Core Business and Revenue Segments

The financial architecture of Coinbase is broadly divided into two primary segments: Transaction Revenue and Subscription and Services Revenue. For the fiscal year 2025, Coinbase reported total revenue of $7.2 billion, representing a 9% year-over-year increase, driven by a deliberate strategic shift to diversify away from purely volume-based trading fees toward recurring revenue streams.3

Transaction Revenue: Historically the primary driver of top-line growth, transaction revenue accounted for approximately 45% to 55% of total net revenue in recent quarters, representing a marked decline from previous cycles where it constituted over 80% of revenue.5 This segment encompasses consumer and institutional trading activity across spot and derivatives markets. In Q4 2025, transaction revenue totaled $983 million, a 6% sequential decline reflecting a broader contraction in global cryptocurrency spot volumes during the period.3 Consumer trading generates the vast majority of this segment's margin, while institutional trading—facilitated through Coinbase Prime—operates on significantly tighter spreads but generates substantially higher nominal volumes. In 2025, total Coinbase trading volume reached an all-time high of $5.2 trillion, up 156% year-over-year, allowing the firm to capture a 6.4% global market share in crypto trading volume.1

Subscription and Services Revenue: This segment serves as the anchor for Coinbase’s revenue diversification offensive, growing 23% year-over-year to reach a record $2.8 billion in 2025—a figure 5.5 times higher than the peak of the 2021 bull cycle.3 The primary sub-components include:

Stablecoin Revenue: Coinbase earns interest income on fiat reserves backing USD Coin (USDC) through a strategic consortium agreement with Circle Internet Financial. In Q4 2025, stablecoin revenue was $364 million, with average USDC balances on the platform reaching a record $17.8 billion.3

Blockchain Rewards: Revenue generated from staking services, primarily Ethereum (ETH) and Solana (SOL), where Coinbase takes a commission on the yield generated by delegating customer assets to secure proof-of-stake networks.2

Custody Fees: Generated through Coinbase Prime, the company holds over $200 billion in institutional assets under custody, serving as the dominant custodian for the majority of US-based spot Bitcoin and Ethereum exchange-traded funds (ETFs).10

Subscriptions: The "Coinbase One" premium retail subscription tier reached nearly 1 million paid users in 2025, representing a 3x growth over three years. The service offers zero-fee trading and enhanced yields for a flat monthly fee, effectively converting unpredictable trading flow into predictable software-as-a-service (SaaS) revenue.3

#### Unit Economics and Monetization Mechanics

Coinbase monetizes its retail user base through a blended take rate that averages approximately 0.60%, depending heavily on the asset, volume tier, and execution style (Simple vs. Advanced Trade).11 The company charges both a flat transaction fee and a spread embedded in the quoted asset price. Institutional clients utilizing Coinbase Prime operate on a maker-taker fee schedule that scales down to fractions of a basis point for high-volume participants, ranging from 0.00% to 0.40% for makers and 0.04% to 0.60% for takers.12

Beyond traditional trading, unit economics are increasingly driven by on-chain activity. Through its proprietary Layer-2 (L2) network, "Base," Coinbase generates revenue via sequencer fees. The company retains the margin between the cost of submitting transaction batches to the Ethereum mainnet and the micro-fees paid by users on the Base network.2 The L2 ecosystem effectively monetizes the underlying compute demand of decentralized finance (DeFi) and AI-agent microtransactions, representing a highly scalable, high-margin revenue vertical.2

#### Geographic Exposure and Market Positioning

While Coinbase operates globally across more than 100 countries, its revenue profile remains disproportionately reliant on the United States, subjecting it to intense domestic regulatory and macroeconomic sensitivities.15 Internationally, the company has aggressively expanded its footprint to mitigate domestic reliance. The most notable expansion occurred through the strategic acquisition of Deribit, the world’s leading cryptocurrency options exchange. This integration significantly bolstered Coinbase's international derivatives market share, allowing the firm to capture institutional flow outside of rigid US jurisdictions and positioning the company as a dominant force in global options open interest.1

### 2. Competitive Landscape

The competitive environment for digital asset trading and financial infrastructure has evolved into a bifurcated battleground: global offshore crypto-native exchanges competing on product breadth and leverage, and domestic traditional finance (TradFi) super-apps competing on retail convenience and zero-fee structures.

#### Primary Competitors and Market Share

The spot and derivatives markets exhibit entirely different concentration profiles. In the global spot and derivatives arena, Binance remains the apex competitor. In Q1 2026, Binance commanded a 34.9% market share among the top 10 derivatives exchanges, executing approximately $4.90 trillion in derivatives volume.17 Binance's user asset retention of $152.9 billion is roughly 9.6 times the size of OKX, the nearest offshore competitor.18 Coinbase, while the undisputed leader in US-regulated spot trading, ranks second globally in spot volume, averaging $2.6 billion daily compared to Binance's $7.0 billion.17

In the United States, the primary competitive threat originates from diversified fintech brokerages such as Robinhood and Kraken. Robinhood has aggressively targeted the Millennial and Gen-Z demographic by offering a seamless, low-cost trading experience. In Q3 2025, Robinhood led total platform trading volume at $727 billion across all asset classes, compared to Kraken at $577 billion and Coinbase at $295 billion.13 It should be noted that Robinhood's volume metrics are bolstered by traditional equities, while Coinbase and Kraken remain overwhelmingly (over 98%) reliant on crypto transaction revenue. Crucially, Robinhood exerts severe fee compression pressure on Coinbase; Robinhood’s blended crypto take rate is estimated at 0.18%, heavily undercutting Coinbase’s 0.60% baseline.13

#### Durable Moats vs. Contested Moats

Durable Moat: Institutional Custody and Regulatory Premium Coinbase’s most impenetrable moat lies in its institutional infrastructure. The company’s "Prime" platform has achieved near-monopoly status as the custodian of choice for traditional asset managers launching spot Bitcoin and Ethereum ETFs.10 The operational friction, regulatory licensing, and deep cold-storage security required to service entities like BlackRock and Grayscale establish a massive barrier to entry. Furthermore, the integration of Deribit secures a highly defensible ~90% market share in global crypto options open interest, creating a network effect where institutional liquidity naturally aggregates where the deepest order books already reside.20

Contested Moat: Retail Spot Trading Conversely, the retail fiat-to-crypto gateway is a deeply contested moat. The commoditization of spot trading means that Coinbase can no longer rely on early-adopter premiums. As platforms like PayPal, Cash App, and Robinhood expand their crypto offerings, and traditional brokerages like E*Trade integrate digital asset exposure, Coinbase's high-margin retail trading segment faces structural, long-term erosion.13

#### Threats from Adjacent Industries and Technology Shifts

The broader technological shift toward decentralized finance (DeFi) and decentralized exchanges (DEXs) like Uniswap presents a persistent threat of disintermediation. As users become more sophisticated, the need for centralized intermediaries diminishes.22 However, Coinbase has strategically hedged this threat via the Base L2 network and the self-custodial Coinbase Wallet, effectively capturing value at the infrastructure layer even if trading volumes migrate fully on-chain.2

Additionally, the potential introduction of Central Bank Digital Currencies (CBDCs) poses a tail-risk threat to the lucrative private stablecoin economics currently shared between Coinbase and Circle.23 A fully sovereign, yield-bearing digital dollar could crowd out private stablecoins, instantly evaporating a multi-hundred-million-dollar quarterly revenue stream for the company. Finally, the rapid rise of prediction markets has introduced regulatory threats; while Coinbase views this as an adjacent growth vertical, state-level litigation (such as the recent New York Attorney General lawsuit) threatens to classify these markets as illegal gambling operations, creating a fragmented and hostile regulatory environment for new entrants.24

#### Capital Allocation

Share Repurchases vs. Dividends: Coinbase does not currently pay a dividend, prioritizing internal reinvestment and share repurchases.35 Management has aggressively utilized its cash flow to combat stock-based compensation dilution. Between November 2025 and February 2026, the company repurchased 8.2 million shares at an aggregate cost of approximately $1.7 billion.14 In January 2026, the Board of Directors expanded the share repurchase authorization by an additional $2.0 billion.3

M&A Activity: Coinbase's capital allocation strategy leans heavily toward strategic, bolt-on acquisitions that immediately expand market share or geographical reach. The preeminent example is the late 2024 to early 2025 acquisition of Deribit, the global leader in crypto options, in a transaction valued at approximately $2.9 billion (comprising $700 million in cash and 11 million shares).6 The firm also completed the acquisition of Sentillia B.V. to enhance its international presence.2 These acquisitions signal a structural intent to dominate professional and institutional derivatives markets globally.

#### Balance Sheet Health

Coinbase possesses a fortress balance sheet characterized by exceptional liquidity. As of December 31, 2025, total cash and cash equivalents (including restricted cash and customer custodial cash) reached $16.89 billion, with unrestricted corporate cash and equivalents standing at $11.28 billion.2

The company's debt maturity wall is highly favorable, having executed advantageous convertible note offerings during low-rate environments. The long-term debt profile includes:

2029 Convertible Notes: $1.0 billion principal, maturing October 1, 2029.34

2030 Convertible Notes: $1.3 billion principal, maturing April 1, 2030.15

2032 Convertible Notes: $1.5 billion principal at a 0% coupon rate, maturing October 1, 2032 (conversion price of ~$454.44 per share).34

This staggered maturity profile, anchored by zero or low-coupon convertibles, allows Coinbase to fund aggressive ecosystem expansion without the burden of high debt-service costs. This structural financial health earned the company a BB- credit rating from S&P Global and an upgraded B1 rating from Moody's.2

### 4. Recent Events (Last 12 Months)

#### Earnings Surprises vs. Consensus

Coinbase's Q4 2025 earnings report, delivered on February 12, 2026, resulted in a significant headline miss that triggered acute stock volatility. The company reported a GAAP EPS loss of -$2.49 (or -$2.43 diluted), severely missing the Wall Street consensus estimate of a -$0.94 loss.3 Net revenue of $1.71 billion also fell short of the $1.81 billion consensus.7

However, second-order analysis reveals that the miss was driven primarily by non-operating, non-cash items—specifically $718 million in unrealized mark-to-market losses on the company’s internal crypto portfolio, alongside a $395 million loss on strategic investments.3 Beneath the GAAP accounting distortion, the company posted its 12th consecutive quarter of Adjusted EBITDA profitability ($566 million) and generated robust free cash flow, proving the resilience of its diversified model even amid an 11% quarter-over-quarter drop in total crypto market capitalization.3

#### Product Launches, Strategic Shifts, and Restructurings

Late 2025 and early 2026 marked a pivotal strategic inflection point as Coinbase fully deployed the "Everything Exchange." To mitigate structural exposure to cryptocurrency cycles, the platform integrated traditional asset trading, launching equities, commodities, and prediction markets.2

Furthermore, the integration of the Deribit acquisition drove institutional transaction revenue up 37% sequentially in Q4 2025 to $185 million, solidifying derivatives as the primary growth vector for professional trading volume.7 The company also formalized its "Asset Accumulation Flywheel," heavily subsidizing USDC yield to attract and retain sticky fiat capital on the platform, which resulted in a record $17.8 billion in average USDC balances held in Coinbase products.3 The firm did not announce any major corporate restructurings or mass layoffs in the trailing twelve months, reflecting a stabilization of headcount at approximately 4,950 employees following the severe downsizing of the 2022 bear market.3

#### Management and Board Changes

While the core C-suite—comprising CEO Brian Armstrong, CFO Alesia Haas, and COO Emilie Choi—remains intact, the Board of Directors is undergoing consolidation. In April 2026, Coinbase reported via an 8-K filing that Director Paul Clement will not stand for re-election at the upcoming 2026 Annual Meeting of Shareholders. Following his departure, the Board intends to reduce its size from ten directors to nine, with no immediate plans to fill the vacancy.39

#### Regulatory and Legal Developments

The regulatory landscape remains a complex tapestry of existential victories and emerging state-level threats.

SEC Litigation Dismissal: In a landmark victory for the firm, the US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) and Coinbase filed a joint stipulation on February 28, 2025, to dismiss the SEC's overarching civil enforcement action with prejudice.34 This removal of existential regulatory overhang effectively de-risked the company's core spot trading and staking businesses, validating the platform's compliance posture.

Prediction Market Lawsuits: As Coinbase expanded into prediction markets, it immediately drew the ire of state regulators. On April 21, 2026, New York Attorney General Letitia James filed a lawsuit against Coinbase and Gemini, alleging that their prediction platforms operate as illegal, unlicensed gambling operations accessible to users under 21.24 Coinbase Chief Legal Officer Paul Grewal publicly rebuked the suit, asserting the products are federally regulated national exchanges registered with the CFTC, thereby escalating the conflict to federal courts.24 Similar lawsuits have been initiated or contested in Wisconsin, Connecticut, and Illinois.24

The Everything Exchange: Scaling the newly integrated stock and ETF trading functionalities launched on April 1, 2026, to directly challenge Robinhood's multi-asset dominance.3

#### Sector and Macro Events

The most critical legislative catalyst on the horizon is the markup and potential passage of the CLARITY Act. This legislation aims to provide a definitive market structure for digital assets in the United States. However, negotiations are currently bottlenecked over provisions regarding whether institutions can pass stablecoin yields to consumers—a debate heavily lobbied by the traditional banking sector fearing deposit flight.50 A favorable resolution would structurally cement Coinbase’s stablecoin revenue model. It should be noted that a White House Council of Economic Advisers report suggested that stablecoin yield bans would cost consumers $800 million, a figure cited heavily by industry lobbyists to push the bill forward.51 Additionally, the company's application for a national trust company charter from the OCC remains a pending catalyst that could vastly expand its custody operations for traditional financial institutions.52

The sell-side community maintains a generally constructive, albeit cautious, outlook on Coinbase due to the inherent volatility of its revenue streams and recent macroeconomic pressures.

#### Bull Case and Bear Case

The Bull Case:

Institutional Custody Dominance: The "Prime" platform has established an insurmountable liquidity moat, securing over $200 billion in institutional assets and serving as the foundational custodian for the majority of US spot crypto ETFs.10

Stablecoin Structural Growth: The expansion of USDC as a cross-border payment rail and AI-agent settlement layer provides $1-$2 billion in highly visible, incremental annual pre-tax profit that is immune to token price volatility.37

Derivatives and Diversification: The $2.9 billion Deribit acquisition successfully captured roughly 90% of the global crypto options market, while the "Everything Exchange" rollout diversifies revenue away from pure crypto beta into traditional equities and prediction markets.7

Base L2 Utility: Base has established itself as a premier Layer-2 scaling solution. Rising transaction volume generated by decentralized applications and tokenized real-world assets creates a high-margin, compounding sequencer fee revenue stream.10

The Bear Case:

Retail Margin Compression: Intense competition from zero-commission fintechs (Robinhood) and traditional brokers entering the space is irreparably commoditizing the retail fiat-to-crypto gateway, threatening the legacy 0.60% take rate upon which the company's historical margins were built.13

Opex Bloat: Rising operational expenditures—including $1.5 billion in Q4 2025 alone—driven by headcount growth, policy lobbying, and USDC reward subsidies, are severely compressing operating margins during periods of stagnant volume.3

Regulatory Caps on Yield: Potential legislative compromises in the CLARITY Act could restrict or ban the distribution of stablecoin yields to consumers, stifling Coinbase's asset accumulation flywheel and severely impacting its fastest-growing subscription revenue segment.50

Macro Vulnerability: The core business remains heavily reliant on retail risk appetite, which is vulnerable to sustained high interest rates and the crowding-out effect of the artificial intelligence capital expenditure supercycle, which is drawing speculative liquidity away from digital assets.64 (It should be noted that targets forecasting $510 per share represent single-sourced, hyper-bullish outlier scenarios reliant on total macro convergence 14).

Short interest in Coinbase remains elevated as hedge funds frequently utilize the stock as a liquid vehicle to short the broader crypto market, hedge long token exposure, or execute statistical arbitrage against Bitcoin ETFs. As of mid-April 2026, short interest stands at 24.07 million shares, representing 10.94% of the free float.35 The days-to-cover ratio is approximately 2.23 to 2.42 days, suggesting low immediate risk of a violent short squeeze.35 However, an abnormally high off-exchange short volume ratio (frequently exceeding 45% of total off-exchange volume) indicates heavy institutional hedging activity occurring in dark pools outside traditional lit exchanges.72

#### Options Skew and Unusual Derivatives Activity

The derivatives market reflects a populace bracing for near-term volatility, particularly heading into the May 2026 earnings print. The 30-day implied volatility (IV) is exceptionally elevated at 77.1%, placing it in the 98th percentile rank historically.73

The options skew—measured by the 25-delta Put-Call spread—exhibits a persistent, albeit moderate, defensive posture. The 25-delta puts are trading at a slight premium to equidistant calls (a positive spread ranging from +1.6% to +3.1% in mid-April), indicating that market makers are demanding higher premiums to insure against downside tail risk.73 Unusual options activity highlighted heavy volume on the April 24, 2026, $212.50 calls, which saw 13,873 contracts traded, suggesting speculative retail positioning attempting to front-run a technical breakout, even as institutional positioning remains firmly hedged.75

### 8. Risks

#### Company-Specific Risks

Margin Degradation and Execution: Coinbase is structurally transitioning from a high-margin, passive retail trading app into a lower-margin, diversified financial services ecosystem. Operating expenses have ballooned, with R&D and SG&A remaining highly rigid regardless of cyclical downturns. If the "Everything Exchange" fails to capture sufficient traditional asset volume to offset the lower fee tiers necessary to compete with legacy brokers, net margins will suffer permanent degradation.13

Regulatory Litigation: While the SEC's existential threat to the core business has been dismissed, the April 2026 lawsuit from the NY Attorney General targeting the company's new prediction markets introduces fresh legal peril. The state's aggressive pursuit of fines and restitution under anti-gambling statutes threatens to derail one of the firm's most promising new retail engagement funnels, potentially forcing the geofencing of products and stunting growth in key markets.24

Customer Concentration: The institutional Prime brokerage relies heavily on a handful of massive entities (e.g., BlackRock's ETF). Should these traditional financial behemoths eventually internalize custody or migrate to legacy banking custodians (if regulations allow), Coinbase would face a severe shock to its Assets Under Custody metrics and corresponding fee revenue.

#### Sector Risks

TradFi Encroachment: The fundamental thesis of Coinbase—providing safe, regulated access to digital assets—is being rapidly commoditized. Traditional asset managers have successfully financialized Bitcoin and Ethereum via ETFs, allowing retail investors to gain exposure through existing brokerage accounts without ever interacting with a crypto-native exchange.

DeFi Disintermediation: As on-chain infrastructure improves, user volume naturally migrates toward decentralized exchanges (DEXs) like Uniswap, threatening centralized exchange volumes. While the Base L2 acts as a hedge, the monetization of L2 sequencer fees produces lower absolute revenue per transaction than traditional centralized retail spreads, requiring exponentially higher volume to replace lost exchange revenue.

#### Macro Sensitivities

Interest Rate Vulnerability: A significant portion of the company’s subscription revenue is intrinsically linked to the Federal Reserve's rate path via the USDC reserve interest. The company acknowledged that rate cuts in late 2024 and 2025 resulted in lower effective interest rates on reserves.3 An aggressive easing cycle fundamentally impairs this multi-hundred-million-dollar quarterly revenue stream, compressing the very earnings that insulate the company during crypto bear markets.

The AI Capex Squeeze: The digital asset market is currently fighting a brutal battle for marginal liquidity against the artificial intelligence supercycle. With mega-cap technology firms projected to spend nearly $700 billion on AI infrastructure in 2026, institutional capital is rotating heavily into semiconductor suppliers and utility/energy plays.64 This massive capital drain starves speculative, high-beta assets like crypto of the liquidity needed to sustain a bull market.78

Geopolitics and Stagflation: Global events, particularly the early 2026 Iran conflict and subsequent oil shocks (with Brent crude surging 90%), have raised the specter of durably higher inflation and supply chain disruption.79 Stagflationary regimes force central banks to hold rates higher than economic growth warrants, historically crushing the risk tolerance required to support high-beta retail trading volumes.

### 9. Valuation & Peer Context

#### Current Multiples vs. Historical Averages

Valuing Coinbase presents unique challenges due to the extreme volatility of its GAAP earnings, which are frequently distorted by mark-to-market accounting of its corporate crypto treasury.30 However, evaluated on standard multiples, the equity appears priced for perfection relative to historical norms.

As of April 2026, Coinbase trades at a trailing Price-to-Earnings (P/E) ratio of approximately 43.6x to 45.0x (up from 19.5x at the end of 2025).81 This remains slightly below its 5-year historical average P/E of 48.1x, but trades at a massive premium to the broader diversified financials sector median of roughly 11.2x.81

On a cash-adjusted basis, the Enterprise Value to EBITDA (EV/EBITDA) multiple sits at 17.8x to 28.3x depending on the specific trailing metric utilized, significantly above the 5-year median of 21.1x.82 The Price-to-Sales (P/S) multiple of 6.7x to 7.8x indicates that the market is willing to pay a heavy premium for the company’s top-line revenue, largely due to the high-margin nature of its software and subscription services compared to traditional capital-intensive financials.73

#### Key Macro Correlations

The pricing of COIN equity does not exist in a vacuum; it is governed by a complex matrix of macroeconomic correlations:

The US Dollar (DXY): Coinbase and the broader crypto market exhibit a deeply entrenched inverse correlation with the US Dollar Index. When the DXY strengthens—typically during periods of global risk aversion or hawkish Fed policy—global liquidity contracts, suppressing Bitcoin prices and, by extension, Coinbase's transaction volumes.86

The S&P 500 & AI Divergence: Historically, Bitcoin and Coinbase traded as high-beta proxies to the S&P 500. However, in 2026, this correlation has fractured. While the S&P 500 has been buoyed by the concentration of the "Magnificent 7" and the AI infrastructure boom, Coinbase has suffered from the aforementioned liquidity rotation. Investors are explicitly demanding clear line-of-sight to cash flows, rewarding AI capex beneficiaries while penalizing consumer-reliant trading platforms.88

Credit Spreads: As a barometer of systemic financial stress, widening credit spreads typically front-run a contraction in the highly speculative digital asset sector. The arbitrary nature of credit spread shocks during geopolitical crises (such as the early 2026 Middle East escalations) immediately translates into wider bid-ask spreads and lower retail participation on the Coinbase platform.52
