# Audit: SWMR long thesis, 9 October 2026

Auditor: Grok, after a primary-source pass and independent lanes. The note is an observational LONG. This audit breaks that framing. It does not rewrite the note.

## Verdict: UNSOUND

The LONG, as a bet that Erik Prince consolidates Ukraine's drone base into SWMR, is not defensible. Two load-bearing statements are wrong, and both errors favor the long. Prince's option is 60 percent time-based and 40 percent milestone-based, not the reverse. The FY2026 Ratel earnout is a revenue test and an $8.47 million operating-income test, not a revenue test that $86.6 million of advances can answer. Fixes do not clean the long. They remove it. What remains is a small speculative ticket with a high chance of a total loss. The note already says to size a total loss so that it is boring. That is a different claim from the title.

Answers to the five questions:

1. The round-trip warning is the strongest part of the note, and the economic point is right. The booking verb is too strong. As of 30 June 2026 only $0.4 million had reduced revenue. The $2.4 million fee had not been invoiced and reduces revenue when it is paid.
2. The $77 million earnout is not presented fairly. H1 net sales of $9.6 million do imply about seven times that pace in H2 if revenue were the only test. The advances are a contract liability. Management expects to recognize them as revenue. The filing does not show that they convert one for one into earnout net revenue, and it does not disclose a VAT haircut. A 20 percent VAT reduction is UNVERIFIED. The operating-income gate is omitted.
3. "Prince the consolidator" is not supported. He owns zero shares. Most of the option vests for staying. Signing Ratel is not the customer-introduction milestone. The first target has substantial going-concern doubt, and the purchase agreement does not require Swarmer to finance it.
4. The "100,000 combat missions" claim is mostly at the right strength. It is company-only, and the real reported swarms are 3 to 8 drones. Two strength fixes remain. "Any level of autonomy" is a little stronger than the filing. The "70,000 operations" line is the CEO speaking to Defense One, not an independent count.
5. The verified facts support a different conclusion. Do not underwrite a Prince roll-up. A sized-to-zero lottery is the most the evidence allows, and the title does not say that.

## Logic errors (broken inference in the signal-to-trade chain, or NONE)

1. The incentive chain is broken. The note says the option structure shows Prince "is paid to introduce large customers, raise capital, and lift the market cap," and that the Ratel signing was "the first of these." The dominant tranche pays him to remain through 8 December 2026 and the following year. The introduced-customer test is $10 million of realized revenue from customers he introduces. Ratel is a target. No filing shows Ratel as a customer Prince introduced, or shows any milestone met. He holds zero shares, so a total loss of the equity is not his capital loss. Public holders take the dilution and the going-concern asset. Source: POS AM and DEFM14A footnote (5). The zero-share quote in the note is verbatim and CONFIRMED.

2. The advances-to-earnout bridge is broken. The note treats $86,632,403 of customer advances as the reason a $67.4 million second-half revenue gap is "a delivery question, not an arithmetic impossibility." CONFIRMED facts that cut the other way: the balance is a contract liability, not cash; management "expects to recognize substantially all" of it as revenue in 2026 (CLAIMED expectation); $22,626,057 of Ratel cash is restricted; $44.0 million is already prepaid to suppliers and $16.7 million sits in inventory; cash and cash equivalents are $2.3 million and short-term debt is $2.0 million; the auditor's going-concern language covers the annual statements and the interim statements; the purchase agreement "does not obligate Swarmer to provide financing" and "the acquisition does not alleviate the substantial doubt." Earnout "Revenue" means "aggregate net revenue" calculated under Schedule 2.5(a). That schedule body is not in the filed exhibit. VAT appears as its own working-capital line, and non-recoverable VAT is excluded from the operating-income definition. The filing does not state that the $86.6 million must be reduced by Ukrainian VAT. Do not apply a 20 percent haircut. Also do not treat gross advances as earnout-eligible net revenue.

3. Firing the founder can pay the full earnout. CONFIRMED, and omitted. EX-2.1 section 2.5(g): if the buyer terminates Ostapchuk without Cause, or he resigns with Good Reason, unpaid earnout periods are deemed payable in full. He also keeps budget and Ukraine commercial autonomy under section 2.5(f). A "Prince consolidates the asset" story leaves out the person who can accelerate dilution by leaving.

4. The cash and share caps in the note are the seller piece only. Closing is about $7.2 million cash plus 1,064,942 shares. The FY2026 cash earnout is up to another $7.2 million. Employee incentives are $800,000 and 118,326 fully vested RSUs at closing, and up to the same again if the 2026 earnout pays, plus 137,600 retention RSUs. The proxy's own combined cap, before closing adjustments, is up to $16.0 million cash and up to 5,723,719 shares. "Up to 5.49 million" is the seller stock only (1,064,942 plus 4,422,125). It is not the whole dilution.

5. "Independent reporting confirms" the combat record, then cites "more than 70,000 operations." Defense One attributes that count to Kupriienko. The independent part of the September 2025 reporting is the scale: typically three drones, other units up to eight, tests to 25, and a crew cut from nine people to three. The 100,000 figure remains CLAIMED. Ukrainska Pravda is a summary of the WSJ, not the WSJ page. https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2025/09/03/7529154/ and https://www.defenseone.com/business/2025/09/ukrainian-startup-has-re-invented-drone-swarming/408099/

6. If the 80 percent Vectus claim were true, it would cut against the thesis. The air-defense upside would sit outside the public equity, in a related party. The claim is not an SEC fact. Do not use it either as support or as a proven conflict. State the gap.

7. One falsifier fights a confirmed collection. "The round-trip counterparty does not pay" sits next to a filing that says $1.4 million of the $1.5 million invoiced was collected by 30 June 2026, with $67,500 in accounts receivable. The open quality issue is fair value, future payments, and possible restatement, not a demonstrated refusal to pay. The $1.5 million invoiced and the $1,467,500 of collected plus receivable do not tie by $32,500. The filing states both. Do not force them.
